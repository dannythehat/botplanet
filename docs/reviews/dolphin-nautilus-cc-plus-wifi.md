# Dolphin Nautilus CC Plus Wi-Fi review

**The short answer.** The Nautilus CC Plus with Wi-Fi is a corded floor-and-wall
cleaner that does a specific job reliably and does not pretend to do more. It plugs
in, runs a two-hour cycle, climbs the walls, and never needs charging. If that is
the job you have, it is one of the safest choices on the market. But it does **not**
scrub the waterline, and a great deal of writing about this robot — including, until
today, our own — implies that it does. If the greasy tide-mark at the tile line is
the thing bothering you, this is the wrong machine and no amount of scheduling will
fix that.

---

## Who this is for

Someone with an in-ground pool up to about 40 ft who wants the cleaning to happen
without thinking about it. You want the floor done, you want the walls done, you do
not want to remember to charge anything, and you would quite like to start a cycle
from your phone rather than walking out to the poolside.

That is a common set of wants, and the Nautilus CC Plus meets all of them without
drama. Maytronics has been building Dolphin cleaners for a long time and this is one
of their volume models rather than an experiment.

## Who should look elsewhere

Three groups.

**If you need the waterline cleaned**, this is not your robot. More on that below,
because it is the single most likely reason someone buys this machine and ends up
disappointed.

**If you want cordless**, obviously — this is a mains-powered cleaner with a 56 ft
cable and a poolside power supply. That is a feature, not a compromise, but it is a
choice you should make deliberately.

**If your pool is longer than 40 ft**, the manufacturer's own guidance stops there.
The cable is longer than that, which confuses people, and we will come back to why.

---

## The waterline question, first

Maytronics' technical specification for this model lists its cleaning coverage as
**floor and walls**. Waterline scrubbing is not among the stated capabilities.

This matters more than it sounds. "Climbs walls" and "cleans the waterline" get used
interchangeably in product copy across the whole category, and they are not the same
thing. A robot can drive up a wall — many do — without ever holding position at the
surface and brushing the narrow tile band where body oils, sunscreen and airborne
grime collect into a visible ring. Waterline cleaning is a separate behaviour, and
it usually requires the machine to park itself at the surface deliberately.

The Nautilus CC Plus climbs. Per the specification, it does not do the second part.

We are being blunt about this because our own product copy claimed waterline
cleaning for this robot until we checked it against the manufacturer's technical
sheet on 3 August 2026. It was wrong, it is corrected, and if you were about to buy
on the strength of that line, we would rather lose the sale than have you open the
box and find a tide-mark still there.

**What to do with that information:** if the waterline is genuinely your problem,
you want a model that states waterline scrubbing explicitly in its own specification
— and you should check that on the exact product page for the exact SKU you are
buying, not on a category page or a retailer listing that may be describing a
different variant.

## What it actually cleans, and how

**Coverage:** floor and walls.

**Cycle:** a single default cycle of 120 minutes. There are no enhanced or
alternative cleaning modes on this model, and no delay-start function is disclosed.
You get one cycle, and it is a good length — two hours is enough for a thorough pass
on a pool at the upper end of its size range.

**Brushes:** two, described by Maytronics as a Combine Brush. Note that this is a
passive brush arrangement rather than an actively driven scrubbing brush. The
brushes work as the robot moves rather than spinning independently. For settled dirt
and general grime this is perfectly adequate; for stuck-on deposits, an actively
driven brush does more work.

**Suction:** 4,500 gallons per hour, or roughly 17 cubic metres per hour. That is a
meaningful figure rather than a marketing one, and it sits comfortably in the middle
of the category.

**Navigation:** here we have to be honest about a gap. Maytronics describes the
cleaner as having automated navigation, but the cited product pages do not specify
which navigation technology is used. Some cleaners in this class use gyroscopic
mapping; others use a semi-random pattern with coverage logic. We cannot tell you
which this is, because the manufacturer does not say on the pages we checked. Anyone
telling you precisely how it navigates is either reading a different source or
guessing.

## Pool size, and the cable that confuses everyone

The stated maximum pool length is **40 ft (12 m)**.

The cable is **56 ft (17.1 m)**, with an anti-tangle swivel.

Those two numbers do not match, and that is not an error — it is the normal
relationship between the two. The cable has to reach from a poolside power supply,
down into the water, and across the pool floor with slack to spare. The pool-size
guidance is deliberately shorter than the cable length, because a robot that can
physically reach the far end is not the same as a robot that can *work* the far end
within a cycle.

**Use the 40 ft figure.** Some official bundle copy has referenced 50 ft, and we are
flagging that because you may encounter it. Where a manufacturer's own materials
disagree, the technical specification is the number to trust, and the technical
specification says 40 ft. If the specific product page for the SKU you are buying
says otherwise, that page wins for that SKU — but do not assume it.

The anti-tangle swivel is worth a word. Corded cleaners twist their cable as they
turn, and on a pool with corners or a freeform shape that twist accumulates over a
season into a genuinely annoying coil. A swivel does not eliminate it. It does
substantially reduce it, and its absence on a cheaper corded cleaner is a real
downgrade rather than a spec-sheet detail.

**Shapes:** rectangular, round, freeform and kidney pools are all listed as
compatible.

**Surface types:** not disclosed by the manufacturer on the pages we checked. If you
have a vinyl liner, a painted surface or anything unusual, confirm with the retailer
before buying rather than assuming.

## Filtration and the maintenance reality

The filter is **top-load**, which is the detail that determines whether you keep
using a pool robot or quietly stop.

Top-load means you lift the lid on top of the machine and pull the filter out from
above, while the robot sits the right way up on the poolside. The alternative —
bottom-access — means turning a wet, heavy machine over to get at the cartridge, and
it is the reason a lot of pool robots end up in a shed. Top-load is the better
arrangement and it is a genuine reason to prefer this model.

**The filter kit itself depends on which SKU you buy.** The current US listing
specifies an Ultra-Fine Filter Kit. The global listing specifies a Fine Filter Kit.
These are different products, and neither page is wrong — they describe different
regional SKUs. We are not going to tell you which one is in the box, because that
depends on the exact item the retailer ships. Check the listing.

**Filter capacity is not disclosed** in litres or any other unit on the sources we
checked.

**There is no full-filter indicator.** This is the one omission that will actually
affect your week. A clogged filter quietly reduces suction, and without an indicator
you find out by noticing the pool is not as clean as it was. The practical answer is
a habit rather than a feature: rinse after every cycle, or at minimum every second
cycle, and do not wait to be told.

## The app

**MyDolphin Plus**, over Wi-Fi.

Disclosed functions are start and stop, plus the weekly scheduling and status
functions that the app ecosystem provides. There is a **weekly timer**, which is the
feature that turns pool cleaning from a task into something that simply happens.

**There is no physical remote control included**, and no remote is listed as
available for this model. If you like the idea of manually steering the cleaner into
a particular corner, that is not available here — the app is the only interface.

Bluetooth support is not disclosed.

A general caution that applies to every app-controlled robot rather than this one
specifically: an app is a dependency for as long as you own the machine. Ask what
happens if the app stops being supported. On this model the answer is reassuring —
the weekly timer is a scheduling function, and the cleaner is fundamentally a device
that runs cycles — but it is the right question to ask of anything that connects.

## Weight, handling and getting it out

**Dry weight: 20.8 lb (9.45 kg).**

That is the number on the box. It is not the number you lift. A pool robot comes out
of the water full of water, and the working weight is meaningfully higher — the
manufacturer does not publish a wet weight, and we are not going to invent one.
Assume noticeably heavier than 21 lb and judge accordingly.

**Dimensions: 10.4 in high, 16.8 in long, 16.4 in wide** (26.5 × 42.7 × 41.6 cm).

**Retrieval** is by handle, after the robot reaches an accessible point. There is no
waterline parking function disclosed, and no retrieval hook is listed. In practice
that means walking the cable to bring the machine to the side, then lifting it out
by hand.

If lifting a wet 10 kg machine out of a pool twice a week is a problem — and for
some buyers it genuinely is — that is worth weighing before you buy, because it is
the single most repeated physical interaction you will have with it.

## The SKU trap

This is the part most reviews skip, and it is the part most likely to cost you.

There are two SKUs in circulation:

- **99996409-PCI** — the current US listing (UPC 850015249969)
- **99996406-PCI** — the global listing

They are the same model family, and they are not identical on paper. Two documented
differences:

**Filter kit.** Ultra-Fine on the US SKU, Fine on the global SKU.

**Pool installation type.** The current US listing states **in-ground only**. The
global listing states in-ground *and* above-ground.

That second one is the important one. We are reviewing this as a US product against
the US SKU, and on that basis **we are not claiming above-ground compatibility**. If
you have an above-ground pool and you find a listing that explicitly confirms
above-ground support for the exact item being shipped, that is your evidence. A
global page describing a different SKU is not.

This is not a criticism of Maytronics — regional variants are normal and the pages
are each accurate about their own product. It is a warning about buying from a
review, including this one, without checking the listing in front of you.

## The verdict

The Nautilus CC Plus with Wi-Fi is a good robot with an honest remit. Corded means
no charging routine and no runtime anxiety. Top-load filtration means you will
actually maintain it. A two-hour cycle and a weekly timer mean the pool gets cleaned
whether or not you think about it. The anti-tangle swivel is the kind of detail that
matters in year two rather than week one.

Against that: no waterline scrubbing, no full-filter indicator, no active brush, no
remote, and a navigation system the manufacturer will not describe. None of those
are dealbreakers for the job it is sold to do. All of them are reasons to buy it for
what it is rather than what a product description implies.

**Buy it if** you have an in-ground pool up to 40 ft, you want floor and wall
cleaning handled on a schedule, and you would rather plug in than charge.

**Do not buy it if** the waterline is your actual complaint, your pool is longer than
40 ft, or you need a cordless machine.

Check the SKU before you buy. Then rinse the filter more often than you think you
need to, because nothing on this robot is going to remind you.
