## The three SERP tests

### 1. Is the head term a shopping SERP, or the shape that killed security robots?

**Shopping, decisively.** `robot snow blower` returns Amazon at position 2 with
*"YARBO 2-Stage 24/7 Autonomous Robot Snow Blower"* — a real product listing for
a real machine, not a conventional blower ranking on a loose match. Alongside
it: `popular_products` carousel, Reddit ×2, YouTube, Lowe's, Trustpilot on the
brand term, and two small commercial operators (smart-dots.com,
cnygreenteam.com).

No institutional procurement. No encyclopedia entry. That was the exact failure
signature of 005 Home Security Robots, and it is absent here.

People Also Ask on the head term is four buyer questions and nothing else:

- Do robot snow blowers work?
- How much does a robot snow blower cost?
- What is the best robotic snow blower?
- Are remote snowblowers worth the money?

Those are four sections of a hub page, written by the searcher.

### 2. Does the conventional snow-blower market swallow the robot term?

**No — and this was the biggest risk going in.** Shared top-ten domains against
the conventional control:

| Pair | Shared |
|---|---:|
| robot snow blower ↔ snow blower | **2/10** (YouTube, Lowe's) |
| robotic snow blower ↔ snow blower | **2/10** |
| yarbo snow blower ↔ snow blower | **1/10** |
| autonomous snow blower ↔ snow blower | **2/10** |
| best robot snow blower ↔ snow blower | **2/10** |

`snow blower` returns Lowe's, PowerSmart, Ace Hardware, Toro, Ariens, Husqvarna
and Consumer Reports. `robot snow blower` returns almost none of them. These are
two different results pages, which is the same 2/10 separation that justified
building the lawn best-of page on 11 August.

This is the opposite of the pet-camera finding, where `best pet camera robot`
turned out to be answered by Wirecutter's best pet cameras.

### 3. Does the brand own the category?

**Largely, yes — and this is the real constraint.**

| Pair | Shared |
|---|---:|
| robot snow blower ↔ yarbo | **7/10** |
| robot snow blower ↔ yarbo snow blower | **6/10** |

Yarbo brand terms total roughly **35,700/mo** against roughly **22,700** for
every generic robot phrasing combined. `yarbo` alone (18,100) outranks `robot
snow blower` (12,100).

Same shape as Grillbot, which survived at 18,100 brand against 5,400 category —
but more pronounced, because here the brand is bigger than the category *and*
holds position 1 on its own terms with yarbo.com.

Under the standing rule that this site does not take a brand term off its owner
(`litter robot` 165,000, `roborock` 110,000 and `irobot roomba` 27,100 were all
refused on exactly this ground), the buildable demand is the generic column:
**≈22,700/mo at KD 0–9**, which is the lowest difficulty of any category ever
researched for this site.

### 4. Seasonality — the test no other category has needed

Every category on this site sells year-round. This one does not.

`robot snow blower` runs 1,600 in August and 60,500 in January: a **38× swing**.
`yarbo snow blower` is worse at **103×** — 720 in July against 74,000 in
January. `autonomous snow blower` is **158×**.

Two readings, and both are true:

- **Against it.** For roughly eight months of the year the category earns close
  to nothing. Annualised, the 12,100 headline is a monthly average that is never
  actually 12,100 in any month — it is a few hundred or it is tens of thousands.
- **For it.** Researched in August, a page has four to five months to age and
  rank before the demand arrives. Every other category on this site was built
  into demand that already existed and had incumbents holding it. This is the
  only one where the calendar offers a head start, and KD 0 means there is
  almost nothing to displace.

## What this research does NOT establish

Stated plainly, because the next step depends on it and the run was not
scoped to answer it.

- **Whether there is more than one product.** Yarbo is the only machine that
  appeared repeatedly with US retail presence. thesnowbot.com, smart-dots.com
  and cnygreenteam.com appeared once or twice each and were not verified — two
  of them read as commercial/municipal rather than consumer. A category with one
  buyable product is a review page, not a hub with a matcher: BotMatch needs at
  least two published products before it can answer anything
  (`MIN_PRODUCTS_FOR_A_MATCH`).
- **Yarbo's price, availability, affiliate programme or model line-up.** None of
  it was checked. `yarbo m1` at 50/mo and `yarbo s1` at 20/mo suggest a
  multi-module system rather than a single SKU, which would need the same
  identity verification every other product on this site has had.
- **Whether the Amazon listing is first-party.** It carries the YARBO name in
  the title, which is where the roborock S8 MaxV Ultra investigation started
  and ended badly.

### Conflicts found, recorded rather than resolved

1. **Runtime: 90 minutes against 4 hours.** yarbo.com states "approximately 90
   minutes". Third-party reviews (electrokit.blog, lawncareguides.com) state
   "up to 4 hours" on a 36V/38.4Ah pack. First-party wins; the 4-hour figure is
   not carried.
2. **Throw distance: feet against yards on Yarbo's own site.** The Snow Blower
   Module page reads "Throws snow up to 40 feet" and, in the same panel,
   "6–40 Yards Throw Control". One of the two is wrong and both are Yarbo's.
3. **Price: $4,999 against $1,999.** Third-party reviews headline "$4,999" and
   also describe a "standalone snow blower package"; the module alone is
   $1,299. Anything published here has to name which SKU it is pricing.
4. **Zero reviews at Lowe's.** No owner evidence exists at that retailer at
   all, which for a $4,999 purchase is worth stating rather than filling with
   third-party enthusiasm.

### What could not be read

The Amazon listing's own details table. WebFetch returned the page head only,
and DataForSEO's Amazon merchant endpoints are not enabled on this account
(`40501 Invalid Field`). Brand and model therefore rest on Lowe's and Best Buy,
which is a stronger position than the WYBOT C1 record started from, but the
Amazon first-party check is outstanding and would be needed before a buy link
ships.

## Adversarial review, and what it broke — 11 August 2026

The verdict below went out for adversarial review. Four of its criticisms
landed, and this section records them before the corrected verdict rather than
quietly rewriting history above.

### Conceded 1: the stated reason for NO-GO contradicted the Grillbot precedent

The first verdict said no-go because "all four category surfaces are
comparative and there is nothing to compare". That is not the bar this site
actually applies. **006 Grill Cleaning Robots was built as a one-product
category with its matcher gated off, and it earns** — on generic volume of
5,400 against snow's 22,700. Applied consistently, Grillbot calibration says
snow *qualifies*.

Using "nothing to compare" as the reason silently moved the category bar and
would have made Grillbot's survival unexplainable to the next person. The
defensible reasons are different and are now stated as such:

- Yarbo's Core **is** the lawn platform. The entity belongs under lawn on the
  merits, not as a consolation prize for a failed category.
- `robot snow blower` shares **7/10** domains with `yarbo`. Google has already
  collapsed the generic market onto one brand, so a category shell would be
  four surfaces chasing one SERP.

### Conceded 2: the three-page map broke two house rules, on its own data

- **Rule 2 (>5/10 shared = merge).** Page 1 (Yarbo review) and page 2
  (targeting `robot snow blower`) sit at 7/10 and 6/10 against the brand terms.
  The map proposed as separate URLs two pages its own measurements said were
  one SERP.
- **Rule 5 (no prices in a guide).** Page 3 was a cost guide whose entire
  purpose was the $1,299-against-$4,999 question. The angle is genuinely good,
  which is exactly why the rule got forgotten.

### Conceded 3: "~36,000/mo combined" was double-counting

That figure summed brand and generic volumes across SERPs the same document
had just proved were 6–7/10 identical. The same searchers were counted twice.
**Honest unique demand is ~20,000–22,000/mo.**

## Go / no-go — final

**NO-GO as a category. ONE page, not three.**

### Why no-go, stated correctly this time

Not "nothing to compare" — Grillbot disproves that bar. Two reasons:

1. **Google has already collapsed this market onto one brand.**
   `robot snow blower` shares 7/10 domains with `yarbo` and 6/10 with
   `yarbo snow blower`. A hub, a comparison table, a matcher and a best-of
   would be four URLs competing for a single results page that Yarbo already
   holds position 1 of.
2. **The entity belongs under lawn on the merits.** Yarbo is a modular yard
   robot; the snow blower is one attachment on the same Core that carries the
   mower. Filing it under robotic-lawn-mowers describes the product rather
   than working around a failed category.

`best robot snow blower` (140/mo) is refused outright under rule 4 — a best-of
with one product is an advertisement, which is the standard applied when this
site declined to invent awards for the WINBOT W1 PRO and the HOBOT 298.

### Timing, which the seasonality dictates

`yarbo snow blower` runs 720 in July to 74,000 in January — 103×. A page
published into that curve has no chance against one that has been indexed for
three months.

- **Review live by 15 September.** That is the deliverable date, not a
  preference.
- **Cross-links from the lawn hub and the lawn best-of go up with it**, both of
  which exist as of 11 August. Links stay permanent year-round; the page states
  its own seasonality under rule 7 rather than being seasonally hidden.
- **First week of January: price re-read and refresh.** Last January's spike was
  partly news-driven — the ABC7 viral video is still ranking on the head term
  in August — and freshness will decide January CTR.
- **Before any buy link ships:** Amazon's own details table still has not been
  read first-party. Brand and Model Number rest on Lowe's ("Model #YARBO S1")
  and Best Buy. That check is outstanding.

### What would flip this to a full category

One thing only: **a second consumer autonomous machine from a distinct
manufacturer, in mainstream US retail.** The Yarbo M Series does not count —
same maker, and pre-order. Remote-control and hybrid gas machines do not count;
they are a different class, though the PAA question *"are remote snowblowers
worth the money"* earns them a walk-away paragraph inside the review.
