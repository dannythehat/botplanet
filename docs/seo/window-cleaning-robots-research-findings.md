## 2. Seasonality — the strategic finding, and it is the opposite of pool

| Month | Volume |

| March / May | 18,100 |
| **December** | **18,100** ← gift spike |

| Jan / Feb / Oct | 9,900 |
| **Jul / Aug / Sep** | **8,100** ← we are here |

Two peaks: **spring cleaning (Mar–May)** and **Christmas (Dec)**. The trough
is high summer, which is exactly now.

**Why this matters more than the volume number.** The pool category was
published in August into its own December trough — 9,900 against a June peak
of 90,500 — so its first months live at the bottom of its curve. Window robots
published now get roughly **four months to mature before December's 18,100**
and **eight before April's 22,200**. Same publishing date, opposite outcome.
This category is correctly timed and pool was not.

## 3. What the SERPs actually say

**One competitor holds almost everything.** NYTimes (Wirecutter) ranks top-3
for `window cleaning robot`, `best window cleaning robot`, `best robot window
cleaner`, `best for high rise`, `best cordless`, `best frameless` and
`comparison` — with a *single generalist article*. ecovacs.com (the
manufacturer) ranks alongside it on nearly every term. TechRadar is the other
recurring name.

**Reddit dominates every question SERP** — do they work, are they worth it,
streaks, how they stay on the glass. Real demand that no publisher is
answering properly.

**The decisive finding: the "best X for Y" SERPs are not distinct.**
`best budget`, `best cordless`, `best for high rise` and `best for frameless
glass` return essentially the **same top six** (NYT, ecovacs, techradar,
amazon, reddit). Google does not treat these as separate intents.

This is the reverse of pool, where `best cordless robotic pool cleaner` had
its own list-content SERP at 22,200/KD 0 and clearly earned its own page.
Here, four sub-best-of pages would fight each other and the hub for one
undifferentiated result set.

**Three seeded queries returned no SERP at all** — `winbot vs hobot window
cleaner`, `can window cleaning robots fall`, `which window cleaning robot
should i buy`. Negligible demand. They are strong *sections*, not pages.

## 5. Cannibalisation ledger

- **Hub owns** `window cleaning robot` and its four groupings, plus
  `automatic window cleaner` (8,100) and `glass cleaning robot` (320).
- **Best-of owns** `best …` modifiers only, and absorbs budget / cordless /
  high-rise / frameless as sections. It never chases the head term.
- **Reviews own** their model name + `review` only. No review chases the head
  term or a `best` modifier.
- **The guide owns** the works / worth-it / how-it-works questions. The hub
  answers them in a sentence and links out; it does not compete.
- `window cleaning robot reviews` (1,000) is **deliberately assigned to the
  best-of**, not to any single review — a plural-reviews searcher wants a
  comparison, and eleven pool reviews taught us a single review never wins it.

## 6. The angles this category argues about

Every one of these is a section the SERP's PAA is asking for and nobody is
answering well:

- **Will it fall?** Every model ships a safety tether and a UPS backup; the
  PAA asks it constantly. Manufacturers answer with protection-level counts.
- **Frameless glass.** The hard exclusion. Several models need a frame to
  find an edge.
- **Streaking on the first pass.** The single most common owner complaint.
- **Consumables.** Pads and solution run out mid-clean — buy spares upfront.
- **Power cut behaviour.** What the UPS actually does, and for how long.
- **Corners and edges.** Where every one of them is weakest.
- **Can you use Windex?** In the PAA on multiple model SERPs.
