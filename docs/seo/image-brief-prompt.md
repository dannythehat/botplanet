# The prompt to give your image AI

Paste the block below. It is written from what actually went wrong on 9 August,
so it is worth keeping rather than rewriting each round.

---

You are producing product artwork for BotPlanet, a robotics review site. Four
images per product, in this order, and nothing else:

1. **hero** — the product in the situation a buyer imagines using it in. A room,
   a person or an animal, real light. Not a studio cut-out.
2. **figure-1** — the one thing that makes this model different from its nearest
   sibling. If two models are easily confused, put them in the same frame at the
   same scale.
3. **figure-2** — the second thing worth showing: what it does when nobody is
   watching, what comes in the box, or how big it actually is next to something
   ordinary.
4. **card** — the product alone, three-quarter view, clean background. This one
   is used small, in a grid, so it must read at 360 pixels wide.

## Hard requirements

**Size and format.** PNG, at least 1440 pixels on the long edge. 1254×1254 or
1448×1086 are ideal. Never JPEG, never under 1000 pixels. Anything smaller
cannot fill the responsive sizes the site serves and will look soft on a laptop.

**One file per slot, uploaded in slot order.** No extras between them. If you
supply a fifth image it will not be published, because there is no slot for it
and guessing where it goes is how the wrong picture ends up on the wrong page.

**Never print a specification into the picture.** No resolution, no battery
life, no dimensions, no price, no runtime. Two reasons, both practical. Text in
an image cannot carry the date it was checked or the source it came from, and
every number on this site carries both. And it cannot be corrected — a caption
is one edit, a rendered figure is a regeneration. On 9 August two cards said
"1080p" for cameras that are 2K and 2.5K, and one size chart said 3.1 inches for
a robot that is 3.8. All three had to be set aside.

**Never print a claim the site has not tested.** Especially never print anything
about encryption, data handling, privacy, safety, or health. A panel reading
"end-to-end encryption" or "reduces stress in people living with dementia",
under the BotPlanet logo, is BotPlanet asserting it. We have not tested it and
cannot stand behind it. Say what the picture shows, not what the product
promises.

**Keep BotPlanet branding minimal.** A logo is fine. A headline naming the
product is fine. Feature chips, bullet lists and marketing straplines are not —
they turn a photograph into a poster, and a poster cannot be used as the
product's image in search results.

**Never print the brief into the picture.** If the instruction says "a rolling
robot in a hallway with a dog watching", the image shows that — it does not
print that sentence as body copy.

**Never print the slot names.** No "Figure-1", no "Card". A contact sheet of
four panels labelled with their slot names is a layout mock, not artwork, and
cannot be used for any of them.

**Get the product right.** The specific model, not the family. Where a maker
sells several near-identical models, the one named is the one to draw. If you
show two models in one frame, label them with their model names only — no specs.

## What good looks like

The best files from 9 August were the plain ones: a golden retriever taking a
treat from an open hopper on a kitchen floor; a small robot sitting in an open
palm with nothing else in frame; two cameras side by side on the same floor at
the same scale, labelled with just their names. No panels, no chips, no
straplines. Every one of those went straight in.

---

## Checking a batch before you upload it

Run down this list once per image:

- Is it PNG, 1440 pixels or more on the long edge?
- Is there a number in the picture? Remove it.
- Is there a claim in the picture? Remove it.
- Does the picture print any part of the brief, or any slot name? Remove it.
- Is it the right model, and is the model that's shown the one named?
- Would this read at 360 pixels wide? Only the `card` has to.
- Four files, in slot order, nothing extra.
