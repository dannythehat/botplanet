(127 terms, 12 leads, 12 live SERP reads; five timed out and were re-run). Nothing is built. This is the
evidence for a discussion.

**Things the data says plainly:**
* Almost none of the volume is in our existing categories as news. "new robot vacuum" is 320, "new robot lawn
  mower" 30, "pool robot news" and every "<brand> new release" term is under 20. The volume is in humanoid, AI,
  robot dog, cooking and companion robots.
* Several of the biggest names cannot be bought (Tesla Optimus, Figure 03). They are news and traffic, not
  affiliate revenue. The buyable ones are 1X NEO, Unitree G1 and R1, and a growing set of smaller companion and
  kitchen robots.
* Terms to avoid on brand grounds: "ai robot girlfriend" (2,900), "humanoid female robots for sale" (880) and
  similar. They have volume and no place on this site.

## 2. What the live results show

* **robot news / ai robot news:** publisher results (Robot Report, IEEE, MIT, BBC, TechCrunch, Guardian, Inc).
  Hard to enter. A People Also Ask box carries "Is there a $20,000 robot that can do chores for me?"
* **home robot / humanoid robot for home:** led by 1X's own pages, then WSJ, CNET, Business Insider, BBC,
  Reddit. A buyer guide that answers "can I actually buy one, and what does it cost" is missing.
* **robots for sale:** retailers and product pages (RoboStore, Unitree shop, Robots International, RobotShop,
  a keyirobot.com guide). Difficulty 6. A commercial page with real products and honest "not for sale" notes
  has room.
* **unitree g1 price:** small sites (9srobotics, roboticscenter.ai, ustechautomations), a Hacker News thread, eBay.
  Low authority. A price-and-availability page we keep current can compete.
* **new robot gadgets / robot gifts:** roundup blogs (Gadget Flow, SlashGear, RobotShop, Amazon). Roundups work.
* **new robots 2026:** news and trend pieces (Reuters, StartUs, Yahoo, NVIDIA, humanoid.guide). Mixed.
* **ces robots:** event coverage, peaking in January (4,400).
* The People Also Ask line "Is there a $20,000 robot that can do chores for me?" appears in six of the twelve
  results. One honest page that answers it, and the 1X NEO question behind it, is the strongest single hook found.
* Overlap between these results is almost nil, so by the one-URL rule they are separate pages, not one.

## 3. What this means for the plan

**Do not chase "robot news" as a head term.** Win on specific, buyer-useful terms and use the blog for freshness,
internal links and social, not as the main traffic bet.

Recommended pages, in order of evidence:
1. **`/new-robots/`, the arrivals list.** Target: new robots (2,400), new robots 2026, cool robots (2,900, KD 0),
   robot gadgets. Only products we can send a buyer to. Updated as items are added, each dated.
2. **A "can you buy a humanoid robot" guide.** Targets: humanoid robot for sale (4,400, KD 15), buy humanoid
   robot (4,400, KD 10), humanoid robot price, home humanoid robot, the $20,000-chores question. Honest about
   what ships, what is a pre-order and what is not for sale.
3. **A robot dog best-of or hub.** robot dog is 90,500 at KD 7 and peaks at 165,000. We already have the robot
   dog toys guide (14,800). The head term is the largest opportunity found.
4. **Unitree pages:** G1 (9,900), R1 (2,400, KD 1), Go2 (2,900), H2 (880, KD 3). Price and availability pages.
5. **Cooking / kitchen robots** (8,100 and 1,900) and **laundry folding robots** (1,600): new categories with low
   difficulty. They need a buyable product check first.
6. **The blog** (`/news/`): news briefs, explainers and event roundups that link into pages 1 to 5 and the
   existing category money pages.

## 4. The "New Robots" entry rule (so the list stays honest)

A robot goes on the list only if it passes every one of these:
* A US customer can order it now, or there is a dated pre-order with a stated ship window.
* A real price, from a real retailer or the maker.
* Identity checked: exact model, not a bundle or a look-alike listing.
* A working affiliate route (Amazon, Awin, CJ, Impact, Rakuten, ShareASale, FlexOffers, or a direct programme),
  or it is listed as "news only" with no buy button.
* A return policy we can state.
Optimus and Figure 03 fail the first test, so they appear in news posts, never on the list.

## 5. Automation, proposed (human approves everything that publishes)

1. **Watch:** brand newsrooms, The Robot Report and IEEE feeds, Google Alerts, Kickstarter and Indiegogo, retailer
   new-arrival pages, SerpApi and DataForSEO trend checks.
2. **Score:** is it buyable, is there an affiliate route, is there search demand, does it match a category.
3. **Draft:** a news brief or arrivals entry in the page-file format, with sources linked and every figure
   attributed. Prices and availability are checked live, never typed from memory.
4. **Review:** nothing publishes without the owner's approval.
5. **Link:** the internal-link anchors and the "older pages must link to new ones" rule run automatically.
6. **Social:** each post generates a short script and image brief for Pinterest, YouTube Shorts and TikTok, X, and
   an Instagram carousel, each with tracked links.
7. **Index:** submit through Search Console (needs a connection we do not have yet).
8. **Digest:** a weekly email of what was added.

## 6. Open decisions for the owner
1. Names and addresses: `/news/` or `/blog/`, and `/new-robots/`.
2. Scope: do humanoid, robot dog and AI robots belong as news and guides even when we cannot sell them?
3. Automation level: drafts for approval (recommended) or more.
4. Which affiliate accounts already exist, so the arrivals list starts with products we can monetise.
5. Social order of play (the data and format points to Pinterest, short video and X first).
6. Cadence: for example three to five briefs a week and one evergreen guide a week.
7. A Search Console connection or a CSV export so we can see what is actually indexed.
