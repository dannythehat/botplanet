The ROLA PetPal is the only pet camera we know of that drives to your dog and
then gives it a treat.

That sentence is the entire product, and at $179 it is the reason to choose it
over anything else Enabot sells.

## Treats that come to the animal

Furbo tosses a treat from a shelf. It works, and it means the dog has to be in
the room with the shelf.

The PetPal drives. You find the dog wherever it has gone, talk to it, and
dispense from there. For an anxious animal that has taken itself off to a
bedroom, the difference between a camera that calls it and a camera that goes
to it is the difference between a feature and a use.

The dispenser is built in rather than an accessory. There is also an optional
Interactive Module, sold separately, which Enabot does not price on the
listing — budget for it as an unknown rather than a certainty.

## The rest of it

**2.5K camera** with night vision, which is sharper than the $149 Air 2 and
below the $359 Air 2 Plus.

**Two-way talk**, driven manually from the app.

**Auto-docking.** It takes itself back to charge, which on a treat robot
matters more than on a plain camera — an empty battery means no treats.

**No subscription.** The listing answers the question outright: "Do I need a
subscription to use ROLA PetPal? No."

## It is much bigger than the others

9.02 × 9.02 × 10 inches, against 3.74 inches square for the Air 2. Roughly
three times the footprint, and it has to be: the treats go somewhere.

That rules out the under-furniture trick the EBO SE does. This is a robot that
drives around rooms, not under them, which for a dog is the right choice and
for a cat is usually the wrong one.

## Who this is for

Dog owners. Specifically, dog owners whose animal is anxious when alone and who
want to do something about it rather than watch it happen.

It is also the machine for a household where somebody wants to feel useful from
the office. Watching is passive; feeding is not.

## Who should look elsewhere

**Cat owners, mostly.** Cats hide in low places, this is a tall robot, and
treat-dispensing motivates a dog far more reliably than a cat. If you own a cat
and what you actually want automated is the tray rather than the watching, a
self-cleaning litter box is the machine that solves your problem and this is
not.

**Anyone whose pet is upstairs.** Wheels, again.

**Anyone who wants the cheapest way in.** The EBO SE at $119 does the driving
and the talking without the treats.

**Anyone confused by the ROLA name.** The **ROLA Mini** at $139 shares it and
has **no treat dispenser**. The dispenser is the only reason to pay $179, so
check the model name before ordering.

## Where this sits in the range

This is the only Enabot with a treat dispenser and the largest of the seven the
company sells. The Enabot range page covers the rest, including the ROLA Mini
that shares this one's name and does not share its dispenser.

## What we cannot tell you

**What the Interactive Module costs or does.** Sold separately and not priced
on the listing.

**Treat capacity or which sizes fit.** Not published, and it is the first thing
a dog owner would ask.

**Battery runtime.** Same gap as the rest of the range: no duration published
anywhere we could read.

**How it holds up.** 4.0 stars from 26 ratings. That is a new product and not
enough feedback to draw a conclusion from.
