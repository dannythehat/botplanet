Seven hundred dollars for a litter box. That is the number everybody starts
with, and the honest way to think about it is not as a gadget but as the
difference between a chore you do twice a day and one you do once a week.

The Litter-Robot 4 takes cats from 3 to 25 lb through an entry 15.75 inches
square, sifts after every visit, and drops the waste into a drawer you empty
about every eight days for one cat. Whisker publishes those figures, which is
more than two of its three competitors manage.

## Who should not buy this

Anyone with a kitten under three pounds. Whisker's stated minimum is 3 lb and
the machine works by weight — under the threshold it does not know the cat is
in there, which is the whole reason the sensor exists. Casa Leo's Leo's Loo Too
detects from 1 lb and is the answer under that threshold.

Anyone who will not use clumping clay litter. Whisker is specific: standard
clumping clay works, plant-based and non-clumping do not. If you have spent
years on walnut or paper, this box asks you to stop.

And anyone who wants the health history without a monthly fee. More on that
below, because it is the part of the pitch that quietly changes shape after you
buy.

## The entry is the specification nobody compares

Fifteen and three-quarter inches square, with a globe 16.5 inches high inside.

That matters more than any other number here and almost nobody prints it in a
comparison. A cat that has to duck and squeeze to get in is a cat that
eventually stops going in, and the failure mode of an automatic litter box is
not that it breaks — it is that the cat goes on the rug instead. The PETKIT
Purobot Max Pro 2, which is called Max and costs two hundred dollars less, has
an entrance 10.51 inches wide and 10.03 inches high. That is five inches
narrower and it is the reason to look at the Litter-Robot for a big cat even
though the PETKIT's stated weight ceiling is only three pounds lower.

What to do with that: measure your cat's shoulder height standing, and if the
answer is anywhere near ten inches, the entry size decides this purchase and
nothing else does.

## The subscription question

The Whisker app is not required. The box runs, sifts and fills its drawer with
no account at all, and that is worth saying plainly because subscription
anxiety is the most common objection in this category.

What the app gives you free is seven days of your cats' visits and individual
weights. Whisker+ extends that to two years of history with trends and daily
reports, for a monthly fee.

Seven days is enough to notice that something changed this week. It is not
enough to notice that your cat has been drinking more since March, which is the
kind of trend that gets a kidney problem caught early — and that is the thing
the health tracking is sold on. The tracking is real either way. The *history*
is the paid part, and the pitch does not make that obvious until you are in the
app looking for last month.

## What it costs after the seven hundred

The drawer takes ordinary clumping clay from any shop, which is the single
biggest running-cost difference between this and the PetSafe ScoopFree at a
third of the price — the PetSafe works only with PetSafe's own crystal trays,
for the life of the machine. Over three years that gap closes a good deal of
the purchase-price difference.

The warranty is one year, with three years available for $100 at purchase. One
year on a $699 appliance with a motor, a globe that rotates and a weight sensor
is short, and the fact that Whisker sells the extension separately tells you
what Whisker thinks about that.

## What we could not find out

Whisker publishes a weight range, an entry size, a globe height, a litter type
and a warranty. It does not publish a waste drawer volume in litres — the
figure it gives is "as low as once every 8 days", which depends on how many
cats and how big they are and is therefore not comparable with the PETKIT's
8 litres or Casa Leo's 9.5.

It does not publish a decibel figure, a cycle time, or the weight of the unit.
Those are recorded as not disclosed rather than borrowed from a review site
that borrowed them from another review site.

## Who should buy one

Somebody with a large cat, ordinary clumping litter and seven hundred dollars,
who wants the biggest opening in the category and does not need two years of
health history. That describes a lot of people and this is the right answer for
them.

Somebody with a kitten should buy the Casa Leo. Somebody who wants per-cat
recognition rather than per-visit weights should look at the PETKIT and save
$190. Somebody who wants the cheapest hands-off box and does not mind being
tied to one brand's trays should read the PetSafe review and then look hard at
its rating.
