Eighty per cent slopes for $1,599.99. The only other machine in our catalogue
that climbs like this is the Mammotion LUBA 3 AWD, and it starts eight hundred
dollars higher.

The catch is the ground: 1,000 square metres, which is a quarter of an acre.
This is a steep small garden's mower and it is built for nothing else.

## Who should not buy this

Anybody with more than a quarter acre. Dreame rates it at 1,000 m² — about
10,764 square feet — and the lawn matcher treats a rated area as a rule-out
rather than a ranking, because a mower that cannot finish is not a slow mower,
it is an unfinished lawn. Over that, the LUBA 3 AWD 1500H covers 0.37 acre and
the 3000H covers 0.75.

Anybody with a flat quarter acre. All-wheel drive is the whole reason for the
price here, and on level ground the Segway Navimow i110N covers the same
quarter acre for $1,099 and the eufy E15 does 800 m² for $1,199.99.

## What this product was very nearly not

The brief that started this catalogue named a "Dreame A1". Six passes across
search and product lookups on 8 August 2026 returned no such current product.
Dreame's mowers are the A3 AWD 1000 and the A3 AWD Pro.

We recorded the failure and did not silently substitute, because a successor is
a different product and has to be judged on its own. This page is the judging.
If you arrived here looking for an A1, that is what happened to it as far as we
can see from outside — and we cannot tell you whether it was withdrawn or never
existed under that name.

## OmniSense 3.0, and why it suits a steep garden

360-degree 3D LiDAR plus binocular AI vision. Not satellite positioning.

That distinction decides more purchases in this category than any other, and
almost nobody leads with it. A mower that finds itself by satellite is defeated
by a canopy of mature trees or a tall building close by, and there is no setting
that fixes it. Steep gardens are frequently wooded gardens — banks are where
people plant things — so a machine that climbs but needs clear sky solves half
the problem.

Cutting height runs 1.2 to 3.9 inches, which is a wider range than the Segway,
the WORX or the eufy manage and covers warm-season grasses that want to be left
long. It crosses obstacles up to 2.17 inches, and EdgeMaster trims to within
1.91 inches of a fence line.

## Eighty per cent against forty-five

38.7 degrees. To put it beside the rest of what we hold:

- This machine and the Mammotion LUBA 3 AWD — 80%
- Husqvarna Automower 410iQ — 45% inside the area, 15% at the boundary
- Segway Navimow i110N and WORX Landroid Vision — 30%
- eufy E15 — 18°

The Husqvarna's second figure is the one to think about if you are comparing:
a machine can be rated to cut a slope and not rated to run along its edge, and
the edge is where a bank meets a fence.

## What we could not find out

Dreame publishes slope, drive, coverage, cutting height, obstacle crossing,
edge distance and the navigation stack. It does not publish a cutting width for
the A3 AWD 1000 anywhere we could read — the 15.8-inch dual-blade figure in
circulation belongs to the Pro, which is a different and dearer machine, and we
are not carrying it across.

Battery capacity, runtime, noise, IP rating, weight and warranty are also not
published on the pages we read, and are recorded as not disclosed. That is a
thinner sheet than Husqvarna's and it is worth knowing when you are spending
sixteen hundred dollars.

## Who should buy one

Somebody with a steep quarter acre, possibly under trees, who does not want to
pay LUBA money. It climbs the same eighty per cent as machines costing twice
as much, it navigates without needing to see the sky, and it cuts higher than
anything else at its price.

Anybody with more grass than that should buy a LUBA 3. Anybody with a flat
quarter acre is paying eight hundred dollars for wheels they will not use.
