A Grillbot sits on the grates, you press a button, and it scrubs on its own
for as long as you set the timer.

That is the whole product. It is worth being clear about what it is not: it is
not a deep clean, it is not a replacement for pulling the grates out twice a
season, and at $129.99 it is roughly eight times the price of a bristle-free
brush that removes the same hazard.

## What it actually does

Three motors drive three brush heads across the grates. The unit steers itself
with what Grillbot calls a computer brain adjusting speed and direction, and
an LCD timer on the top decides how long it runs. You set the time, close the
lid, and walk away. It shuts itself off.

It works on a hot grill or a cold one. That is the difference that matters
most in practice: the easiest time to shift residue is straight after cooking
while the grates are still hot, and that is exactly when nobody wants to stand
over a grill scrubbing.

## The bristle question, which is the real argument

Loose wire bristles from conventional grill brushes end up in food. It is a
documented injury, and it is the honest case for this whole category.

Grillbot's three heads are nylon, brass and stainless steel — none of them the
loose twisted wire that breaks off. Grillbot's own guidance is nylon for most
grills, brass for porcelain and stainless, stainless steel for cast iron and
expanded steel.

**But a bristle-free brush costs about fifteen dollars and removes the same
risk.** If avoiding wire is the only reason you are here, buy the brush. The
robot earns its money on the not-standing-there part, not on the safety part.

## What we could not find out

The published specification is thin, and some of what a buyer needs is simply
not disclosed. Grillbot states three motors, three brush
types, hot-or-cold operation, an LCD timer with auto shut-off and a one-year
warranty. It does not publish a runtime for the standard battery, a cleaning
cycle length, a weight, or dimensions — and the units it fits are described as
"all grills" rather than a size range.

The runtime figure it does publish belongs to the upgraded lithium battery:
up to eight hours, sold separately. Read carefully, that is a statement about
an accessory, not about what arrives in the box.

## The warranty is a year, and there is a reason to notice

One year is short for a $130 appliance that lives outdoors and gets put on hot
metal. Grillbot sells a three-year extension for $26.95 as a separate product,
which tells you what the company thinks the risk is. Replacement batteries are
$22.95 and a replacement USB charger is $12.95, both listed on its own store —
so the consumable costs are at least visible, which is more than most makers
in this category manage.

## What we could not verify about the listing

Amazon served a bot check rather than a product page when we tried to read the
listing directly on 10 August 2026, so the identity of the pinned ASIN rests
on Amazon's own brand field returned through a third-party API on that date,
not on a page we read ourselves. The price, the 4.1-star rating and the 5,400
ratings come from the same read and carry the same date.

There are four Grillbot ASINs sharing one review pool — red and black, each
with and without a carry case. They are the same machine. A link that does not
force a variant can land you on the $139.99 bundle when you meant the $129.99
unit.

## Who should buy one

Somebody who grills often enough that scrubbing has become the reason they do
not, and who would rather spend $130 once than keep not doing it. That is a
real person and this is a real answer for them.

Anyone buying it because wire bristles frighten them should buy a $15
bristle-free brush instead and keep the difference. Anyone expecting a deep
clean should know that this is maintenance between proper cleans, not a
substitute for one.
