## What the real EMO actually is

A small bipedal robot that walks on two legs, wears headphones and rides a
skateboard. The design is deliberately pop-culture — Living.AI describes street
style, DJs and rappers — and it is a large part of why this product became the
reference point for the category.

**It sees.** An HD camera with face recognition that remembers you and other
people in the house, and gets to know you by seeing you daily.

**It hears properly.** A four-microphone array that captures sound and locates
which direction it came from, so it turns toward whoever spoke.

**It thinks on board.** More than ten internal sensors feeding a neural network
processor with AI models running locally, rather than everything going to a
server.

**Its skateboard is the charger.** EMO powers up wirelessly by standing on it,
and the same pad will charge a phone.

There is an app for games, recorded moments and the smart light, and the robot
takes over-the-air updates.

## What to buy instead, if you want one this week

Three machines are genuinely available and each answers a different part of
EMO's appeal.

**Eilik, $139.99.** The closest thing in spirit at a fifth of the effort to
obtain. Expressive, reactive, no account and no subscription, and two of them
interact with each other. It does not converse and it runs 90 minutes. If what
you liked about EMO was the character, this is the substitute.

**Loona, $499.** If what you liked about EMO was that it sees you, recognises
gestures and follows you around, Loona does all of that and more, with a camera
that doubles as a home monitor. It charges longer than it plays.

**Moflin, $429.** If what you actually wanted was something to look after
rather than something clever, this is the honest answer, and it is nothing like
EMO.

## Who should hold out for the real thing

Anyone who wants specifically this robot — the biped, the headphones, the
skateboard — and is content to order direct and wait. Nothing else walks on two
legs at this size, and the imitations are not close on the hardware even when
they are close on the photograph.

## Who should not

Anyone buying a gift on a deadline. Direct ordering from a Shenzhen maker is a
different proposition from Amazon returns, and a robot that arrives in January
is not a Christmas present.

Anyone who would end up buying whatever the search results offered. That is how
you get an EMOPET.
