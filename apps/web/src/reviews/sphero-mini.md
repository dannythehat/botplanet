The Sphero Mini is $50 and runs the same app as the $179 BOLT.

That is the whole reason to buy one. It is the cheapest honest way to find out
whether a child likes coding, before anybody spends real money on the answer.

## Same software, quarter of the price

Sphero Edu. Drawing, block coding and text — the identical app the BOLT uses.

The lessons are the same lessons. The programs a child writes here would run on
the expensive robot with no changes. What $129 more buys on the BOLT is the LED
matrix, the sensors, the four-hour battery and the size; it does not buy a
different curriculum.

So the sensible order is this one first, and the BOLT later if the interest
survives a fortnight.

## One hour, and that is the thing people are annoyed by

About sixty minutes of play per charge. It is the specification, not a fault of
a bad unit.

An hour is a session. It is not an afternoon, it is not a car journey, and it
is not a birthday party. If the plan involves a room of children taking turns,
this robot runs out before they do.

The shell opens to charge, which is fiddly in small hands and the part most
likely to be broken by an eight-year-old in a hurry.

## The size cuts both ways

1.57 inches across — a ping-pong ball. 0.11 kg.

Small enough to live in a coat pocket and go to a grandparent's house, which is
genuinely useful. Also small enough to roll under a bookcase and stay there,
and light enough that a programmed route on carpet does not always go where the
code said it would. On a hard floor it behaves.

## It doubles as a game controller

Tilt it to steer. The Sphero Play app carries arcade games it drives.

Worth knowing because it is the feature that keeps a child holding the thing on
the days they have no interest in writing programs. A coding robot that gets
used as a toy is a coding robot that is still in the house in March.

Joystick and Slingshot drive modes are there too, alongside the programmable
LED and a gyroscope and accelerometer the code can read.

## Who this is for

A first coding robot. A present for a child who has said something vague about
robots and may or may not have meant it.

At $50 the wrong answer costs $50, and that is the number that makes this the
right starting point rather than a compromise.

## Who should look elsewhere

**Anyone who already knows the interest is real.** Buy the BOLT and skip the
step. The Mini's ceiling is its battery and its lack of a display.

**Under-eights.** Rated 8+, and the app is the whole experience. For a
four-year-old the Sphero indi does this without a screen at all.

**Anyone wanting an afternoon of play.** One hour. It will not change.

**A child who wants to build something.** This is a sealed ball. The Makeblock
mBot is assembled from parts and costs $69.

## What we cannot tell you

**Which colour you receive.** Sphero sells the Mini in several and our buy
button points at the Blue, ASIN B072B6QVVW. Other colours are separate
listings at prices we have not checked.

**How the shell holds up.** It opens for charging, it is the obvious failure
point, and no manufacturer figure exists for how many cycles it takes.

**Real battery life against the stated hour.** We have not run one down
ourselves. Every figure on this page is Sphero's.
