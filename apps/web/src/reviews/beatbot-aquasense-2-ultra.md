**The short answer.** The AquaSense 2 Ultra is the most capable machine we cover,
and the most expensive: $2,299 from Beatbot's own store against a struck-through
$3,150. For that money it does five jobs — floor, walls, waterline, the water
surface, and a water-clarification trick no rival on this site attempts — behind a
camera that maps your pool and an industry-first 3-year full replacement warranty.

The honest framing is that this is two purchases in one: a flagship cleaner, and an
insurance policy shaped like a warranty. Whether the pair is worth flagship money
depends almost entirely on how big and how complicated your pool is. Also worth
knowing before the details: our own artwork gets one number and one name slightly
wrong, and both corrections are printed below.

---

## Who this is for

A large in-ground pool — Beatbot rates the Ultra's cleaning area at 3,875 sq ft —
with the full menu of problems: sunk debris, climbing walls, a tide-mark waterline,
leaves landing faster than they sink, and water that clouds in high summer. This is
the one machine in our catalogue that claims all five, and the only one that doses
clarifier into the water as it drives.

It is also the machine for the owner who wants the longest promise in the business
behind a four-figure spend. Beatbot's 3-year full replacement warranty — its claim,
and the first of its kind in the industry — is a longer and stronger term than
anything else we cover.

---

## Who should look elsewhere

- **Your pool is modest and flat-bottomed.** A WYBOT C1 does floor, walls and
  waterline for about a fifth of this price. Most pools need that, not this.
- **You want surface skimming without flagship money.** The Aiper Scuba X1 Pro Max
  vacuums and skims for hundreds less, with a published pool-length rating.
- **Your pool is above-ground.** Beatbot markets the Ultra for in-ground pools.
- **You distrust young premium brands.** The warranty is exceptional on paper, and
  the brand making the promise is only a few years old. Both facts are real.

---

## One machine, five jobs — including two nobody else does

Beatbot's own phrase is 5-in-1: floor, walls, waterline, water surface, and water
clarification. The first three are the standard flagship set. The last two are what
you are actually paying the premium for.

Surface mode turns the Ultra into a skimmer, collecting floating leaves before they
sink — in our catalogue only the Aiper Scuba X1 Pro Max also claims this, and the
dedicated Betta SE Plus does nothing else. Clarification is stranger and gets its
own section below. One nuance from our artwork: the panel labelled "platform
(multizone mode)" describes what Beatbot calls Adaptive Multi-Platform Cleaning —
the machine detecting and cleaning areas at different depths, tanning ledges and
raised shelves included. The capability is Beatbot's claim; the "multizone" label
is our artwork's phrasing, not Beatbot's.

---

## The surface hours: our artwork says 11, Beatbot says 10

Our battery creative prints "up to 11 hours for surface cleaning". Beatbot's own
page says up to 10 hours, and 10 is the number our records have held since 31 July.
No source we have read supports 11, so treat the artwork as generous by an hour and
plan on Beatbot's figure.

Ten hours is still the longest runtime claim in our catalogue by a distance, and
the reason is physics rather than magic: skimming the surface means floating, not
driving up walls, so the low-draw mode stretches the battery furthest. The figures
that matter for the wet work are lower and stated plainly by Beatbot: up to 5 hours
on floors, up to 5 hours on walls and waterline.

---

## Clarification: the robot that doses your water

The fifth job is the odd one out: the Ultra carries Beatbot's ClearWater
clarifying technology — described by Beatbot as 100% natural — and releases a
clarifying agent into the pool as it works, helping suspended particles clump so
filtration can catch them. Beatbot sells refill kits for it, which tells you it is
a consumable system, not a gimmick switch.

We have not tested what this does to a real pool's chemistry, and nothing here
replaces your own water testing. What we can say is that no other machine we cover
even attempts water treatment, and that a buyer who already pays for clarifier may
find part of this machine's premium quietly offsets a line in the chemical budget.

---

## HybridSense, CleverNav and the camera that maps your pool

The navigation stack is the deepest on the site. HybridSense is Beatbot's name for
the sensor fusion: a front-facing AI camera, infrared sensors, ultrasonic sensors
and dual time-of-flight sensors, used to detect debris, identify platforms and map
the pool. CleverNav is the path-planning layer that turns the map into routes. Our
navigation creative shows the practical result: a machine that knows an inlet, a
drain and a ladder are obstacles rather than floor.

Between this and the catalogue's other camera robot, the Aiper Scuba V3, the
difference is scope: the V3's camera recognises debris and adjusts its route; the
Ultra's stack claims full pool mapping with obstacle awareness, plus smart return —
it comes back to the surface at the poolside on one tap of the app. That is the
capability the price is arguing for.

## The battery: three runtimes and a lab-tested footnote

13,400 mAh — the largest battery in our catalogue, charged in 4.5 hours by
Beatbot's figures, on a wireless dock rather than a plug-in port. The three
runtimes are worth restating as a set: up to 10 hours skimming, up to 5 hours on
floors, up to 5 hours on walls and waterline.

The 3,875 sq ft area rating deserves its footnote read aloud: Beatbot's own note —
reproduced on our artwork — says maximum coverage is based on floor cleaning in one
full battery cycle, lab-tested. That is an honest asterisk, and it means the number
describes the floor job specifically. A pool near the ceiling that also wants
walls, waterline and a skim is a two-cycle pool, with 4.5 hours of charging in
between.

## The warranty that changes the arithmetic

Beatbot backs the Ultra with a 3-year full replacement warranty and calls itself
the first in the industry to offer one. Nothing else we cover comes close: the
Dolphin Nautilus CC Plus states one year, BuBlue states one, WYBOT states two, and
Aiper's product pages for the machines we review state no term at all.

On a $2,299 machine, that term is doing real work. Amortised bluntly, a Ultra that
lives its full warranty costs about $766 a year with a replacement promise
attached; a $700 machine with a one-year term and a second-summer failure costs
the same money with none. The price theatre note applies here as everywhere:
Beatbot's $3,150 "original price" is struck through on its own store, so judge the
machine as a $2,299 purchase, because that is what it costs.

## The name, the family and the listing we could not machine-read

Beatbot ships AquaSense, AquaSense Pro, AquaSense 2, AquaSense 2 Pro and AquaSense
2 Ultra — five machines whose names differ by a suffix and whose capabilities
differ by four figures. Our own hero artwork prints the name transposed as
"AquaSense Ultra 2"; the machine's correct name is AquaSense 2 Ultra, and the
family's naming is exactly why we bother correcting a two-word swap in print.

The Amazon listing our buy button points at was supplied and confirmed by the
owner on 3 August. Amazon has served its bot-mitigation page to every automated
read we have attempted since, so no machine check stands behind that listing's
identity yet, and our price checker keeps it on a daily watch until its first
clean read. Beatbot also sells direct from its own store at its own price. If you
buy on Amazon, check the listing's details table names the 2 Ultra — not the 2,
not the 2 Pro — before you pay.

## The verdict

The AquaSense 2 Ultra is the ceiling of this catalogue: the most jobs, the biggest
battery, the deepest sensor stack, and the only warranty that reads like a promise
rather than a formality. Nothing else we cover claims its five-job set, and the
3-year full replacement term genuinely changes the arithmetic on a four-figure
spend.

The verdict discipline is remembering who needs the ceiling. A big, complicated,
debris-heavy in-ground pool with a floating-leaf problem gets its money's worth
here and nowhere else. A mid-sized pool with walls and a waterline gets ninety
percent of the outcome from a WYBOT C1 or an Aiper Scuba S1 at a fraction of the
price — and if it is specifically the skimming you want, the Aiper Scuba X1 Pro
Max does both jobs for hundreds less. Buy the Ultra for the pool that uses all of
it.
