There is one autonomous snow blower you can buy in the United States, and this
is it. That is not a compliment. It is the reason this page has no ranking, no
comparison table and no "best of" list attached to it — there is nothing to
rank it against.

## The first thing to understand: it is not a snow blower

Yarbo sells a robot called the Core. The Core is a tracked, self-driving
platform with a battery, satellite positioning and cameras. It does nothing on
its own.

What it does depends on what you bolt to the front of it. There is a mowing
module that turns it into a robot lawn mower, a snow blower module, a leaf
blower and a trimmer. Yarbo calls this "1+N": one intelligent core, several
attachments, hot-swapped.

This matters because of what it does to the price, and the price is where
almost everybody meets this machine for the first time.

## Why you will see two prices, and which one is real

The Snow Blower Module costs $1,299. That is the auger, the impeller and the
chute — the part that throws snow. It is not a robot. Bolted to nothing, it
does nothing.

The product Yarbo calls the "Yarbo Snow Blower" costs $4,999. That is the Core,
the snow blower module, the Data Center, the battery, the docking station, a
snow track, a tow hitch, a charger and the installation kit. That is the thing
that clears your driveway.

Both prices were read on Yarbo's own site on 11 August 2026. Every third-party
review we found headlines the $4,999 figure and describes it as the
"standalone" snow blower, which is how a reader arrives expecting a $1,299
robot and finds a $1,299 accessory.

If you already own a Yarbo Core for mowing, the module is the number that
applies to you. If you do not, it is not.

Two more, for completeness: the Lawn Mower Pro and Snow Blower together are
$7,199, and the complete four-in-one is $7,999. Dealers discount. On the same
day, SmartDots listed the Core at $4,999 struck to $3,599 and CNY Green Team
listed a modular snow blower robot at $4,530.

## What owners say, and it is not what the specification says

This is the part most pages about this machine leave out.

Search "yarbo snow blower reviews" and the third result is a Reddit thread
titled "Extremely Disappointed with the Yarbo Snow Blower". Below it sit
Trustpilot, Yarbo's own support forum under the heading "reviews/opinions", and
two Facebook threads asking what the performance is like in practice.

Lowe's, which sells it, carries **zero** customer ratings.

So there is owner evidence. It is simply not where you would look for it, and
the loudest of it is unhappy. We are not going to summarise a Reddit thread as
though it were a test result — one angry owner is not a failure rate, and we
have not used this machine. What we will say is that anybody about to spend
five thousand dollars should read those threads before ordering, and that a
review which quoted only Yarbo's specification sheet would be contradicted by
the third result on its own name.

## The published specification

Everything here is Yarbo's own figure, read from yarbo.com on 11 August 2026.

A 24-inch clearing width and a 12-inch intake height, both at the useful end
for a domestic driveway. Throw distance is 6 to 40 feet, adjustable. Two-stage,
so an auger feeds an impeller rather than one paddle doing both jobs — the same
arrangement as a decent walk-behind machine.

The battery is 38.4 Ah and runs approximately 90 minutes. It clears up to 6,000
square feet per charge at one inch of snow, and recharges from 20 to 80 per
cent in 90 minutes.

It handles a 36 per cent slope, which is about 21 degrees. It is rated from
minus 13 Fahrenheit to plus 140, built in Q355 steel and carries an IPX5
ingress rating. Warranty is two years standard, up to five.

## Two numbers we are not carrying

**Runtime is 90 minutes, not four hours.** Several review sites state "up to 4
hours" on the same 38.4 Ah battery. Yarbo's own page says approximately 90
minutes. Where a maker and a review site disagree about the maker's product,
the maker wins, and the four-hour figure appears nowhere on this page.

That difference is not academic at 6,000 square feet a charge. A long driveway
in deep snow is a machine that docks, charges and comes back, rather than one
that finishes in a single run.

**Throw distance is a conflict Yarbo has not resolved.** Its own Snow Blower
Module page prints "up to 40 feet" and "6-40 Yards Throw Control" in the same
panel. One of those is wrong and both are Yarbo's. Forty feet and forty yards
are a hundred and twenty feet apart, which is the difference between clearing a
driveway and clearing your neighbour's car. We record the conflict and use the
conservative figure.

## Who should not buy this

Anybody who has not read the owner threads. That is the first filter and it
costs nothing.

Anybody expecting $1,299 to buy a robot. It buys an attachment.

Anybody with a driveway much over 6,000 square feet who wants it done in one
pass, because the machine will dock and charge partway through.

Anybody with a slope past 36 per cent, which is the published ceiling.

And anybody for whom a contractor is the honest comparison. A season of
driveway clearing costs a fraction of five thousand dollars in most of the
country, and the robot's argument is that you never go outside and never
arrange anything — not that it is cheaper. If you would happily pay somebody, it
probably is not for you.

## Who it might be for

Somebody in a snow belt with twenty or more clearing events a winter, a
driveway inside 6,000 square feet, a physical reason that shovelling or
walking behind a machine is a problem, and the money.

There is also a real case we do not want to undersell: if you are already
buying a Yarbo to mow, the snow blower module at $1,299 is a genuinely
different proposition from the $4,999 entry price, because the expensive part
is already in your garage. If that is the position you are in, our mower
shortlist is the place to work out whether the Core earns its keep the rest of
the year.

## What we could not establish

Amazon's own details table. This page shipped without a buy link because the
ASIN we held came from a search result title rather than from a listing anybody
had read — the same rule that kept a link off the roborock S8 MaxV Ultra, where
every result carrying the searched name turned out to be an accessory kit.

The listing has since been read directly. It serves brand YARBO and a title
naming this machine, printing two-stage, a 6 to 40 foot throw, a 12-inch intake
and a 24-inch clearing width — four figures that match Yarbo's own site. That
is enough for the buy button now on this page.

It is worth saying what still was not read: the details table itself, which is
where a Model Number field would sit. Every other buy button on this site rests
on one, so this one rests on a weaker foundation than the rest, and identity
leans partly on Lowe's item 8256113 publishing "Model #YARBO S1" and Best Buy
titling it "Black Yarbo S1". Our review methodology sets out what has to be
true before a buy button appears here.

One small thing the Amazon title settles, or nearly. It says **feet**, which
agrees with the conservative figure this page already uses. It is not Yarbo
correcting its own module page, so the conflict above stands as recorded.

We also have no independent measurement of throughput, noise, or how it behaves
in wet heavy snow as against powder. Yarbo publishes no decibel figure. One
publisher, PCWorld, has used one; almost nobody else has.

## About the M Series, and the name Snowbot

Yarbo is taking Kickstarter pre-orders for an M Series, described as the
essential modular yard robot. It is not sold, so it is not reviewed here. When
it ships, it gets its own page.

If you have been searching "Snowbot", you have been searching for this machine
under its old name. Hanyang Technology launched the Snowbot S1 in 2021 at
$1,999 and an S1 Pro was to follow at $2,999; the company now sells under the
Yarbo brand, and snowbot.com no longer resolves. The reviews you will find from
2021 and 2022 are about an earlier machine from the same company.

## The seasonal note, stated rather than hidden

Searches for this machine run about a hundred times higher in January than in
July. Prices move accordingly, dealer discounts appear and disappear, and stock
in December is not stock in August.

Every price on this page carries the date it was read. If you are reading this
in the middle of winter, check them again before you order.
