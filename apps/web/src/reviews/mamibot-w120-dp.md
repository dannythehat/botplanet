The Mamibot W120-DP is the third brand in this catalogue and the one whose
listing leads with a high-rise rating. Stated 3,200 Pa, four spray nozzles, a
60 ml tank.

## Who this is for

Someone who does not want to buy ECOVACS.

That sounds flippant and it is a real position. Six of the eleven machines in
this catalogue are ECOVACS, which is what the market looks like rather than a
preference of ours, and there are good reasons to want an alternative: a
different service network, a different app, or simply not wanting a category
decided by one company.

Mamibot has been making these longer than most of the newer entrants, the
W120-DP sits at a sensible mid-range price, and four nozzles is a proper spray
spread rather than a token one.

## Who should look elsewhere

Anyone comparing on stated suction. 3,200 Pa is below the WINBOT Mini's 7,500,
the W2 PRO's 5,300 ±500 and the W3 Omni's 10,000. Those numbers are not
measured the same way by different makers and should not be treated as directly
comparable — but within this catalogue it is the lower end of what is claimed.

Anyone with sloped glass. Only the HUTT S55 Pro offers that.

Anyone who needs the machine to work away from a socket. Only the WINBOT W2 PRO
Omni does.

## The high-rise claim

The listing is rated for high-rise use, and that is the thing worth thinking
about carefully rather than taking as a feature.

Every window robot on the market ships with a safety rope, and above the ground
floor you attach it every single time. What a high-rise rating means in
practice is a combination of the power-off hold, the strength of the seal
against glass and the machine's behaviour when it loses grip — and of those
three, the only one anybody publishes a number for is the hold time.

We do not have a published hold figure for this machine. ECOVACS states 30
minutes across its range. Until Mamibot publishes an equivalent, treat the
high-rise wording as a manufacturer's positioning rather than as a
specification you can compare.

The rope is the real answer at height, on any of these machines, at any price.

## Four nozzles and a 60 ml tank

The spread matters more than the volume. Streaking is what people actually
complain about in this category, and a narrow spray on a wide pane is one of
the causes — the cloth ends up dragging half-dry across glass that has already
started to dry.

Four nozzles puts this machine ahead of the W1 PRO's dual cross nozzle and the
W2S's three, and behind the six that the W2 PRO, W2 PRO Omni and W3 Omni carry.
The 60 ml tank matches everything in the ECOVACS range except the W3 Omni's 80.

## The verdict

A reasonable mid-price alternative to a range that dominates this catalogue,
with a decent spray spread and a sensible tank.

Buy it if you want a machine that is not an ECOVACS and your windows are
ordinary. Do not buy it for the high-rise wording specifically, because the
figure that would justify that wording is not published — and on a machine
working above your head, an unpublished number is not a reassurance.
