Four hundred and fifty dollars, 39,206 ratings, 4.6 stars. That combination
does not come up often in this category, and it is the reason the X10 Pro Omni
is the machine most people asking about robot vacuums should look at first.

It vacuums. It mops. The pads lift 12mm when it drives onto carpet, it empties
itself into the tower, and it looks at the floor with a camera rather than
finding your cable by driving into it.

## Who should not buy this one

If your floors are deep or shag pile from wall to wall, none of this matters
and you should stop here. Twelve millimetres of lift is enough for a fitted
carpet and a thin rug. It is not enough for a deep pile, and no robot vacuum
in this price bracket is, which is a fact about the category rather than about
eufy.

If you want the machine to work three floors on its own, it will not. Nothing
here climbs stairs. It maps more than one storey, so you carry it up and it
knows where it is — but you are the lift.

And if you have already been looking at eufy's newer C28, note that eufy's own
listing for that machine describes itself as "Upgraded from X10 Pro". The
company has moved on. At $499.99 the C28 is fifty dollars more with a roller
mop instead of dual pads, and it is a genuine alternative rather than a
successor that makes this one obsolete.

## What the 12 millimetres actually buys

A vacuum-mop with fixed pads in a house with carpet is a machine that drags a
wet cloth across your rug on a schedule. That is the single most common
disappointment in this category and it is entirely predictable from the
specification, which is why the BotMatch questionnaire asks about floors before
it asks about anything else.

Twelve millimetres is roughly the height of two stacked coins. It clears an
ordinary fitted carpet and it clears a thin rug. What to do with that: if the
rugs in your house are the kind you can slide a finger under and no more, this
works. If you can lose a finger in them, it does not, and the honest answer is
a vacuum-only machine plus a separate mop.

## The suction number, and why it is the wrong number

Eight thousand Pascals. The DEEBOT T90 PRO Omni in the same price band claims
30,000, and the Shark Matrix Plus at $279.99 does not publish a figure at all.

Nearly four times the suction for the same money would be a real argument if
suction above a modest threshold decided anything. It does not. What decides
whether a robot vacuum picks up dog hair is the brush design and whether the
hair wraps round it; what decides whether it cleans your house is whether it
gets stuck. Both are in the specification and neither is a Pascal figure.

This is why the BotMatch scoring for this category models capabilities and
price and does not model suction at all. A number that every listing leads
with and that separates nothing is a number a comparison site should refuse to
rank on.

## Two ASINs, one machine

B0CPFBBHP4 and B0DG5G9HQM are the same X10 Pro Omni in two colours, at the
same price, sharing one review pool of 39,206. It is a variation family, so a
link that does not force a variant lands you on whichever child Amazon feels
like serving.

That matters less here than it does elsewhere in this category — both children
are the same machine at the same money — but the habit is worth keeping,
because two listings down the shelf the same trick separates a $499 vacuum
from a $799 vacuum-and-mop.

## What we could not find out

eufy publishes a suction figure, a lift height and a capability list. It does
not publish, on the listing we read, a runtime, a dust bag capacity, a bin
volume, a noise figure or a set of dimensions. Those are recorded as not
disclosed rather than borrowed from a review site that borrowed them from
another review site.

The price and the rating here were read from the ASIN's own page on 10 August
2026. Amazon's search row for the same product reported 4.3 rather than 4.6 on
the same day; the listing read is the one printed, and both carry that date.

## Who should buy one

Somebody with hard floors and ordinary carpet who wants one machine to do both
and does not want to think about the bin. At $449.99 with a review pool that
size, this is the safest four hundred and fifty dollars in the category.

Anyone with a house full of deep pile should buy a vacuum-only robot and mop
by hand. Anyone whose real problem is that the floor is covered in things
should read the obstacle avoidance section of a machine twice this price
before deciding this one is enough.
