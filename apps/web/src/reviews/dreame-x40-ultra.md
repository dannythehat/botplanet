## Who should not buy this

Anyone whose floor is usually covered in things. Clutter is the third question
the BotMatch questionnaire asks, and it is the one that decides whether a
robot vacuum cleans your house or gets stuck under a chair with a sock in its
brush. Until dreame's own page can be read, this is not the machine to buy on
that basis.

Anyone with deep or shag pile, for the same reason as most of this category.
The mop lifts and detaches, which is good, but no high-pile claim is made
anywhere and clearance is not published.

## What the extending brush and the washboard are for

Two of dreame's ideas here address the two visible failures of a robot
vacuum-mop. The side brush extends outward so the machine reaches into a
corner rather than describing an arc around it. The washboard scrubs the mop
against a ridged surface with hot water rather than rinsing it in a tray,
which is the difference between a clean pad and a wet one.

What to do with that: if your complaint about a previous robot was the line of
dust along the skirting board and a mop that spread dirt around after ten
minutes, these two features are aimed exactly at you and they are the reason
to consider this over the cheaper eufy.

## Who should buy one

Somebody with hard floor and ordinary carpet, a clear floor, and a specific
irritation about corners or about mops that stop being clean. Six hundred
dollars buys a dock that refills itself, which none of the cheaper machines in
this catalogue do.

Anyone who needs a machine that copes with a cluttered floor should buy the
eufy X10 Pro Omni at $449.99 or the ECOVACS T90 PRO Omni at $599, both of
which document obstacle avoidance and cost the same or less.
