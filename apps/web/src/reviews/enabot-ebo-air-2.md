The EBO Air 2 is a 2K camera on tracked wheels that you drive around your house
from your phone. It costs $149, parks itself when the battery runs low, and
charges nothing per month.

It is also the model to buy out of the seven Enabot sells, and the reason is
that the more expensive ones do not give you a better robot.

## The original EBO Air is gone

If you searched for the EBO Air and landed here, that is why. Enabot no longer
lists it, and neither does Amazon.

The Air 2 replaced it: 2K instead of 1080p, and a face that displays custom
emoticons. Everything the old reviews said about driving a camera round your
house still applies, because that is the part that did not change.

## What $149 gets you

A camera you steer. That is the whole category and it is worth being plain
about how different it is from a camera on a shelf.

**2K, with night vision.** Enough to see a cat under a table at midnight.

**Tracked wheels.** It goes over rugs and thresholds rather than getting stuck
on them, which is the practical difference between a robot you use and one that
lives on its dock.

**Two-way talk.** You hear the room, the room hears you.

**It docks itself.** When the battery is low it drives back and charges. This
is the feature that decides whether you actually use one of these — a camera
you have to remember to plug in becomes a camera you stop using.

**No subscription.** Not on this model, not on any Enabot we read. In a
category where Furbo and Petcube both sell plans, that is the strongest thing
on this page.

## The range, and what the extra money actually buys

Enabot sells seven machines and the naming is unhelpful. These are the prices
on the day we read them.

The **SE** is $119 and drops to 1080p. The **ROLA Mini** is $139 at 2K. This
one, the **Air 2**, is $149. The **ROLA PetPal** is $179 and adds a treat
dispenser. The **EBO Mini** is $199. The **Air 2S** is $299 at 2.5K. The **Air
2 Plus** is $359, with 3K and a GPT and Gemini chatbot.

The full table is in the specifications above. Read down it and the pattern is
resolution and software, not mobility.
The $359 Air 2 Plus drives exactly as well as the $149 Air 2. You are paying
for a sharper picture and a chatbot.

Two exceptions worth the money if you want the specific thing: the **ROLA
PetPal** at $179 has a treat dispenser, which nothing else here does, and the
**SE** at $119 is smaller and gets under lower furniture.

## The limitation nobody advertises

**Stairs.** It has wheels. No pet camera robot sold in the US climbs stairs.

If your animal is upstairs and you are downstairs, this robot patrols whichever
floor you leave it on and nothing else. That is fine when you know it and it is
the most common disappointment when you do not. A house with bedrooms upstairs
needs either two of them or a static camera on the other floor.

## Who this is for

Somebody on one floor who wants to find their pet rather than watch a doorway.
A static camera shows you the room it points at; this shows you wherever the
animal actually went.

It also works as a house check. Enabot markets these as FamilyBots rather than
pet cameras for a reason — the same machine that finds a cat will show you
whether you left the hob on.

## Who should look elsewhere

**Anyone whose pet is upstairs.** See above.

**Anyone who wants treats.** The ROLA PetPal, $30 more.

**Anyone who wants the best picture.** The Air 2 Plus at 3K, though you are
paying $210 for resolution and a chatbot.

**Anyone who wants it to work by itself.** You drive these. They do not patrol
on a schedule, and a robot you have to open an app to use gets used less than
you imagine.

## What we cannot tell you

**Battery runtime.** Enabot publishes the capacity — 2,500 mAh — and no
duration, on the listing or its own store. Every rival in this space publishes
one.

**How well the auto-docking works in a cluttered room.** It is the feature that
matters most and no maker states a success rate.
