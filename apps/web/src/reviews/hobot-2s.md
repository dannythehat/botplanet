The HOBOT 2S sprays differently to everything else here. Instead of pumping
water through nozzles it uses an ultrasonic atomiser, and it carries two
replaceable water tanks rather than one fixed reservoir.

Whether that is better is not something anyone has measured. Whether it is more
convenient is easier to answer.

## Who this is for

Anyone who resents the refill.

Every other machine in this catalogue makes you take the robot off the glass,
carry it to a tap, fill a 60 or 80 ml reservoir and put it back. Two
replaceable tanks changes the shape of that: you fill both before you start and
swap one for the other without going anywhere.

On a house with a lot of glass that is a genuinely different afternoon, and it
is the clearest practical argument for this machine over a WINBOT.

## Who should look elsewhere

Anyone choosing on documented specifications. HOBOT publishes less than ECOVACS
does, and we have verified none of it — see the gaps below, which are the
longest part of this review for a reason.

Anyone with sloped glass. That is the HUTT S55 Pro alone.

Anyone who wants the machine to work with no socket nearby. That is the WINBOT
W2 PRO Omni alone.

## Ultrasonic spray, honestly assessed

A pump pushes droplets. An ultrasonic atomiser vibrates water into a mist fine
enough to sit on glass rather than run down it.

The theory is that a finer, more even film means less streaking, because
streaking is largely what happens when water dries unevenly before the cloth
reaches it. The theory is plausible. We have not tested it, no independent body
appears to have tested it, and HOBOT's own materials are the only source
claiming it works better.

What can be said without testing: fewer moving parts in the spray path is
usually more reliable than more, and a mist uses less water per pass, which is
part of why two small tanks are enough.

## AI route planning

HOBOT's term, not ours, and it means path planning rather than anything that
recognises what it is looking at.

Every machine here plans a route — the WINBOT range calls its version WIN-SLAM
and publishes a generation number for each model, which is more useful than a
marketing phrase because you can at least tell a 5.0 from a 3.0. HOBOT gives no
equivalent version, so there is nothing to compare against.

## What we cannot tell you

**No specification here is manufacturer-verified.** Everything above is
retailer-stated. The ECOVACS figures in this catalogue were read off
ecovacs.com on 5 August 2026; we found no equivalent published page for the 2S.

**No suction figure at all.** This is the one that matters most. Grip is what
keeps a robot on a window, every ECOVACS in the range publishes a number for
it, and we do not have one for this machine.

**No power-off hold.** ECOVACS states 30 minutes across its range. We cannot
tell you what happens to this machine when your power cuts, which above the
ground floor is the question.

**No warranty term and no weight.**

**Identity not verified.** The ASIN is owner-supplied from 5 August 2026 and no
checker has read the listing's Brand and Model Number fields.

## The verdict

Buy it for the two tanks, which is a real and specific advantage nothing else
here offers, if you have enough glass for refilling to be the annoying part.

Do not buy it expecting to compare it properly against a WINBOT, because you
cannot — the numbers that would let you are not published. That is not evidence
the machine is worse. It is a reason to prefer a documented one if documentation
matters to you, and a reason we would not put this at the top of a shortlist for
a first-time buyer.
