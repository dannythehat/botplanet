**The short answer.** The Betta SE Plus is not a pool cleaner. It is a skimmer, and
the difference is the whole review. It floats on the surface, runs on sunlight, and
picks up leaves, pollen, insects and pet hair before any of it sinks. It will never
touch your floor, your walls or your waterline, and Betta says so plainly.

Understood that way it is the most useful machine on this site, because it is the
only one not competing with the others. Bought by mistake, it is the most
disappointing.

---

## Who this is for

Anyone with trees. If your pool's real problem is what lands on it — leaves in
autumn, pollen in spring, bugs all summer — this is the machine that solves it, and
it solves it continuously rather than in a two-hour cycle you have to remember to
start.

It also suits a pool that already has a floor cleaner. Debris caught on the surface
never sinks, never stains, and never has to be vacuumed off the bottom later. Running
this alongside a floor robot is not doubling up; it is catching the same dirt earlier
and more cheaply.

And it suits a salt pool. Betta builds this one around twin motors it describes as
Salt Chlorine Tolerant, and the manual rates the machine for fresh or salt water up
to 5,000 ppm.

---

## Who should look elsewhere

- **You want the floor cleaned.** It does not go underwater. Betta's own listing
  says "designed for surface cleaning only". If the bottom of your pool is the
  problem, buy a floor robot.
- **You want the waterline scrubbed.** Also no. Nothing about this machine touches
  a wall.
- **Your pool is bigger than about 40 by 60 ft.** That is Betta's stated ceiling,
  roughly 2,400 sq ft.
- **You have no sun.** Solar is the point. There is an adapter for cloudy spells,
  but a permanently shaded pool wastes what you are paying for.
- **You want an app.** There is a wireless remote and no app that Betta mentions
  anywhere.

---

## What a skimmer actually does, and why it is not a lesser robot

Every other machine on this site waits for dirt to sink and then goes and gets it.
This one takes it off the top before it ever arrives.

That ordering matters more than it sounds. A leaf on the surface is loose and easy;
the same leaf on the floor is waterlogged, has started to stain, and now needs a
robot with brushes to shift it. Organic debris that sinks also feeds the chemistry
problem — more chlorine demand, more filter load, more of the work you did not want.

**So what to do with that.** If your complaint is "the pool always looks dirty on
top", this is the right machine and a floor cleaner will not fix it. If your
complaint is "there is silt on the bottom", this is the wrong machine and no amount
of surface skimming will fix that either. They are different jobs and the marketing
for both categories works hard to blur it.

---

## Runtime, and the number that sounds like a typo

Thirty hours, continuous, on a single charge. That is Betta's figure and it is not a
misprint — it is a small motor moving a floating hull, not a heavy machine dragging
itself up a wall.

Charging is 5 to 6 hours in direct sunlight, or 3.5 hours on the supplied adapter.
In practice, in a sunny month, the machine tops itself up while it works and the
runtime figure stops being something you think about at all. That is the actual
selling point, and it is why "24/7" appears in Betta's own product title rather than
in ours.

The adapter is the honest bit. Betta includes it because solar alone does not survive
a bad week, and a manufacturer that ships a backup for its headline feature is being
more realistic than one that does not.

---

## Filtration, and the one number Betta does publish

A 200 micron fine-mesh basket with a handle on top.

That is a genuinely useful figure, and worth pausing on: two of the three pool robots
we have reviewed so far publish no micron rating at all. Betta publishes one. Two
hundred microns is coarse compared with a pressure filter, which is the right choice
here — this basket is catching leaves and insects, not clarifying water, and a finer
mesh would clog in an afternoon under a tree.

What Betta does not publish is the basket's volume. It is described as large and
never measured, so how often you empty it in leaf-fall is something you will find out
by owning one.

---

## Navigation, and what "ultrasonic radar" is doing here

Ultrasonic radar for obstacle detection, per Betta's own listing, plus a UV-resistant
coating on the shell.

Both are more meaningful on a floating machine than they sound. A skimmer that cannot
detect an edge spends its day nudging the same wall; one that cannot handle sunlight
degrades in the one place it is designed to sit. A robot that lives on the surface in
full sun is a harder material problem than one that spends its life underwater, and
Betta has at least named the answer.

There is a shallow-water safeguard as well, and a stated minimum of 3.5 inches of
water. That is a real number for anyone with a shallow tanning ledge.

---

## What is in the box, and the warranty

A wireless remote is named. A full box-contents list is not published, which is
unusual and mildly annoying.

The warranty is one year, and unlike the last two machines we reviewed, that figure
is stated twice — on the product page and in the manual — so it is not in doubt.

**No docking station is included.** Some of our own artwork on this page shows the
machine sitting on one. Betta's listing describes no dock, no charging base and no
cradle: the robot charges by floating in the sun, or on the adapter. If you are
looking at a picture on this page and wondering where to buy the dock, there is not
one to buy.

## What we cannot tell you

Written from published sources and no pool. Betta publishes less than most:

- **Weight.** Not published.
- **Dimensions.** Not published.
- **Battery capacity.** Runtime and charge time are given; the cell is not
  described.
- **Basket volume.** "Large", never measured.
- **How much debris it actually catches in a day.** No source gives a figure and we
  have not run one.
- **Whether there is an app.** Betta names a remote and never mentions an app either
  way, so "it has no app" is our inference rather than their statement.
- **Depth range beyond the 3.5 inch minimum.** A shallow-water safeguard is
  described; no maximum is given, though for a floating machine that matters less.

---

## The verdict

Buy it for what it is. As a surface skimmer for a pool under trees it is the most
obviously worthwhile machine we have looked at, because it is cheap relative to the
floor robots, it runs on sunlight, and it removes a problem the others only clean up
after.

Do not buy it as your only robot unless the only thing that bothers you is the
surface. It will not clean your floor and it does not claim to — Betta is unusually
straight about that, and we would rather you heard it from us before the box arrives
than after.

The most honest way to think about it: this is not a cheaper alternative to a pool
cleaner. It is the other half of one.
