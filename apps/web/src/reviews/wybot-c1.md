**The short answer.** The WYBOT C1 is the value argument of our cordless class: floor,
wall and waterline cleaning for pools up to 1,615 sq ft, five modes, a real app with a
weekly cycle timer, a 2-year warranty — for about $500 from WYBOT's own store, and
often less on Amazon. That is Seagull SE money doubled, for a machine that does
roughly what the $850 class does.

Two things to know before the details. Our own catalogue under-sold this machine
until today — WYBOT rates it for above-ground pools too, and our database said
in-ground only. And its Amazon listing history is the messiest we cover: the listing
our records started with is dead, and the one our buy button now points at is
confirmed by us, not machine-read. Both get their own sections.

---

## Who this is for

A small-to-mid pool — above-ground or in-ground, WYBOT rates it for both — where the
budget stops well short of four figures but the job list does not: walls climbed,
waterline scrubbed, steps and slopes included, on a battery with no cable to snag.

It is also the right machine for the scheduling-minded owner on a budget. The C1's
cycle timer books up to four cleans a week from one charge, which is a feature the
budget class mostly does not have: the Seagull SE has a power button, and the machines
with real schedules mostly cost hundreds more.

---

## Who should look elsewhere

- **Your pool is bigger than 1,615 sq ft.** The Aiper Scuba X1 Pro Max carries
  roughly double the area rating.
- **You want the finest filter going.** The C1's stated fineness is 180 microns; the
  Aiper Scuba S1 puts a 3-micron layer behind that for a bit more money.
- **You want the surface skimmed.** Floor, walls, waterline — nothing floating. That
  is a Betta SE Plus, or the X1 Pro Max doing both jobs.
- **You want a camera.** The C1 plans paths; it does not see. The Aiper Scuba V3
  watches where it is going.

## The correction: our catalogue said in-ground only, WYBOT says both

WYBOT's own comparison table lists the C1's pool classification as "Above-Ground &
In-Ground", for all pool shapes. Our verification record has held those words since

until the day this review was written.

That mattered more than it sounds. An above-ground pool owner asking BotMatch for a
budget cordless machine is exactly the buyer this product exists for — and until

the same money. Same class of mistake we fixed on the Aiper Scuba S1's waterline and
the Dolphin Nautilus's, in both directions. The catalogue now says what WYBOT says,
and every claim on this page traces to a manufacturer sentence.

## What about $500 actually buys

WYBOT's own store lists the C1 at $499.99 against a struck-through $699.99, with 937
reviews under it. Amazon pricing moves around; our checker reads the listing on its
own schedule and the price on this page follows it.

The value case is simple to state. Floor-wall-waterline coverage, app scheduling and
a stated 2-year warranty is the $850-class feature list, and the C1's spec sheet
gives up mainly two things against those machines: filter fineness and runtime
headroom. If your pool fits inside 1,615 sq ft, the cheaper machine covers it, and a
2-year stated term is longer than several pricier brands manage — Aiper's own S1
page states no term at all.

## Suction: 3,038 GPH, and the numbers on our artwork

WYBOT publishes 3,038 GPH of suction through a 180-micron ultra-fine filter, with
dual PVC brushes doing the scrubbing, from what it calls a triple-motor system. As
always, GPH is the maker's pump rating, not an independent measurement.

Our artwork needs three footnotes here, and honesty says we print them. The
11.5 m³/h flow figure on our creative is the same 3,038 GPH in metric clothing — a
unit conversion, not a second source. The "65W" motor figure appears nowhere in
WYBOT's published material that we have read; treat it as unsourced. And the
"four-wheel drive" panel describes a machine that WYBOT pictures running on treads —
WYBOT itself does not describe the drivetrain either way. The suction, filter and
brush figures are WYBOT's own; those three garnishes are not.

## Five modes, six paths, and the S-path brain

Full, floor, wall, wall-then-floor and eco floor — five modes, against six cleaning
paths, driven by WYBOT's S-path and N-path planning. No camera and no sonar: this is
the planned-path middle class, the same tier as the Aiper Scuba S1's WavePath, a
step above the Seagull SE's random walk and a step below the Scuba V3's camera.

The mode list earns its keep in one specific way: wall-then-floor is a sequencing
choice, not just a zone choice, and eco floor is what makes the cycle timer's
multi-day arithmetic work. Modes, remote control and OTA updates all live in the
WYBOT app.

---

## The cycle timer: four cleans from one charge

The C1's cycle timer is the feature our third creative shows, and it is the right
one to show: WYBOT's app can split a single charge into up to four scheduled cleans
— once at full length, twice at half, three or four times in shorter sessions across
the week. The app's own description says it divides the default runtime evenly and
warns that a low battery stops a cycle early.

One arithmetic note, because both numbers are WYBOT's. The splits in our artwork —
and in WYBOT's app copy — sum to 120 minutes, while the product page's headline
runtime says up to 150. The likeliest reading is that the timer divides a standard
eco cycle rather than the maximum, but WYBOT does not say so, and we will not
pretend it does. Either number covers a 1,615 sq ft pool's cleaning week; just do
not plan the schedule on the bigger one.

## Charging, battery and retrieval

Three hours to charge, from WYBOT's own comparison table — the figure our charging
creative carries. The 4,600 mAh battery capacity printed beside it is our research;
WYBOT's page does not state a capacity, and we would rather tell you that than let
the artwork imply a source that does not exist.

At the end of a cycle the C1 self-parks at the waterline for retrieval — WYBOT's
claim, and the sane design for a 17.6 lb machine in a pool you would rather not
swim. Wet lifting is the whole handling story: this is one of the lighter
wall-climbers we cover, and one owner review on WYBOT's page describes a 60-minute
clean finishing with 58% battery left, which is the kind of margin the spec sheet
cannot promise but is nice to hear about.

Our records started with an Amazon listing for this machine that no longer exists —

The listing our buy button points at now was confirmed by us on 3 August against

that human confirmation rather than machine-read fields, and our price checker
reads it on a daily watch until its first clean pass. WYBOT sells C1, C1 Pro and
C1 Max with no published model number, so the honest advice stands: check the
listing says C1 — alone, no suffix — before you pay.

## What we cannot tell you

Written from published sources and no pool:

- **Battery capacity.** Our artwork's 4,600 mAh is research; WYBOT states no figure.
- **Motor wattage.** The 65W on our creative has no WYBOT source we have read.
- **The drivetrain.** Treads in every WYBOT photo; no drivetrain description in
  WYBOT's text.
- **Which runtime governs the timer.** The cycle splits sum to 120 minutes; the
  headline says up to 150. Both are WYBOT's numbers.
- **A model number.** WYBOT publishes none for the C1, which is exactly why its
  Amazon listing history needed a section.
- **Real-world battery ageing.** A 2-year warranty is stated; lithium chemistry
  over five summers is nobody's spec sheet.

## The verdict

The WYBOT C1 is the best budget case in our catalogue for a pool with walls worth
climbing: it does the floor-wall-waterline job, adds a genuinely useful weekly cycle
timer, states a 2-year warranty that pricier brands will not match, and costs about
$500. The spec sheet's compromises — a 180-micron filter with no ultra-fine layer,
mid-pack runtime — are the honest kind you can see before buying.

What needs eyes-open acceptance is everything around the machine rather than in it:
a young brand, an Amazon listing that has to be identity-checked by a person, and
three numbers on our own artwork that WYBOT does not publish. If those give you
pause, the Aiper Scuba S1 costs a little more and documents a little better. If they
do not, this is the most machine per dollar on the site.
