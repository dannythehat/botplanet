## Who this page is for

Somebody who watched an old Cozmo video, searched the name, found a $399.99
listing on what looks like the official store, and wants to know whether to
press the button.

The answer is no, and the reason has nothing to do with the robot. Cozmo was a
good machine. Anki built it in 2016, sold it well, and went out of business in
2019 anyway. What you are being offered now is a relaunch by the company that
bought the wreckage, and that company is the problem.

## What the maker's own store says today

We opened Digital Dream Labs' shop on 8 August 2026 and read the Cozmo listing.

- **Price:** $399.99
- **State at the top of the page:** COMING SOON
- **The button:** Sold Out
- **Ship date offered:** none

That combination has been the steady state rather than a bad day. A price, no
stock, no date, and a store that stays open.

## The Attorney General's case

On 18 September 2024 the Pennsylvania Office of the Attorney General sued
Digital Dream Labs, LLC and its chief executive, Harold Jacob Hanchar.

The allegation, as reported when the suit was filed: roughly **14,000 orders**
placed between **November 2020 and January 2024**, most of them never fulfilled.
Customers paid between **$147 and $655** per robot. Total sales of more than
**$4 million**. The products named in the case are Vector 2.0, **Cozmo 2.0** and
the Butter Robot.

The Attorney General put it in one sentence. "Consumers paid significant costs
for these products, never received them, and were left out in the cold when they
asked for updates or refunds."

The suit asks for restitution, civil penalties, and an order stopping the
company taking prepayment from Pennsylvania consumers at all.

Set that beside the shop page. The company is under suit for taking money for
robots it did not send, and the listing invites you to be next in the queue.

## The Better Business Bureau file

Digital Dream Labs is **not accredited** and carries an **F** rating. The BBB
records **failure to respond to 71 complaints** and four complaints closed
unresolved, and it carries an alert about the Attorney General's suit on the
company's profile.

An F is the bottom of that scale. It is not a review score and it is not an
opinion about the product; it is a record of a company not answering people who
complained.

## Vector, and what happens to a cloud robot

Cozmo's stablemate is Vector, and Vector is the demonstration of the risk you
are being asked to take.

Vector's personality was never in the robot. Speech recognition and the
conversational part ran on Anki's cloud, and when that infrastructure stopped
being maintained the robots stopped answering. We tried the four old Anki
endpoints — token, chipper, jdocs and accounts — on 8 August 2026 and got no
response from any of them. We cannot tell you from that test alone whether the
services are switched off, broken, or simply unreachable from where we sit; what
we can tell you is that nothing answered.

There is a rescue, and it is not the manufacturer's. **wire-pod** is free,
open-source community software that replaces the voice server, runs on your own
PC or a Raspberry Pi, and works with ordinary production Vectors. It exists
because owners built it. That is a good outcome and a bad advertisement: the
functioning version of this ecosystem is the one maintained by volunteers.

## The receivership, and what we could not confirm

The community record says a judge in the Court of Common Pleas of Allegheny
County appointed a receiver over Digital Dream Labs' and Anki's digital assets
on **9 July 2026** — case GD-25-013191, with the receiver taking control of the
repositories, hosting accounts, domains, billing system and customer data.

**We could not confirm that at the court.** It is reported by the Vector owner
community, consistently and in detail, and we have not been able to open the
Allegheny County docket to read the order ourselves. So we are printing it as
what it is: a serious, specific, widely-reported claim that we have not verified
at source. Weigh it accordingly. What does not depend on it is everything above,
which is on the Attorney General's docket and the BBB's file.

## What we cannot tell you

**Whether a restock is coming.** No ship date is published and we will not guess
at one.

**Whether the lawsuit has been decided.** We found no judgment, settlement or
consent decree on the public record when we looked on 8 August 2026, which is
not the same as there being none.

**Whether a Cozmo bought today would keep working.** It depends on servers owned
by a company in litigation over undelivered orders, and nobody outside it can
answer that.

**How well the robot performs.** We have not tested one, and that gap is not the
reason for the verdict. A machine you cannot reliably receive does not need a
performance review.

## Buy one of these instead

Three coding robots that ship, from companies that are trading normally. Each
one is reviewed here with its own rule-out.

- **Sphero BOLT** — the one that grows with a child, from block coding into
  JavaScript. The closest thing to Cozmo's actual job, which was making
  programming feel like play.
- **Makeblock mBot** — build it first, then code it. The cheapest real entry
  point here and the one for a child who wants to see the wiring.
- **Ozobot Evo** — screen-free to start, with a marker pen and a line on paper,
  then app-based later. The right answer for a younger child.

If what you actually wanted was the character rather than the coding, the
companion robots category is the honest redirect — Vector 2.0 has its own review
here, with the same server problem stated plainly.

## The verdict

Cozmo was a good robot and this is not a review of a robot. It is a review of
whether you can buy one. On 8 August 2026 a state Attorney General is suing the
seller over roughly 14,000 orders that were paid for and never delivered. The
store has no stock and no date. The Better Business Bureau records 71 complaints
the company did not answer.

Buy a Sphero BOLT. Buy an mBot. Buy an Ozobot Evo. If you want a Cozmo, buy a
second-hand original from a person, understand that its cloud features depend on
infrastructure now in dispute, and send no money to Digital Dream Labs.
