The cheapest WINBOT grips harder than the two above it. ECOVACS publishes 7,500
Pa maximum for the Mini against 5,500 for the WINBOT W2 PRO Omni and 5,300 for
the WINBOT W2 PRO. Only the W3 Omni beats it.

That is the single most useful fact in this category, and it tells you what the
money in an ECOVACS actually buys.

## Who this is for

Small panes, and anyone who has to put the thing away.

At 215 × 215 × 55 mm it is substantially smaller than the 271 mm square W2
machines, and at 1.3 kg ±0.1 it is the lightest robot here. On a cottage
window, a bathroom pane or a Georgian sash with glazing bars, the bigger
machines simply do not fit the pane. This one does.

It is also the only machine in the catalogue a renter would sensibly buy. It
lives in a drawer.

## Who should look elsewhere

Anyone with large glass. Three cleaning modes, four spray nozzles and WIN-SLAM
3.0 navigation are entry-level, and a big pane is where navigation quality
shows: the machine has further to plan and more to get wrong.

Anyone who wants scheduling or a serious app. The W2 PRO has seven modes to
this one's three, and WIN-SLAM 4.0.

## Suction is not what you are paying for

Here is the range as ECOVACS publishes it, maximum suction first:

- W3 Omni — 10,000 Pa
- **Mini — 7,500 Pa**
- W2 PRO Omni — 5,500 Pa ±500
- W2 PRO — 5,300 Pa ±500
- W1 PRO — 2,800 Pa

The cheapest machine is second strongest. Moving suction tells the same story:
3,200 Pa on the Mini against 2,800 on both W2 machines, and only the W3 Omni's
3,300 Pa ±100 ahead of it.

So what does the extra money buy? Nozzle count, cleaning modes, navigation
generation and — on the Omni — a battery station. Those are real things. Grip
is not one of them, and anyone choosing on the suction number alone would buy
this machine.

## Frameless glass, at the bottom of the range

ECOVACS rates the Mini for frameless glass, and it rates every other WINBOT for
it too. The category's supposed hard exclusion is not an ECOVACS exclusion at
all.

That matters for what you compare on. If your windows are unframed, the
question is not which machines can — within this range they all claim it — but
how well each one detects an edge it cannot feel. We do not have an independent
answer to that and neither does anyone else we can find.

## Safety

Nine protection stages and a stated 30-minute power-off hold, which is the same
hold ECOVACS publishes for the W2 PRO, the W2 PRO Omni and the W3 Omni.

The cheapest machine in the range gets the same answer to the question people
actually worry about. That is worth saying plainly, because the assumption runs
the other way.

Attach the safety rope above the ground floor. Every time, to something solid.

## What we cannot tell you

**Whether the listing is this machine.** The ASIN was captured from an Amazon
search on 5 August 2026, not machine-read for Brand and Model Number.

**The warranty.** ECOVACS publishes no warranty term on any WINBOT page we
read — not for this model and not for the flagship either. That is a gap across
the whole range rather than a slight against the Mini.

**How it handles a big pane.** We have not tested it. The concern above is
reasoning from three modes and an older navigation generation, not an observed
failure.

## The verdict

Buy it for small windows, and buy it without feeling short-changed on grip.

The honest summary of this range is that the Mini's suction embarrasses two
machines costing several times as much, and what you give up going down to it
is modes, nozzles and navigation rather than the ability to hold glass. If your
panes are small, that is a trade worth making. If they are large, the WINBOT W2
PRO is the machine and the reason is planning, not power.
