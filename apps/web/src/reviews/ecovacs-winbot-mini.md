That is more maximum suction than the W2 PRO Omni's 5,500 and the W2 PRO's

## Who this is for

Small panes. Glazing bars, a bathroom window, a cottage casement.

215 × 215 × 55 mm against 271 mm square for the W2 machines. That difference
sounds academic until you have a window divided into six panes and a robot that
does not fit any of them. Size is the reason to buy this, and the suction figure
is a bonus rather than the argument.

Also the household with nowhere to keep it. It goes in a drawer, which no other
machine here does.

## Who should look elsewhere

**Anyone with large glass.** Three cleaning modes against seven on the W2 PRO,
and WIN-SLAM 3.0 navigation — two generations behind the W2 machines and three
behind the W3 Omni. On a big pane the limitation is the route it takes, not the
grip it has.

**Anyone reading the suction number as a ranking.** It is not one. See below.

**Anyone who wants a station.** This is a corded machine and the socket has to
be near the window.

## The suction figure, honestly

7,500 Pa maximum, and it genuinely is higher than machines costing three times
as much.

Here is what that does not mean. A smaller, lighter robot needs less force to
hold the same glass, so a high figure on a small machine and a lower figure on
a large one can describe the same real margin. ECOVACS does not publish a
moving figure for the Mini — the number that actually decides whether a robot
holds while it travels — and it does publish one for the WINBOT W2 PRO Omni and
the W3 Omni.

So: a strong grip for its size, on the evidence available, and not proof that
it out-performs the flagships. Anyone selling you this machine on that
comparison is reading one number out of two.

## The safety figure it does publish

30 minutes of power-off hold, the same as ECOVACS prints for its flagships.

That is worth more than it looks. The cheapest ECOVACS in the range, the W1 PRO
at a similar price, claims a hold and gives no duration at all. This one gets
the flagship number printed. On the machine most likely to be used on an
upstairs window by somebody who bought it because it was cheap, that is the
specification that matters.

## What we cannot tell you

**Moving suction.** Published for the Omni machines, absent here, and it is the
figure that would settle the comparison above.

**How WIN-SLAM 3.0 behaves on a small pane.** Two generations old, but the
route problem it solves is much easier on a window this size. We have not
tested it and cannot tell you whether the older generation matters here.

**How it performs.** Not tested. The figures are ECOVACS's own.

## The verdict

Buy it because your panes are small, and treat the suction figure as a pleasant
surprise rather than the reason.

It is the right machine for a specific and common window that every other robot
in this catalogue is too big for, and at $150 it is the cheapest honest answer
to that problem. Buy it for large glass and you will spend the money twice.
