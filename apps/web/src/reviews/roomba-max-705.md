There are two machines called Roomba Max 705 and they are three hundred
dollars apart. This page is about the cheaper one, which does not mop.

B0DWG3C3ZF is the Roomba Max 705 Robot Vacuum with AutoEmpty Dock, $499, rated
4.3 from 741 ratings. B0DWG15XKQ is the Roomba Max 705 Combo Robot Vacuum &
Mop with AutoWash Dock, $799, rated 3.6 from 4,800 ratings. Same name, one
extra word, different machine, different dock, and a rating gap worth reading
twice.

## The word is "Combo"

If you want a mop, you want the Combo and this is the wrong page. If you do
not, the vacuum is three hundred dollars cheaper and rated seven tenths of a
star higher by a review pool six times the size.

That rating gap is the interesting part. It is not proof that the mopping
version is worse: a machine with more parts has more to go wrong, and mopping
robots attract complaints that vacuums never do. But 3.6 stars from 4,800
people is a signal, and iRobot's own vacuum-only model outscoring it is a
comparison the listing will not draw for you.

## What this one has

LiDAR navigation, obstacle and anti-fall detection, dual rubber anti-tangle
brushes, and a dock that empties the bin.

Rubber brushes rather than bristles are the part to notice. Pet hair wraps
round a bristle brush and has to be cut off with scissors; a pair of
counter-rotating rubber rollers is the design that stops it, and it is the
reason iRobot's machines have historically done well in houses with animals. Anti-fall
detection means it will find the top of a staircase and stop, which matters if
you leave it running on an upper floor.

## The name iRobot did not use

Nothing on this listing says how much suction it has. iRobot describes it as
"powerful" and prints no figure at all, in a category where the competition
prints 8,000, 12,000, 20,000 and 30,000 Pascals on the front of the box.

We would rather have the number. But a machine with no published Pascal figure
and a documented brush design is a better-specified machine than one with
30,000 Pa and no statement about hair, and the buying decision in this
category rests far more on the second than the first.

## What we could not find out

Suction, runtime, bin capacity, noise, dimensions and weight are not published
on the listing and were not readable elsewhere, and all are recorded as not
disclosed.

There is a third machine one step up, the Roomba 705X at $1,399.99 with 13,200
ratings at 4.3, described as a vacuum and mop combo with "175x more suction".
What the 175 is multiplying we cannot establish from any page we read, and a
multiplier with no baseline is not a specification.

## Who should buy one

Somebody with a shedding animal, hard floor and carpet, and no interest in a
robot that mops. Five hundred dollars buys rubber anti-tangle brushes, a dock
that empties itself and iRobot's navigation, and the anti-tangle brushes are
the reason to choose it over the Shark PowerDetect at fifty dollars more.

Anyone who wants mopping should look at the eufy X10 Pro Omni at $449.99
before they look at the $799 Combo — it is cheaper than both, it mops, and it
lifts the pads onto carpet. Anyone reaching for the Combo on the strength of
this review should know it is a different product with a lower rating.
