Thirty-three thousand people a month search for the eufy S1 Pro. It is the
biggest single product term in the robot vacuum category and one of the biggest
on this site.

Here is what we found when we went to look at it. The Amazon search row for
this machine returns no price at all. Its rating is 3.2 stars from 776
ratings, which is the lowest of the eleven vacuums in our catalogue by a
distance — nothing else is below 4.3. And eufy's own current line has moved
past it.

## Read the price twice

Searching Amazon for this product returns the listing with the price field
empty. Reading the ASIN's own page returns $919.58.

A missing price in a search row normally means there is no buy-box winner:
Amazon is not featuring a seller, and what you see on the product page may be
a third-party offer rather than the one the brand intends. That is not proof
of anything sinister. It is a reason to look at who is selling before you
click, and it is why this page carries no buy button.

## eufy has moved on and says so

The Omni S2 is eufy's current flagship at $1,399.99, rated 4.4 from about
3,000 ratings. Below it sits the C28 at $499.99, whose own Amazon title
describes it as "Upgraded from X10 Pro" and which uses the same HydroJet roller
mop idea this machine introduced.

So the S1 Pro sits at $919.58 between a $1,399.99 machine rated a full point
higher and a $499.99 machine using its own headline technology for half the
money. That is an awkward place for a product to be, and the searches have not
caught up with it.

## The roller mop, which is the good idea here

The S1 Pro's mop is a roller that washes itself against a scraper while it
runs, rather than two spinning pads that get progressively dirtier until the
robot goes home to rinse them. On hard floor that is a real improvement and it
is why the design has propagated across eufy's range.

What the listing does not claim is mop lifting. There is no lift figure and no
carpet claim anywhere in the title, which is why this machine is recorded in
our catalogue as hard floors only and why the BotMatch questionnaire will not
offer it to anybody who says they have carpet.

That may be an under-claim rather than a limitation. We could not check.

## What we could not find out

eufy's own product page for this machine returned a 404 to a direct fetch on
10 August 2026, so everything above comes from the Amazon listing and from
eufy's listings for the machines around it. That is thinner evidence than we
hold for the roborock and ECOVACS machines in the same catalogue, where the
maker's page read cleanly, and it is recorded as thinner rather than dressed up.

Not disclosed anywhere we could read: runtime, dust bag capacity, water tank
volume, noise, dimensions, or whether the roller lifts on carpet. The
"Eco-Clean Ozone" the title advertises is not explained on the listing either.

## Who should buy one

Somebody with hard floors throughout, who wants a self-washing roller mop, and
who has satisfied themselves about the seller and the price on the day they
buy. That is a narrower recommendation than a 33,100-a-month search term
suggests, and the narrowness is the finding.

Everybody else has two better answers from the same brand. The X10 Pro Omni at
$449.99 does more of what most houses need — it lifts its pads onto carpet —
and has 39,206 ratings at 4.6 stars behind it. The C28 at $499.99 carries the
roller mop into the current generation. Neither costs nine hundred dollars.
