Loona is the closest thing to an actual robot pet that anybody sells. It sees
you, recognises your face and your hand gestures, follows you across a room,
talks back, chases a laser pen and brings a ball.

It costs $499 and runs for ninety minutes.

Recharging takes two and a half hours, which means a Loona spends more of its
day on the dock than off it. That is the fact to settle before any of the rest
of this matters.

## The battery, and what ninety minutes actually feels like

1,350 mAh. Up to 1.5 hours of continuous play. About 2.5 hours to charge.

Those are KEYi's own numbers, from the listing's questions and answers.

It drives itself back to the dock when it runs low, and you can set different
return routes for different rooms. KEYi's own top feature bullet is about
having improved this — an "Automatic Recharge 2.0" shipped in the V28 update,
introduced with the words "in response to Loona's charging problem". A maker
leading its feature list with a fix is being unusually candid, and it also
tells you the auto-return did not always work.

What this shapes is how you own one. Loona is a thing you get out, play with
and put back, in the way you would with a games console rather than a cat.
Anyone imagining a companion that is simply around all day is imagining a
different product, and at $499 that is an expensive misunderstanding.

## What $499 actually buys

More capability than anything else in this category, and it is not close.

**It sees.** An HD RGB camera drives face recognition, gesture recognition and
the following behaviour. Point at something and it responds. It needs a
well-lit room to do any of this, which KEYi states plainly.

**It talks.** Amazon Lex handles the voice commands and ChatGPT-4o handles the
conversation. Background noise degrades it and clear speech helps, again per
KEYi.

**It plays.** Chases a laser pen, fetches a ball, and carries a set of app
games and quizzes.

**It watches the house.** The camera and speaker double as a home monitor you
can check from the app while you are out.

That last one is worth pausing on, because it makes Loona the only product in
this category that also does the pet-camera job. If you were weighing a
companion robot against a pet camera and could not decide, this is the machine
that does not force the choice — though a dedicated pet camera does that half
better and costs a fraction of this.

## No subscription, with a caveat about how we know

Nothing on the listing mentions a subscription, and the ChatGPT and Lex
features are described as included.

The caveat: KEYi's own website is built in JavaScript and we could not
machine-read a terms or pricing page from it. So this is **none published**
rather than **none exists**. Given that "does loona robot require a
subscription" is one of the most searched questions about this product, it is
worth checking KEYi's own site before ordering rather than taking a review's
word for it — including ours.

## Why Loona costs what it costs

Because it has a camera, depth-aware navigation, gesture recognition, a
self-return dock and a conversational layer, and nothing else in this category
carries all five.

Whether that is worth three and a half Eiliks is a different question. The
honest framing: Loona is the only one that behaves like an animal rather than
like a toy with a face, and you are paying for the behaviours, the same way you
pay for Moflin's fur. The difference is that Moflin costs $429 and never
promises to do anything.

## The accessory problem, and one warning about buying

Search Amazon for Loona and the accessories rank above the robot. Wheel covers,
clothes, screen protectors, replacement balls — several of these outrank the
$499 machine itself in the results.

**This caught us out during research.** The first automated pass at finding
this product returned "Play Ball for Loona Pet Robot" as the Loona, because an
accessory carries every word of the product it attaches to. Check that what you
are adding to a basket costs several hundred dollars and says "Includes
Charging Dock", not twelve dollars and "compatible with".

## Who this is for

Someone who wants the real thing and knows what they are getting. Loona is the
most alive-seeming robot you can buy in the US, and the ninety-minute limit is
a shape to work with rather than a fault to be disappointed by.

It suits a household with children, and KEYi's own reviewers keep saying so —
the app is simple enough for a five-year-old to drive. It suits anybody who
wants a home camera and a companion and would rather buy one thing.

## Who should look elsewhere

**Anyone who wants constant presence.** Ninety minutes on, two and a half
hours off.

**Anyone on a budget.** Eilik is $139.99 and has a fraction of the capability
but none of the charging problem, because you never expected it to roam. If
what you actually want is a robot dog rather than a robot pet, our guide to
robot dog toys sorts the cheap toys from the AI pets and the programmable
quadrupeds.

**Anyone whose main goal is watching a pet.** The pet-camera robots do that
job properly and cost far less. Loona monitoring your home is a bonus on a
companion robot, not a replacement for a camera.

**Anyone with a dark room.** The camera does most of the work and KEYi says so.

**Anyone who wants something to look after rather than play with.** Moflin does
that, at $429, with no camera and no expectations.

## What we cannot tell you

**How well the auto-return works now.** KEYi shipped a fix and described the
original as a problem. We have not tested the current firmware and will not
report a maker's fix as though we had verified it.

**What KEYi's warranty term is.** Its site advertises "premium warranty
protection" and "lifetime customer service" without publishing a term we could
read.

**How long the battery holds up.** 1,350 mAh cycled through a full charge most
days is a lot of cycles in a year, and no maker in this category publishes a
degradation figure.
