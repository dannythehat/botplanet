**The short answer.** The Bubot 800P Gen2 is the corded contrarian of our
catalogue's upper half. While every flagship went cordless, BuBlue built a
corded robotic pool cleaner that never runs out mid-clean, scrubs the waterline,
climbs steps, and publishes more real numbers than several bigger brands — 3,566 GPH, a
150-watt motor, four brushes, two suction ports, six litres of basket. It costs
about $800 and carries a stated one-year warranty, which half this catalogue
cannot manage.

The catch is not the machine. It is that BuBlue is a young brand you will mostly
meet through Amazon, and two of the claims in our own artwork for it go beyond
anything BuBlue actually states. Both get sections below.

---

## Who this is for

A pool up to 1,076 sq ft whose owner wants every wet surface handled — floor,
walls, waterline, plus steps and platforms — without owning a battery. Corded means
the cycle never dies at the far end of the pool, nothing degrades over five
summers, and the machine weighs whatever it weighs forever.

It is also the right machine for the buyer who reads spec sheets. BuBlue publishes
suction, motor wattage, brush count, port count, basket volume and filter fineness.
Aiper, for comparison, publishes no weight for three of the four machines of theirs
we cover. A young brand earning trust with numbers is worth encouraging.

---

## Who should look elsewhere

- **You hate cables on principle.** This one has TangleEase management and fifty
  feet of reach by our research, but it is still a cable. The Aiper Scuba S1 does
  the same zones cordless for less.
- **Your pool is much over 1,000 sq ft.** The Aiper Scuba X1 Pro Max carries a
  100 ft length rating; this machine is rated by area, and modestly.
- **You want the surface skimmed.** Floor, walls, waterline — not floating leaves.
- **You want a decade-old brand behind the warranty.** BuBlue is new. The warranty
  is real and stated; the track record is short, and we will not pretend
  otherwise.

## A correction about the brand, first

Our own records said, until today, that BuBlue had no reachable website — that the
only way to meet this manufacturer was its Amazon storefront. That was wrong, and
the error was ours: we had recorded the wrong domain. BuBlue's real site resolves,
carries a full product page for this machine, and answered half the questions this
review needed — including the warranty, the suction figures and the filter
fineness, none of which the Amazon listing states.

We flag it for two reasons. First, honesty: several caveats we might have written
about "a brand with no website" would have been false, and we nearly wrote them.
Second, usefulness: the manuals, FAQs and support live on bublue.com, and an owner
who only knows the Amazon listing would never find them.

## Unlimited corded power, and the cable that is not a pool rating

BuBlue sells this machine on "unlimited corded power", and the phrase is fair: no
runtime figure appears anywhere on this page because none exists. The cycle runs
until it is done. Nothing charges overnight, nothing ages on a calendar — the
argument we made for the corded Dolphins, made here with more zones covered.

One number needs guarding. Our research puts the cable at about fifty feet, and a
fifty-foot cord is **not** a fifty-foot pool rating. BuBlue rates this machine by
area — 1,076 sq ft — and the cord has to run from the wall socket, down the deck
and across the water. The cable management is BuBlue's TangleEase system, and
tangling is the honest historical weakness of every corded machine; we have not
tested whether the trademark beats the physics.

---

## Suction: the numbers BuBlue actually publishes

3,566 GPH through two suction ports, driven by a 150-watt three-axis motor, with
four roller brushes that BuBlue's Bluehole branding sums as "4X scrubbing, 2X
suction". Tank-style tracks carry it up walls and over steps.

Strip the branding and the plain numbers remain, and they are respectable: more
claimed flow than the Dolphin Nautilus CC Plus, less than Aiper's cordless
flagships, from a machine that never has to budget a battery. As always, GPH is
the manufacturer's pump rating, not an independent measurement — but a maker that
states motor wattage and port count is showing more of its working than most.

---

## Four zones, and what "shallow" really means here

Floor, walls, waterline and shallow areas — the same four-zone shape as the Aiper
Scuba S1, from the listing's own title, with steps and platforms claimed on top.

Here is the nuance our own artwork gets ahead of. Our creative says the machine
cleans in water as shallow as twelve inches. BuBlue's actual words are different:
its smart sensors "**bypass** shallow zones and obstacles" — that is,
it detects the shallow end and avoids getting stuck there. Detecting and surviving
a shallow zone is not the same claim as cleaning one, no source we read states a
minimum operating depth, and the twelve-inch figure appears nowhere but our own
image. Treat the picture as illustration; treat BuBlue's sentence as the spec. If
a shallow tanning ledge is your actual problem, the Aiper Scuba S1 is the machine
that puts a number on it.

---

## The app: path width, car mode and schedules

Bluetooth or Wi-Fi, and a feature list longer than the mid-range usually gets:
mode selection across wall, floor, eco and auto; cleaning-cycle duration;
customisable path width; scheduled cleans booked from the calendar; one-tap return
to the waterline for retrieval; and a remote-control car mode for steering the
machine at a spot you can see.

Path width and car mode are genuinely uncommon at this price. The scheduling
carries the standard corded advantage: unlike every cordless machine we cover, a
schedule here never fails because somebody forgot to put the robot back on charge.

---

## Above-ground or in-ground? Both answers exist

BuBlue's own FAQ calls the 800P "ideal for above-ground pools up to 1,076 sq ft" —
and in the same breath says it works on vinyl, fibreglass and concrete, which is
an in-ground materials list. The Amazon listing sells it for pools generally. Our
catalogue records both rather than picking a side, and the honest reading is that
a machine rated by area and able to climb walls serves either, with BuBlue's own
emphasis on the above-ground case.

If your pool is a large tiled in-ground one, nothing we read rules this machine
out — but nothing puts BuBlue's weight behind it either, and the brands that state
in-ground ratings plainly are a click away.

---

## Warranty, support and the strike-through price

One year, stated on BuBlue's own page, with a thirty-day money-back guarantee and
24/7 support beside it. A stated term from a young brand beats the blank space
where Polaris's and Aiper's terms should be, and it is worth saying so.

The price theatre deserves one sentence. BuBlue's site lists $799.99 against a
struck-through $1,099 "regular price"; Amazon sells it at effectively the same
$799.97. When two channels both sell at the "sale" price, the sale price is the
price. Judge it as an $800 machine, because that is what it costs.

---

## What we cannot tell you

Written from published sources and no pool:

- **Weight.** Not published by BuBlue or the listing.
- **Cable length, confirmed.** Our research says about fifty feet; neither source
  states it.
- **A minimum operating depth.** Our artwork's twelve-inch figure has no source;
  BuBlue claims shallow-zone avoidance, not shallow-zone cleaning.
- **Runtime per cycle.** Corded, so unlimited in principle; no cycle duration is
  stated either.
- **How TangleEase behaves on hour three.** The trademark is not a test result.
- **Long-term reliability.** The brand is young and the Gen2 is a 2026 release.
  The one-year warranty is the enforceable fact; everything longer is hope.

---

## The verdict

The Bubot 800P Gen2 is the best-documented surprise in our catalogue: a young
brand publishing numbers the established names withhold, wrapping them in a stated
warranty, and betting on corded power while everyone else chases batteries. For a
mid-sized pool where the cable reaches, it does the full set of wet surfaces for
$800 with nothing to charge and nothing to age.

Buy it eyes-open on the two soft spots: the brand's track record is as new as the
machine, and the shallow-water story is avoidance, not cleaning, whatever any
picture — including ours — might suggest. If those are acceptable, this is the
strongest corded case on the site. If they are not, the Aiper Scuba S1 runs the
same zones on a battery, and the Dolphin name costs about the same.
