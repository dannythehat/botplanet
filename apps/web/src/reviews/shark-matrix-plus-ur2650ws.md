The Matrix Plus vacuums, mops, maps by LiDAR, and empties itself into a HEPA
base Shark rates at thirty days. What it does not do is lift the mop pad, and
what it does not have is object detection.

## What $280 costs you

Two things, and they are the two the dearer machines in this catalogue are
selling.

The mop pad attaches by hand and there is no auto-lift. In a carpeted house
that means taking the pad off before a carpet run, or leaving it off. It is a
thing you have to remember rather than a thing the machine handles, and if you
will not remember it, you will be dragging a damp pad across a rug on a
schedule.

And 360-degree LiDAR is mapping, not seeing. It builds an accurate plan of your
rooms and drives it well. It does not recognise a charging cable, a sock or a
dog's mess as objects to be avoided — Shark's own PowerDetect line adds 3D
object detection explicitly and this listing does not mention it, which is how
you can tell the two apart.

## Who this is genuinely right for

A clear floor and a modest budget. That is the honest combination, and it
describes a great many houses.

If your floors are usually clear of things, obstacle avoidance is a feature
you pay six hundred dollars for and never see working. If you have hard floor
and you are prepared to put the mop pad on when you want mopping and take it
off when you do not, the lift is a convenience rather than a capability. Strip
those two out and the gap between this and a $600 machine narrows to a dock
and some Pascals.

## Sonic mopping and the self-cleaning brushroll

Shark's mopping vibrates the pad rather than spinning it, at a rate the
listing calls sonic and does not quantify. On dried-on marks a vibrating pad
does more than a passive one being pushed along.

The self-cleaning brushroll is the part that matters in a house with long hair
or a shedding animal. Hair wrapping round the brush is the single most common
complaint in this category and it is a design question rather than a suction
question. Shark has been building brushrolls that cut the hair off themselves
for years, and at this price that is the specification to notice.

## What we could not find out

Shark's own product page for this SKU redirected to a corporate landing page
when we fetched it on 10 August 2026, so this is written from the Amazon
listing.

Suction is not published — Shark prints no Pascal figure on any of its
listings in this set. Runtime, bin capacity, water tank volume, noise,
dimensions and weight are not published either, and all are recorded as not
disclosed.

"Matrix" also names a second Shark line: the AI Ultra AV2501S and AV2511AE at
about $420 use Matrix Clean and are different machines with a different review
pool. A search for a Shark Matrix robot vacuum returns both lines mixed
together, and we cannot tell you which line a given result belongs to without
reading the model code off the title. Neither can the result.

## Who should buy one

Somebody with a clear floor who wants the cheapest genuinely good robot vacuum
with a self-emptying base, and who is happy to manage the mop pad by hand.
Thirty-six thousand people have rated it 4.6 and that is not an accident.

Anyone with a cluttered floor should spend the extra on a machine with object
detection. Anyone with carpet who will not remember to take a pad off should
buy something that lifts it for them.
