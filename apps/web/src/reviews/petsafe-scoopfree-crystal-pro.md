Two hundred and thirty dollars against five hundred and ninety-nine, six
hundred and ten, and six hundred and ninety-nine. The ScoopFree Crystal Pro is
a third the price of the boxes it sits beside, and there are two things you
need to know before that reads as a bargain.

It works only with PetSafe's own crystal trays, for the life of the machine.
And it is rated 3.1 stars from 184 ratings, which is over a full star below
every other box in this catalogue.

## The rating is the finding

Litter-Robot 4: 4.4. Casa Leo: 4.0. PETKIT: 4.0. This one: 3.1, from 184
people, read on 10 August 2026.

We are not going to explain that away. A gap that size across a review pool
that size is the most useful thing on this page, and a comparison site that
prints the price and quietly omits the score is not doing the job.

What we can tell you is what the machine is and is not, so you can judge
whether the complaints are likely to be yours.

## What it does not have

No app. No Wi-Fi. A digital display health counter on the unit tells you how
many times the cat has been in, and that is the entirety of the health
tracking.

That is not automatically a fault — plenty of people do not want another
account — but it is a different product from the other three, and it is worth
being clear that "health monitoring" here means a number on a display, not a
per-cat history you can show a vet.

No published weight limit either. PetSafe states one for the Crystal *Classic*,
a different and cheaper machine, and does not state one for the Crystal Pro on
any page we could read. We are not carrying the Classic's figure across; a
number lifted from a different product is worse than no number.

## The trays, which are the real cost

"Only compatible with official PetSafe ScoopFree Disposable Crystal Litter
Trays or Reusable Litter Trays."

That is the sentence to read twice. The crystal system is why the box goes up
to thirty days untouched — the longest interval of the four, and a genuine
advantage — but it also means the consumable is chosen for you for as long as
you own it. The other three boxes here take ordinary clumping litter from any
shop.

What to do with that: work out what a year of trays costs at your cat's usage,
multiply by three, and add it to $229.99 before you compare. Do the same
exercise for a $699 box running on supermarket clay. The answer is not obvious
in either direction, and it is not the answer the shelf price gives you.

## Where it genuinely wins

Thirty days is a long time. If the thing you actually want is to stop thinking
about the litter box for a month at a time, nothing else here comes close —
the PETKIT claims seventeen days, the Litter-Robot about eight for one cat.

Crystal litter dehydrates solid waste rather than clumping it, which PetSafe
puts at five times the odour control of clumping litter, and it is 99% dust
free. It also does not stick to paws, which is the thing tracked litter across
a hallway is.

And there is no rotating globe or drum. A rake sweeps a flat tray. Fewer moving
parts is a real argument in a machine that lives with waste, whatever the
rating says.

## What we could not find out

PetSafe publishes dimensions of 28.2 by 20.4 by 16 inches and the tray
compatibility. That is the whole of it on the product page. No weight limit, no
entry dimensions, no warranty length, no tray price, no noise figure, no cycle
time. All recorded as not disclosed.

The tray price is the omission that matters, because the tray is the product's
running cost and the reason the purchase price is low. We could not read it
from PetSafe's own product page.

One more thing worth knowing before you search: "ScoopFree Crystal" is a family
rather than a model, and it contains four live SKUs. The Crystal Pro is
$229.99. There is also a Crystal Pro **Legacy** front-entry at $229.95 — five
cents apart and a previous generation — a Legacy uncovered at $142.49 and a
Crystal Classic at $99. The one reviewed here is the non-Legacy Crystal Pro,
ASIN B0DR3JP2FZ.

## Who should buy one

Somebody whose actual goal is thirty days of not thinking about it, who is
happy to be tied to one brand's trays, and who has read the 3.1 and decided
what they think about it.

Anybody who wants ordinary litter from any shop should buy one of the other
three. Anybody who wants a per-cat health history should buy the PETKIT.
Anybody buying this because it is cheap should do the three-year tray sum
first, because that is the number the price tag is hiding.
