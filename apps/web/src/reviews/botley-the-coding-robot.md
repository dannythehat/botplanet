Botley programs by remote control, and the remote is the whole idea.

A child presses directional buttons on a detachable controller — forward,
turn, repeat — building a sequence of up to 80 steps. Detach the remote,
press go, and Botley drives that exact sequence back. No app opens at any
point. No login, no screen, no reading required to use it.

## How the coding actually works

This is sequencing, not programming in the sense Scratch or Sphero's block
editor mean it. There is no if/then, no loop you can nest, no variable. You
are teaching a five year old that a plan is a list of steps in order, that
the order matters, and that the robot will do exactly what you told it and
nothing you meant instead.

That last part is the actual lesson. The first time a sequence sends Botley
into a wall because a turn was one step too early is worth more than the
sequence that works first try.

## What "screen-free" buys you here

Sphero's Mini is not the comparison: its whole experience lives inside a
tablet app. The honest comparison is Sphero's indi, which is also screen-free
— colour cards laid on the floor, with an app that exists but is optional.

What separates Botley from indi is the interface, not the screen. indi reads
cards a child lays down; Botley is programmed with buttons on a remote, in
sequences of up to 80 steps, and never needs an app at all. The remote is a
physical object a small child can hold and press without an adult unlocking a
device first.

For a household that wants no screen anywhere near a five year old, both
qualify. The choice is cards on the floor from around four, or a remote and a
longer sequence at five to seven.

## Who this is for

Five to seven, per Learning Resources' own rating, and specifically a child
who cannot yet read fluently enough to follow an app's on-screen instructions
unassisted. The remote's buttons are pictures and directions, not menus.

A first coding toy, in other words — the thing you buy before you know
whether the interest is real, in the same slot the Sphero Mini fills for an
older child.

## Who should look elsewhere

**A child who has already done screen-free sequencing.** There is no
coding-app tier this grows into the way Sphero's lineup runs Mini through
BOLT through text-based Python on one shared app. Outgrow Botley and the next
step is a different toy entirely, not an upgrade.

**Anyone counting on a specific piece count.** Learning Resources sells this
under more than one packaging — different sources list 46 pieces, 77 pieces
and 78 pieces for what appears to be the same core set. What is consistent
across every listing is the robot, the remote and activity cards; the exact
card count is not something we would state with confidence.
