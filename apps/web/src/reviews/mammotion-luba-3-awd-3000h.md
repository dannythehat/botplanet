It is also four hundred dollars more than its own smaller sibling for twice the

## Who should not buy this

Anybody whose lawn is under about a third of an acre. This is a machine built
for 32,670 square feet, and our matcher discounts one rated for far more ground
than the reader has. Not because it would fail. A robot built for three
quarters of an acre is larger, heavier, noisier and dearer than a small garden
needs, and you are paying for all four.

The LUBA 3 AWD 1500H covers 0.37 acre for $2,399. The Dreame A3 AWD 1000 climbs
the same eighty per cent over a quarter acre for $1,599.99.

Anybody with a flat lawn of this size. Half an acre of level grass is the WORX
Landroid Vision's territory at $1,022.54, and the Husqvarna 410iQ's at
$2,499.99 with a four-year warranty. All-wheel drive on the flat is money spent
on nothing.

## The 1500H against this one

Four hundred dollars separates them and the specification does not, except for
area:

- 1500H — 0.37 acre, $2,399
- 3000H — 0.75 acre, $2,799

Same eighty per cent, same all-wheel drive, same 360-degree LiDAR and AI
vision, same 15 Ah battery and 215-minute run, same cutting heights.

So the decision is only ever about how much grass you have, and the sum is
worth doing properly. If you are between the two — say half an acre — the 1500H
is rated for less than you have and is therefore not a candidate at all. Our
matcher rules it out rather than ranking it lower, which is the right treatment
for a machine that cannot finish.

## Eighty per cent, and what it looks like

38.6 degrees. A bank rather than a slope, and roughly double what most of this
category will attempt: the Husqvarna does forty-five per cent inside its area
and fifteen at the boundary, the Segway and the WORX do thirty, the eufy
eighteen degrees.

The navigation is LiDAR and vision rather than satellite, which is the pairing
that matters on this kind of ground. Steep gardens are often wooded gardens,
and a satellite-positioned mower under a mature canopy is a machine that stops
in the middle of the lawn and cannot say why.

## The H is a cutting height, not a trim level

Standard version cuts 1.0 to 2.7 inches. High version cuts 2.2 to 4.0.

Warm-season grasses want to be left long — St Augustine and Bahia are cut at
three inches and up — and a machine whose ceiling is 2.7 inches is scalping
them every pass. The two versions differ by one letter in the model name and
the wrong one is a poor lawn all season.

## What we could not find out

Coverage, slope, drive, cutting heights, battery and runtime are published.
Cutting width is not, for either LUBA 3 — the 15.7-inch figure that circulates
belongs to the LUBA 2 series, and a superseded model's number reprinted as this
one's is precisely the error this site exists not to make.

Noise, IP rating, machine weight, warranty length and the number of separate
zones are also not published, and are recorded as not disclosed.

## Who should buy one

Somebody with between a third and three quarters of an acre, real slopes, and
possibly trees. On that description this is the only machine in the catalogue
that fits, and the four hundred dollars over the 1500H buys twice the ground.

Anybody under a third of an acre with a bank should read the Dreame A3 AWD 1000
first — same climb, twelve hundred dollars less. Anybody with this much flat
grass should not be paying for all-wheel drive at all.
