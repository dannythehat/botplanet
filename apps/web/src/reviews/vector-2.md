Most people searching for this robot are not asking what it does. They are
asking whether it still works.

That is not a guess. The ten highest-ranking questions attached to this product
are variations on one worry — is the Vector robot discontinued, does it still
work, are they still being made, why was it discontinued — and Google puts the
same questions in its own answer box on three separate searches.

So the history comes first, and the robot second.

## Who owns Vector now

Anki Inc. designed and sold Vector in 2018, raised close to $200 million, and
shut down in 2019. Customers were left with a robot whose cleverness lived on
servers that were about to be switched off.

Digital Dream Labs bought the assets and kept the lights on. That company now
trades as ANKI, LLC — the copyright line on the site reads exactly that — and
it is who sells you a Vector today, including through the Digital Dream Labs
storefront on Amazon.

So: the original company is gone, the product is not, and it is on its third
identity. Anyone telling you Vector was discontinued is describing 2019.
Anyone telling you nothing happened is describing a different product.

## The subscription, which is the actual buying decision

Vector's cloud features need a paid subscription. **$11.99 a month, or $99.99 a
year**, read from Anki's own subscription page.

The Amazon listing states the requirement in its own title — "ChatGPT
Subscription Required" — which is more candid than most of this category
manages, and it is the single most important line on the page.

Two practical details from the same source. The subscription is keyed to your
robot's eight-character serial number rather than to your account alone, and
Anki asks you to enable it before onboarding the robot rather than after.
Activation can take up to 36 hours, so a Christmas morning is the wrong time to
discover this.

**What Anki does not publish is a feature-by-feature comparison of a subscribed
Vector against an unsubscribed one.** That gap is conspicuous, it is the reason
"vector robot subscription free" and "anki vector without subscription" are
both searched, and we are not going to fill it with a guess. Assume the
conversational side needs paying for, and treat anything beyond that as
unverified until Anki says so.

Run the arithmetic before buying. A year of subscription costs more than a
Miko 3 subscription, and roughly two years of it costs more than the robot.

## What you are actually getting

A robot about four inches long and under six ounces, on treads, with a screen
for a face and an arm that lifts. It drives around a desk, avoids the edge,
recognises faces, answers questions and goes back to its own charger.

The character is the product and it is genuinely well done. Vector reacts,
sulks, gets curious and notices you — the animation work came from people who
had done it professionally, and eight years on nothing at this size does it
better.

One hardware limitation worth knowing before ordering: **it joins 2.4 GHz
networks only**. A modern mesh router that hides its 2.4 GHz band will need
adjusting. Setup runs through Chrome on a computer or through the phone apps.

## Vector against Cozmo

This comparison comes up constantly, and it has a short answer: **Cozmo is not
buyable.** The only Cozmo listing on Amazon US is a $19 replacement battery.

Both robots came from the same company and Digital Dream Labs owns both lines.
If you want one of the two, Vector is the one you can buy, and that settles it
without either machine having to win on merit.

## Who this is for

Someone who wants a desk robot with a personality and has read the paragraph
about the subscription. Vector is the most charming thing in this category and
it asks for a running cost in exchange.

It also suits a tinkerer. Anki maintains OSKR, an open route for people who
want to run Vector their own way, and there is an unusually determined
community around this robot precisely because it has been abandoned once
already.

## Who should look elsewhere

**Anyone who wants to buy something once.** The subscription is not optional
for the interesting half of the product, and it renews.

**Anyone relying on it working in five years.** This robot has already survived
one company's collapse. That is a point in its favour and also a description of
the risk.

**Anyone buying from the 2018 reviews.** The press coverage that made Vector
famous describes a product sold by a company that no longer exists, with no
subscription attached. It is not a fair guide to what arrives now.

**Anyone who wants a robot pet rather than a robot.** Vector is a gadget with
character. For something to look after, Moflin is the honest alternative and
has no subscription at all.

**A 5 GHz-only household**, until you have checked the router.

## What we cannot tell you

**Exactly what stops working without the subscription.** Anki does not publish
it. This is the question readers most want answered and the one the maker is
quietest about.

**How long the servers stay up.** They have outlived one owner. Nobody can
promise more than that.

**What it costs today.** The Amazon price moved from $199.99 to $184 within a
single morning while this review was being written, which is why the figure on
this page comes from our price checker with the date attached rather than from
a number typed in once.
