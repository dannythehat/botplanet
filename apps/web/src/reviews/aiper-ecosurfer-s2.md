**The short answer.** The Aiper EcoSurfer S2 is a solar skimmer, not a pool cleaner. It floats on the surface and collects leaves, pollen and insects before they sink, and it will not touch your floor, your walls or your waterline. It has thousands of Amazon ratings behind it, and its listing makes more specific claims about sensing than the others do.

Everything on this page about what the machine does comes from Aiper's own listing. We have not run one, and where a figure is Aiper's claim we say so.

---

## Who this is for

A pool under trees. If what lands on the water is the problem, a skimmer that runs through the day catches it before it sinks, stains or has to be vacuumed off the bottom.

It also suits a pool that already has a floor robot. Debris caught at the surface never reaches the floor, so the two machines are doing different jobs, not the same job twice.

## Who should look elsewhere

Anyone whose complaint is the floor, the walls or the waterline. A skimmer does none of them, and Aiper's listing does not suggest otherwise. For those jobs see the [best robotic pool cleaners](/best-robots/robotic-pool-cleaners/).

## What a solar skimmer does, and where this one sits

Every machine here floats, runs on a solar panel plus a battery and charges from a wall adapter on dull days. Where they differ is in how they stay out of trouble: how they sense a wall, whether they get stranded on a step, and how much they hold before the basket needs emptying. Those are the questions the Aiper listing spends its time on, and they are the ones that matter in a real pool.

The listing's headline claim is a 35 hour battery and 24/7 cleaning, with what Aiper calls SolarSeeker technology. Both are Aiper's figures. A battery that long is not the same as a machine that cleans for 35 hours on a grey week, because the panel has to refill it.

## Edges, corners and sensors

Aiper says two dToF sensors read the distance to the wall, so the skimmer can run close along edges and into corners instead of bouncing off them. dToF is a time-of-flight measurement, and it is a different sensor from the ultrasonic radar Betta and BRINBO describe. Whether it does better in a real pool is something we cannot tell you; it is a specific, checkable claim, which is more than most skimmer listings offer.

## Not getting stuck

The underside carries adjustable columns that Aiper says stop the machine stranding on a pool step or ledge, adjustable between zero and two inches. This is the practical hazard for any skimmer in a pool with a shallow shelf. A column you can set is a sensible answer, and the range is Aiper's figure.

## Filtration

Aiper's listing names a 150 micron filter and a DebrisGuard leak-proof baffle, which it says stops debris escaping when the skimmer reverses. A finer mesh catches pollen and fine silt, and also clogs sooner. The basket volume is not stated in what we read.

## Charging and the app

Charging is solar, with a wall adapter as the second route. The listing also names app support and over-the-air upgrades. We have not seen the app, and the charge time from the adapter is not stated in what we read.

## What we cannot tell you

- **The name.** Amazon calls it the EcoSurfer S2. The casing on the unit and some independent write-ups say Surfer S2. We use Amazon's name because that is what you will search and buy.
- **Runtime in practice.** The 35 hours is Aiper's number.
- **The warranty term,** the weight and the largest pool it is rated for. None appear in what we read.
- **Basket volume,** which decides how often you empty it.
- **Colours.** Blue and Gray are separate listings at the same price. Renewed stock is also sold; ours is the new Blue.

## The verdict

The EcoSurfer S2 is the skimmer to look at first if you want the most-reviewed machine and a maker that publishes specific claims about sensing and anti-stranding. It is a skimmer, so it adds to a floor robot and never replaces one. The number to hold it to is not 35 hours but how it behaves on a cloudy week.
