Half an acre for $1,022.54. Nothing else in our lawn catalogue covers that much
ground for anywhere near that money — the Husqvarna rated for the same area
costs $2,499.99.

The Landroid Vision navigates by camera rather than by satellite, which is a
genuine advantage in a garden with trees and the reason to read the rest of
this page rather than just the price.

## Who should not buy this

Anybody with a slope past thirty per cent. That is seventeen degrees, and it is
the same ceiling the Segway manages. If your lawn has a bank the answer is the
Dreame A3 AWD 1000 at eighty per cent, or a Mammotion LUBA 3 AWD.

Anybody who wants a long warranty on an outdoor machine. WORX does not publish
one on the product page we read, and the Husqvarna at two and a half times the
price carries four years.

## The camera is the argument

Vision AI with RTK Cloud, which WORX states needs no on-site antenna. A high
dynamic range, full HD, wide angle camera with auto white balance, which the
company says handles extreme contrast and glare.

That last detail is not marketing padding. A camera-guided mower's hard problem
is a lawn half in bright sun and half in deep shade at four in the afternoon,
and auto white balance across that boundary is the difference between a machine
that keeps going and one that stops. Whether it works is not something we can
test from a specification sheet, and WORX is at least naming the problem.

What the camera buys you against a satellite system is a garden under trees. A
mower positioned by satellite is defeated by a mature canopy, no setting fixes
it, and it is the single most common disappointment in this category.

## Half an acre, and what "Landroid Vision" does not mean

"Landroid Vision" is a family, not a model. At least four are live in the US:

- WR320, 2WD, half acre, $1,022.54 — this one
- WO7144, quarter acre, $999.99
- WR342, 4WD, half acre, $2,069.99
- WR344, 4WD, one acre, $2,646.18

Twenty-three dollars separates the half-acre 2WD from the quarter-acre model,
which is the sort of gap that catches people who compared on price alone. And
the 4WD models are a different argument entirely — if you are looking at those,
you have a slope, and the slope is what to compare on.

## The numbers

An 8.7-inch cutting width, which is the narrowest of the three machines here
that publish one — the Husqvarna cuts 9.4 inches, the eufy 8.

Cutting height 1.57 to 3.54 inches, electronically adjusted. That top end
matters: warm-season grasses want three inches and more, and this clears it.

## What we could not find out

WORX publishes slope, cutting width, cutting height, coverage and the camera
specification. It does not publish battery capacity, runtime, charge time,
noise, IP rating, machine weight or warranty length on the pages we read on 10
August 2026. All recorded as not disclosed.

Runtime is the omission that matters at this size. Half an acre is a lot of
ground for one battery, and a machine that needs three charge cycles to finish
a cut is a different proposition from one that needs two. Husqvarna publishes
84 minutes of mowing against 108 minutes of charging for a smaller battery and
the same rated area. WORX publishes nothing comparable and we are not going to
estimate it.

## Who should buy one

Somebody with a flat half acre, trees, and no appetite for spending two and a
half thousand dollars. On that description this is comfortably the best value
in the catalogue, and the camera navigation is the right technology for the
garden.

Anybody with a slope should look at the AWD machines. Anybody who wants
published runtime figures and a four-year warranty before spending a thousand
dollars outdoors should read the Husqvarna and decide what that costs.
