The X50 Ultra's listing claims something none of the other ten machines in our
robot vacuum catalogue claim: it climbs. Dreame states 2.36 inches — six
centimetres — of obstacle crossing, which is a threshold or a raised doorway
rather than a lip.

At $999.99 with 20,000 Pa, obstacle avoidance, 360-degree navigation and a
detangling brush, it is dreame's flagship and it is priced against the roborock
S8 Max Ultra.

## Obstacle crossing is not mop lifting

These are two different claims and it is worth separating them before you
spend a thousand dollars.

Obstacle crossing is how high a step the machine can get over. Mop lifting is
how far the wet pads rise off a carpet so they do not drag across it. A machine
can be excellent at the first and useless at the second, and the failure that
ruins a robot vacuum in a carpeted house is the second one.

Dreame's listing claims the crossing figure and says nothing about lifting.
Our catalogue therefore records no mop lifting for this machine, which will
keep it out of BotMatch results for anybody who says they have carpet.

## What the six centimetres is genuinely for

Split-level rooms, a raised threshold into a bathroom, a doorway with a bar
across it, a thick rug with a hard edge. Any of those stops most robot vacuums
and turns a whole-floor clean into a one-room clean without telling you.

What to do with that: walk the route the robot would take and look at every
change of level on it. If there is one, and it is under six centimetres, this
machine is worth its premium over a cheaper one that cannot cross it. If your
floor is flat, the premium buys nothing.

## Who should buy one

Somebody with a level change the robot has to get over, on mostly hard floor,
who wants dreame's dock and detangling brush. That is a specific house and
this is a good answer for it.

Anyone whose deciding factor is carpet should buy the roborock S8 Max Ultra at
$949.99 instead, which is fifty dollars cheaper and whose maker publishes a
20mm lift figure on its own page. Anyone with a flat floor and no unusual
requirement is looking at a thousand-dollar machine when a four hundred and
fifty dollar one does the same work.
