**Moxie does not work.** Not "works with limitations" — the company that made it
shut down, the servers it depended on went off, and the robot on the desk stopped
talking. We hold no buy button for it and we will not add one.

A used one at $200 to $400 is a plastic shell with a screen unless you are
prepared to run a server for it yourself.

---

## Who this page is for

A parent who saw Moxie recommended for an anxious or lonely child, found second-
hand listings, and is trying to work out whether it is a bargain or a brick.

It is closer to a brick, and the reason is worth understanding because it applies
to more robots than this one.

## What happened

Embodied sold Moxie as a companion for children — conversation, daily missions,
reading, social and emotional learning — at **$799**.

None of that lived in the robot. Speech recognition, the conversation, the
emotional inference: all of it ran on Embodied's servers. In late 2024 the
company told owners it was ceasing operations and that Moxie would stop
functioning. The shutdown is reported across the coverage as **30 January 2025**.

We could not read Embodied's own closing FAQ to quote it: the page returned a
403 to us on 8 August 2026. So the date above is what the reporting says
consistently rather than something we lifted off the company's own page, and we
would rather tell you which it is.

## The refunds

**Most owners did not get one.** Embodied's position, as reported at the time,
was that it could not offer refunds given its financial state, with a narrow
carve-out for people who had bought within about thirty days of the closure
announcement. Buyers on payment plans were told it was out of the company's
hands.

An $800 product, sold to families, switched off, mostly without money back. That
is the fact that should decide what you think of the brand rather than any
specification.

## OpenMoxie, and what it actually asks of you

The robots are not all silent, and the reason is volunteers rather than the
manufacturer. **OpenMoxie** is a community project that stands up a local server
on your own network so a Moxie can work without Embodied.

Read what it needs before you treat it as a rescue. From the project's own
documentation, you need three things:

- a computer on the same wireless network — a PC, Mac, Linux box or a Raspberry
  Pi 5, left running;
- **an OpenAI account with credits**, because speech-to-text and the
  conversation are billed to you now;
- Docker.

And it is not the whole product. The project says plainly that much of the
content is supported — Daily Missions, Reading, Wild Workout — while newer
modules including Ocean Explorer, Animal Faces and Story Maker are missing.

So the honest description of a working Moxie in 2026: a robot kept alive by
strangers, on hardware you supply, with an API bill you pay, missing some of what
it was sold with. That is an admirable piece of community engineering and a poor
thing to hand a seven-year-old expecting the robot from the advert.

## The one thing that decides whether a used unit can be saved

Reporting at the time of the shutdown said Moxie needed an update installed
before the servers went off in order to be pointed at a local server afterwards,
with the instructions emailed to owners around 30 December 2024 — about a month
before the deadline.

**We could not confirm that requirement in OpenMoxie's own documentation.** We
read the project's README and it does not state a pre-shutdown firmware
condition. It may be there in the wider setup guides; we did not find it, and we
are not going to assert a technical prerequisite we could not check.

What that means for you, practically: a second-hand Moxie may or may not be
recoverable, the seller almost certainly cannot tell you which, and there is no
way to find out before the money changes hands. Treat any used listing as a
robot that might never speak.

## What we cannot tell you

**Embodied's exact refund wording**, because its own page would not open for us.

**Whether a specific used unit can join OpenMoxie**, for the reason above.

**How Moxie performed with a child.** We never tested one, and by the time this
page exists there is no working product to test.

**Whether anything will restore the original service.** Nothing suggests it, and
we are not going to hint at a rescue that nobody has announced.

## Buy one of these instead

If the reason Moxie appealed was a child having something to talk to, these are
reviewed here and their companies are trading.

- **Miko 3** — the closest replacement for what Moxie was sold as: a
  conversational robot aimed at children, with content behind it. Our review is
  blunt about what you get without the subscription, which is the thing to read
  before buying.
- **Loona** — a petbot rather than a tutor. If the appeal was company and play
  rather than lessons, this is the livelier machine.
- **Ropet** — a small furry desk companion for somebody who wanted a creature,
  not a curriculum.

And read the Vector 2.0 review before any of them. Vector has the same
architecture Moxie had — the personality on somebody else's servers — and its
review is where we explain what that exposure costs.

If the child is anxious rather than bored, the guide on robotic pets for elderly
relatives covers the same evidence question from the other end: what these
machines have actually been shown to do, and what is marketing.

## The verdict

Do not buy a Moxie. New is impossible and used is a gamble on a rescue you
cannot test before paying, run on a server you have to maintain, with an API
bill attached and pieces of the product missing.

The lesson is bigger than the robot, and it is the reason this page exists on a
site that would rather sell you something. **A companion robot whose personality
lives on the maker's servers is rented, not owned, and the rent can stop being
collectible.** Moxie is what that looks like when it happens: an $800 product,
switched off, mostly without refunds, kept partly alive by volunteers. Ask of any
robot you are about to buy for a child what happens to it if the company folds.
Moxie's owners did not get to ask.
