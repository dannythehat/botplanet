Eilik is a 230-gram desk robot with an OLED face, four servos and a great deal
of character, sold for $139.99. It has no microphone conversation, no cloud
account and no subscription.

It also runs for about ninety minutes and carries a ninety-day warranty.

Both of those numbers come from Energize Lab's own specification table, neither
appears in the marketing, and together they are most of what separates a happy
owner from a disappointed one.

## The battery is the thing to know

450 mAh. **1.5 hours of use, 1 hour to charge.**

That is the shortest runtime of anything reviewed in this category by a wide
margin, and it changes what Eilik is. This is not a robot that sits on a desk
being alive all day. It is a robot you switch on, play with, and put back on a
USB-C cable.

Plenty of owners never notice, because ninety minutes is longer than most
people spend interacting with a desk toy in one sitting. Anyone imagining a
companion that is simply present all day is imagining a different product.

The port is USB-C and the input is 5 V 1 A, so it charges from anything.

## The warranty is 90 days

Casio gives a year on the Moflin. Miko gives a year on the Miko 3. Energize Lab
gives ninety days.

At $139.99 that is a smaller exposure than it would be at $429, and it is still
the shortest cover in the category. Anyone buying this as a Christmas present
in November should know that the warranty runs out in February.

## What it actually does

It reacts. Touch it and it responds, pick it up and it responds differently,
leave it alone and it sulks. The face is a 1.54-inch OLED and the expressions
are the product — four EM3 servos move the body, and the animation work is
better than the price suggests.

**Two Eiliks interact with each other**, which is why Energize Lab sells a pair
for $269.98 and why "two eilik robots" is a real search. One is a toy. Two is a
small ongoing scene on your desk. Whether that is worth an extra $130 is a
personal question, but the pair is not a gimmick.

It does not hold conversations. There is no assistant here, no questions
answered and no microphone listening to the room — which for a lot of buyers is
the feature rather than the gap.

## The range, and what the DQ actually is

Energize Lab sells five things and the names do not explain themselves. These
are the maker's own prices, read the same day.

**Eilik — $139.99.** The one this review is about and the one to buy first.

**Eilik DQ — $199.98.** DQ is Desert Quester. It is the same hardware in a
desert colour scheme, with an exclusive Monster Shooter game, a three-weapon
accessory kit and its own animations. Sixty dollars for a skin, a game and some
props. If you want the character, buy the base Eilik; if you have one already
and want the other thing, that is what the premium buys.

**Eilik AI Station — $99.** The dock that adds voice chat and face recognition,
which is how you get conversation out of this range. Budget for it if talking
is the point.

**Panxer — $119.90.** A remote-control vehicle for Eilik to ride. Not a robot.

**Eiliko — $59.90.** A smaller, different product rather than a cheap Eilik.

The trap for a buyer is that Amazon lists most of these under one brand with
similar artwork. Check the name before ordering: DQ, Station, Panxer and Eiliko
are four different things.

## Colours

Energize Lab's own store sells Blue and Pink. Amazon carries more, and gold,
silver, yellow, orange and green all get searched by name.

Nothing about the machine changes with the colour. There are also outfit sets
from $9.90 if dressing it up is part of the appeal, which for a large part of
this product's audience it plainly is.

## Who this is for

Someone who wants character on a desk without an account. No login, no cloud,
no monthly charge, no microphone listening — Eilik is self-contained in a way
almost nothing else in this category is.

It suits a teenager or an adult more than a small child, despite the toy
appearance. It suits an office desk. It suits anyone who has looked at a $429
Moflin and wanted to spend a third of that to find out whether they like the
idea at all.

## Who should look elsewhere

**Anyone who wants to talk to it.** The base robot does not converse. That is
the AI Station at $99 more, and it should be priced in from the start rather
than discovered later.

**Anyone who wants it running all day.** Ninety minutes.

**Anyone who wants a pet rather than a toy.** Eilik is expressive and it is not
warm. For something to look after, Moflin is the honest alternative at three
times the price.

**Anyone who needs long warranty cover.** Ninety days is ninety days.

## What we cannot tell you

**How long the servos last.** Four moving parts on a $139.99 robot handled
daily by design, and no maker publishes a cycle count.

**Whether the app keeps being updated.** Energize Lab promises regular updates
through its cross-platform software and has delivered them so far. Nothing
guarantees that continues, and unlike Vector there is no subscription revenue
funding it — which cuts both ways.

**How the Amazon colours differ from the store's.** Energize Lab lists Blue and
Pink; Amazon carries more, and we have not verified each one against the
maker's catalogue.
