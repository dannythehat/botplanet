**The short answer.** The Proteus DX4 Plus is a corded Maytronics machine that climbs
walls, does the sun ledges and — according to Maytronics' own listing copy — scrubs
the waterline too. It is the one Dolphin in this range that claims the full set of
surfaces at a mid-range price.

Two things you need before you buy it. It is rated for pools up to **33 ft**, which is
shorter than most people assume and shorter than the Proteus DX4 it sits next to. And
the waterline claim comes from a sales listing rather than a technical sheet — which
matters more than it sounds, because we have caught this exact manufacturer's
listings and technical sheets disagreeing on this exact feature before.

---

## Who this is for

An in-ground pool under 33 ft where you want floors, walls and the tile line handled
by one corded machine and you would rather not think about batteries. Maytronics is
the most established name in this category and the Proteus sits in the middle of its
range: more surfaces than the entry models, none of the app and mapping cost of the
top ones.

It suits someone with steps and a sun ledge in particular. Those are named explicitly
on the listing, and they are exactly the shapes cheaper robots skip.

---

## Who should look elsewhere

- **Your pool is longer than 33 ft.** This is the hard one, and the easiest to get
  wrong — see the next section.
- **Your pool is above ground.** In-ground only.
- **The waterline is the entire reason you are buying.** Read the waterline section
  before you commit. We would not tell you not to buy it; we would tell you to get
  the claim confirmed in writing.
- **You want an app.** Nothing on this listing mentions one, and there is no Wi-Fi
  claim to check.

---

## The 33 ft limit, and the sibling that is rated for 50

The Proteus DX4 Plus is one of **six** machines Amazon files under a single parent
listing: DX1, DX3, DX3 with Caddy, **DX4 Plus**, DX5 with Caddy and DX5i. They share
a name, a shape and a product page, and they do not share a specification.

The one that catches people is the plain Proteus **DX4**, which is rated for pools up
to 50 ft. Ours — the **DX4 Plus** — is rated to 33. A model with "Plus" in its name
covering a third less pool is not intuitive, and the two are one dropdown click apart
on the same page.

**So what to do with that.** Check the title of the exact listing you are buying says
33 FT, and check the model number. Ours is 99996207-LESW on the US listing, though
the same machine is also researched as 99996290-DX4 — Maytronics part numbers carry
market and retailer suffixes, so two genuine numbers can describe one product. If the
listing you are on says 50 ft, you are looking at a different robot.

---

## The waterline question, and why we are being careful

Maytronics' listing bullet is unambiguous: this robot "actively scrubs your pool
floor, walls, waterline, steps, and sun ledges". The product title is narrower — it
says "Wall & Sun-ledge Scrubbing" and does not mention the waterline at all.

Both of those are Maytronics' own words on Maytronics' own listing, and they do not
say quite the same thing.

Here is why we are flagging what most reviews would smooth over. We have already
found this manufacturer's marketing and its technical documentation disagreeing about
waterline scrubbing on a different machine: the Dolphin Nautilus CC Plus is sold with
copy implying waterline coverage, and the technical sheet for its part number lists
Waterline Scrubbing as **No**. We got that wrong ourselves before we checked, and
corrected it publicly.

We have not been able to read a Maytronics technical sheet for the Proteus DX4 Plus.
No Maytronics product page for it has been found; everything on this page is sourced
from the retail listing. So the waterline claim here is *the manufacturer's, on a
sales page, uncorroborated by a spec sheet*.

If the waterline is your whole reason for buying, ask the seller to confirm waterline
scrubbing against the part number before you pay. If it is a bonus on top of floors
and walls, this is a non-issue.

## What it actually cleans

Floor, walls, steps and sun ledges, with the waterline as discussed. It is a
wall-climbing machine on tracks rather than wheels, which is what gets it up a
vertical surface and around the curve of a ledge.

Corded, so the cycle never ends early. There is no battery to charge, nothing that
degrades over five summers, and no runtime figure to plan around — which is the
quiet advantage corded machines keep over the cordless ones costing half again as
much.

Maytronics does not state a cycle time on this listing, and does not state a cable
length either. For a 33 ft pool the cable is unlikely to be the constraint, but it is
not published and we will not guess at it.

---

## Filtration

A top-load filter, accessed from above rather than from underneath.

That is worth more than it sounds. A bottom-access filter means turning a wet
eighteen-pound machine over to get at it; a top-load one means lifting a lid at the
poolside. It is the difference between rinsing the filter after every cycle and
telling yourself you will do it next time.

Maytronics does not publish a micron rating for it, so anyone quoting one for this
machine got it somewhere other than the listing.

---

## Weight, handling and the things nobody publishes

Eighteen and a half pounds dry, and 22.2 × 17.6 × 12.8 inches. Both figures come from
the listing's own details table.

Eighteen pounds is a two-handed lift out of water, and it comes out heavier than it
went in. There is no caddy in this configuration — one of the six siblings ships with
one and this is not that one.

More than usual, and the reason is worth stating: **we have not found a Maytronics

That is a weaker source than we like, it is disclosed rather than hidden, and it is
why several rows in the specification table below say nothing instead of guessing.

- **Micron rating.** Filter type is given; fineness is not.

## The verdict

If your pool is under 33 ft and in the ground, this is a sensible mid-range corded
buy from the most established brand in the category, and the top-load filter is the
kind of small thing you appreciate weekly for years.

Buy it for floors, walls and ledges. Treat the waterline as a probable bonus rather
than a guarantee until someone shows you a technical sheet, because we have watched
this manufacturer's marketing and its own specifications part company on precisely
that feature before — and it was our mistake to repeat the first time.

And check the listing says 33 FT. Six machines share that page, and the one whose
name looks most like this one is rated for a pool half as long again.
