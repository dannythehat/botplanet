We had this machine's rated area wrong until 10 August 2026 and the correction
is the first thing on this page.

Our catalogue recorded 10,890 square feet — a quarter acre, a round number
somebody reached for. eufy states 800 square metres, which is 8,611 square feet.
That is twenty-six per cent less grass, and because rated area is a rule-out in
this category rather than a ranking factor, a reader with 10,000 square feet was
being offered the E15 as a fit when its own maker does not rate it for the job.
Corrected, and if you were about to buy on the strength of our figure, buy on
eufy's instead.

## Who should not buy this

Anybody with more than 8,611 square feet of grass. See above. The Segway
Navimow i110N covers a genuine quarter acre for $1,099, a hundred dollars less
than this.

Anybody with a slope past eighteen degrees. That is the lowest ceiling of the
seven mowers we hold, and it is a gentle incline rather than a bank.

Anybody who cuts long. The E15 tops out at three inches, and warm-season
grasses — St Augustine, Bahia — are cut at three and up. This machine is at its
limit where those lawns start.

## No wires, no antenna, no satellites

The E15 navigates by camera alone. eufy calls it pure vision FSD:
high-precision cameras and algorithms, with no boundary wire and no RTK station
of any kind.

That is the shortest setup in the category and it is the right technology for a
garden with trees. The comparison worth making is against the Segway at a
similar price: the Segway is satellite-positioned, which is more precise on
open ground and a hard failure under a mature canopy. If you can see the sky
from your lawn, the Segway is a quarter acre for less money. If you cannot, the
Segway is not a candidate at all and this is.

The camera sees 96 degrees horizontally and 80 vertically, which eufy publishes
and its competitors mostly do not.

## Small, and honest about it

Eight-inch cutting width. One to three inches of cutting height. Eighteen
degrees of slope. 800 square metres.

Every one of those is at the modest end of this catalogue, and read together
they describe the machine accurately: a suburban lawn robot for a flat garden
under about a fifth of an acre, at $1,199.99.

What it is not is a cheap way into the category. The WORX Landroid Vision
covers half an acre for $1,022.54 — a hundred and seventy-seven dollars less
for two and a half times the ground — and also navigates by camera. On paper
that is a hard comparison for the E15 to win, and the honest thing is to say so
rather than find a reason it does not count.

## What we could not find out

eufy publishes cutting width, cutting height, slope, coverage and the camera's
field of view. It does not publish battery capacity, runtime, charge time,
noise, IP rating, machine weight or warranty length on the pages we read.
Recorded as not disclosed.

There is also an E18 in the same family, and we did not verify it. If you are
comparing the two, the figures on this page are the E15's only.

## Who should buy one

Somebody with a small flat lawn under trees who wants the machine to work out
of the box with nothing mounted anywhere. That is a genuine niche, camera-only
navigation is the right answer for it, and eufy's setup is the least fiddly
here.

Anybody with more than 8,611 square feet, any real slope, or a lawn they cut
above three inches should look elsewhere. And anybody choosing on price should
put the WORX beside it first, because the WORX covers more ground for less
money using the same kind of navigation.
