The HUTT S55 Pro is the only machine in our catalogue that claims sloped glass,
and that single claim is why it is here. Everything else about it we know less
well than we would like, and this review says so more than once.

## Who this is for

Angled glass. A conservatory roof, a sloping skylight, a slanted gable window —
the surfaces that every other robot in this catalogue quietly declines.

No WINBOT claims it. Neither does the Mamibot W120-DP, the HOBOT 2S or the Cop
Rose X5S. If your problem is angled glass, the shortlist is one machine long,
and that is the whole argument for buying it.

It is also a strong claim on paper for vertical glass: 6,500 Pa of stated
suction puts it above every ECOVACS except the W3 Omni, with an 80 ml tank
matching the W3's and a HydroJet pump feeding it.

## Who should look elsewhere

Anyone with ordinary vertical windows, because you are buying an unusual
capability you will never use, from a brand we have verified less thoroughly
than ECOVACS.

Anyone who wants manufacturer-confirmed numbers before spending. Read the gaps
section before you decide — it is longer here than on any other page in this
category.

## The sloped-glass claim, and how much weight to put on it

Sloped glass is genuinely harder than vertical glass. A robot on a vertical
pane fights gravity in one direction and its suction only has to hold weight.
On a slope the machine is partly resting and partly hanging, the water runs a
different way, and the failure mode if grip breaks is that it slides rather
than falls straight down.

HUTT states 6,500 Pa and a SLAM 4.0 navigation system, and its retail listing
claims inclined glass. We have not been able to read that claim from HUTT's own
product page, and there is a real difference between a specification a maker
publishes and a line in a retail listing.

So: treat the sloped-glass capability as the reason to consider this machine,
and as something to confirm with HUTT directly before you buy for a
conservatory roof. That is an unsatisfying answer. It is the true one.

## The pump, and why it is not the same as spray nozzles

Most machines here mist water through fixed nozzles. HUTT uses what it calls a
HydroJet pump, and the 80 ml tank is the joint largest in the catalogue
alongside the W3 Omni's.

A bigger tank means fewer trips to the sink, which is the part of owning one of
these that people actually resent. On a conservatory roof, where taking the
machine down is more involved than lifting it off a window, that matters more
than it does anywhere else.

## What we cannot tell you

More than usual, and none of it is padding.

**We have not verified any of these specifications with HUTT.** Every figure
above is retailer-stated. For the ECOVACS machines in this catalogue we read
the numbers off ecovacs.com on 5 August 2026; for this one we could not find an
equivalent published page.

**We cannot confirm the sloped-glass rating from the manufacturer.** It is the
reason the product is in the catalogue and it rests on a retail listing.

**The ASIN is owner-supplied and not identity-verified.** It replaced a
different HUTT model in our records on 5 August. No checker has read the
listing's Brand and Model Number fields.

**No warranty term, no power-off hold figure, no weight.** ECOVACS publishes a
30-minute hold across its range. We have no equivalent number for this machine,
and on a robot you may put on a roof that is the figure we would most want.

## The verdict

Buy it if you have sloped glass and you have confirmed the rating with HUTT
first. It is the only machine here that offers to do that job, the stated
suction is strong, and the tank is the largest available.

Do not buy it as a general-purpose window robot. For vertical glass the WINBOT
W2 PRO is better documented, better supported and better understood, and
"better understood" is worth real money on a machine that has to hold itself to
a window above your head.
