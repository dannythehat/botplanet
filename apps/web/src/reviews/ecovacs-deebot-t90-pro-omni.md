## "ECOVACS DEEBOT" is four machines, not one

The DEEBOT T50 MAX PRO Omni at $349. This T90 PRO Omni at $599. The T80S Omni
at $479. The X12 OMNICYCLONE at $1,499.99.

One name in the search box, an eleven hundred dollar spread. We pinned the T90
PRO Omni because it is what Amazon returns first for the family term, and
because it sits in the middle of the range where most readers arriving on that
search will end up. If you have been comparing "a DEEBOT" against something,
check which one.

## Fifteen millimetres and a roller

ECOVACS states plainly that "on carpets, the mop roller automatically lifts to
prevent moisture", with a published figure of 15mm. That is three millimetres
more than the eufy and five less than the roborock S8 Max Ultra, which puts it
comfortably in fitted-carpet-and-thin-rug territory and nowhere near deep pile.

The roller design matters more than the number. A roller that scrapes itself
clean as it turns is wet with clean water for the whole run; two spinning pads
get dirtier from the moment they touch the floor until the robot goes back to
the dock. On a large hard floor that is the difference between mopping and
redistributing.

## The suction number, and why it is the wrong number

Thirty thousand Pascals against the eufy's eight thousand, for a hundred and
fifty dollars more.

Above a modest threshold, suction stops discriminating. What decides whether a
robot vacuum picks up pet hair is whether the hair wraps round the brush;
what decides whether it cleans your house is whether it gets stuck. ECOVACS'
own ZeroTangle brush and AIVI 3D navigation are the parts of this listing
addressing those two questions, and neither is a Pascal figure.

Buy this machine for the roller, the lift and the ninety-day dock. If you are
buying it for 30,000 Pa you are buying a number.

## What we could not find out

ECOVACS publishes more than most makers in this category and its own page read
cleanly, which is why the capability record here is fuller than the two dreame
machines in the same catalogue.

Still not published anywhere we read: runtime, bin capacity, water tank volume,
noise, dimensions and weight. Those are recorded as not disclosed. The "YIKO
Agent" the title advertises is a voice assistant and the listing does not say
what it does without an internet connection.

## Who should buy one

Somebody with a large hard floor and ordinary carpet who wants the mop to stay
clean across a whole run, and who will use a dock that goes three months
between emptyings. Six hundred dollars is a fair price for that and the
capability record here is one of the two most complete in this catalogue.

Anyone spending less should take the eufy X10 Pro Omni at $449.99, which does
the same four things for a hundred and fifty dollars less with dual pads
instead of a roller. Anyone with deep pile is not served by this or by nine of
its ten stablemates.
