The WINBOT W2 PRO Omni is the only machine in our catalogue that will clean a
window nowhere near a plug socket. That is the whole product, and everything
else about it is a good mid-range window robot that costs flagship money.

## Who this is for

You have windows the cable will not reach. A stairwell landing, a conservatory,
a bedroom where the nearest socket is behind a wardrobe — the places that stay
dirty because getting power to them is a job in itself.

The station holds a battery and the robot works off it. ECOVACS states 5.5 kg
for the station and 1.6 kg ±0.1 for the robot, so you carry one thing to the
window and set the other on the glass. That is a different afternoon from
running an extension lead through a bedroom door.

It is also the sensible choice if you simply hate cables near glass, which is a
preference rather than a problem and worth paying for if you feel it.

## Who should look elsewhere

If every window you own has a socket under it, you are paying several hundred
dollars for a battery you will never use. The WINBOT W2 PRO is the same robot
without the station — same WIN-SLAM 4.0 navigation, same six spray nozzles,
same 60 ml ±5 tank — and ECOVACS rates it at 5,300 Pa ±500 against the Omni's
5,500 ±500. That difference is inside both tolerances. You would not be able to
tell them apart on glass.

If your panes are small, none of this applies and the WINBOT Mini is the honest
answer. If your glass is unframed, read the section below before you spend
anything at all.

## The battery station is the product

ECOVACS gives the robot 5,500 Pa ±500 of maximum suction and 2,800 Pa while it
is moving, which is the number that matters — a robot only holds glass while
travelling if the moving figure is high enough. Six spray nozzles, a 60 ml ±5
tank, seven cleaning modes and WIN-SLAM 4.0 navigation.

Set that against the W3 Omni, which ECOVACS rates at 10,000 Pa maximum and 3,300
Pa ±100 moving, with WIN-SLAM 5.0 and eight modes. On paper the W3 is the better
machine and it costs more. The Omni's case is not that it cleans better. It is
that it cleans where the others cannot go.

## Safety, and the number to actually read

Twelve stages of protection, and a power-off hold ECOVACS states at 30 minutes.

That second figure is the one worth understanding. If the mains drop out — or
the battery empties, which on this machine is the likelier event — the robot
holds the glass on reserve power for half an hour while you fetch it. Thirty
minutes is enough to walk upstairs. It is not enough to leave the house.

Every machine here ships with a safety rope, and the rope is not decoration.
Above the ground floor you attach it every time, to something that will take the
weight, and you do that whether or not the specification impresses you.

## Frameless glass

ECOVACS rates this machine for framed and unframed panes, and that is a real
distinction rather than marketing. A robot cleared for frameless glass has to
work out where the edge is from sensors rather than feel for a physical stop,
and one that gets it wrong goes over the edge onto the rope.

If your windows are frameless, that rating is the first thing to check on any
robot, not the suction figure. Two machines in this catalogue are worth reading
on the subject: this one, and the HUTT S55 Pro, which goes further and claims
sloped glass as well.

## The verdict

Buy it for the windows without sockets. That is a real problem, this is the only
machine here that solves it, and if you have those windows the premium is
straightforwardly worth paying.

Buy the W2 PRO instead if you do not. Same robot, same glass, no station, and
the suction difference is inside the tolerance ECOVACS prints on its own page.
If you are unsure which of those two you are, BotMatch asks where the sockets
are before it asks anything about suction.
