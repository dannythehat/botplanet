**The short answer.** The Beatbot iSkim is a solar skimmer with the largest basket volume any of the four we cover states. Beatbot lists it at 9 litres and says that is twice the basket of the skimmers it compares itself with. It floats, skims the surface, and does nothing to the floor, the walls or the waterline.

Everything below about what it does comes from Beatbot's own listing. We have not run one, and each figure is Beatbot's claim unless we say otherwise.

---

## Who this is for

A pool that sheds a lot. A bigger basket means fewer trips to empty it, which matters most in autumn or under heavy tree cover, when a small basket fills in days. Beatbot's own chart has the basket full after 28 days, and says its estimates depend on pool size and debris load.

It also suits a pool with a floor robot already doing the bottom, since debris caught at the surface never has to be vacuumed up later.

## Who should look elsewhere

Anyone expecting a clean floor. For that see the [best robotic pool cleaners](/best-robots/robotic-pool-cleaners/). Beatbot also sells larger machines under the iSkim name, and those are different products.

## The basket

Nine litres is Beatbot's figure, set against what it says is 4.5 litres on other skimmers. Beatbot's own chart has the iSkim's basket full after 28 days and the smaller one full after 14, and it adds that actual emptying depends on pool size, debris load and how often you run it.

## Keeping debris in

The basket is also where Beatbot puts its anti-spill claim: a covered intake that it says keeps debris inside even when the skimmer reverses, where an open intake lets it drift out again. That is a design claim we cannot test from a listing, but it addresses the real failure of an open-front skimmer.

## Power

A 24 watt solar panel and a 10,000 mAh battery, in Beatbot's description, for cleaning round the clock even in moderate rain. The listing does not give a runtime in hours, which is the number we would most like to see. The 24/7 claim is Beatbot's, and without a stated runtime it cannot be compared with Betta's 30 hours or Aiper's 35 hours.

## Following the sun

Beatbot describes a patented SolarTrack feature that steers the machine toward sunlight to charge it. It is a sensible idea for a machine that spends its life on open water, and it is Beatbot's claim; we have not tested it.

## Obstacles

Beatbot calls its obstacle detection SonicSense and describes it as dual-ultrasonic. Its panel shows the iSkim turning away from pool steps while an unnamed rival snags on them, and the choice of rival is Beatbot's.

## Edges

A guide wheel helps the machine follow a pool edge, in Beatbot's description, for more complete coverage along walls. Edges are where a floating machine tends to miss debris, so this is worth checking against owner reports.

## Parking and the app

Through the app the machine can park itself after cleaning or on a single tap, which answers the practical question of how you get a floating machine out of the water.

## What we cannot tell you

- **Runtime in hours,** and what it does after a week of cloud.
- **Which listing you are buying.** The iSkim is sold in more than one colour, with and without a charger, and there is a larger iSkim Ultra. Ours is the Navy Blue listing; the Ink Blue set with a charger is a different one. Check the listing says iSkim and not iSkim Ultra.
- **The warranty term,** the weight and the largest pool it is rated for. None appear in what we read.
- **The comparisons in Beatbot's panels** are Beatbot's choice of competitor, unnamed.

## The verdict

The iSkim is the skimmer for heavy debris. The 9 litre basket and the covered intake are the two claims that set it apart, and they are the two you should check against owner reviews on the Amazon listing. It does not publish a runtime, so it is hard to line up against the others on battery alone.
