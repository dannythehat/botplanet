This machine does not mop. It is the first thing to say, because Shark sells a
mop version fifty dollars away with almost the same name, and the two are easy
to confuse in a basket.

The AV2820S is the PowerDetect Self-Empty Robot Vacuum: $549.99, 4.4 stars
from 3,648 ratings, 360-degree LiDAR with 3D object detection, and a HEPA base
Shark rates at thirty days.

## The fifty-dollar word

AV2820S is the vacuum. RV2820ZE — the PowerDetect NeverTouch Pro — is the
vacuum and mop, at $599.99. There is also an RV3020XE UV Reveal at $849.99.

Three machines, one family name, and the difference between the first two is
one word in a title that runs to a hundred and forty characters. If you want a
mop, this is the wrong page and the wrong ASIN, and nothing about the listing
will stop you.

## What "PowerDetect" is doing

Four detections, in Shark's own naming: DirtDetect, EdgeDetect, FloorDetect and
NeverStuck. The first raises suction where the floor is dirty. The second
turns the machine to run along a wall rather than past it. The third changes
behaviour between hard floor and carpet. The fourth is Shark's name for the
wheels and clearance that get it off a threshold.

3D object detection is the one worth paying attention to. It is the capability
that separates a machine which drives round a phone charger from one that eats
it, and Shark states it explicitly on this listing — which is more than dreame
does at the same money.

What no part of PowerDetect addresses is pet hair. Shark publishes no
statement about the brushroll on this SKU, which is the specification that
decides whether hair wraps or gets cut, so a house with a shedding animal
should read the iRobot Roomba Max 705 at $499 first — its dual rubber
anti-tangle brushes are documented and these are not.

## Not mopping is a feature for some houses

A vacuum-only robot has no water tank to fill, no pads to wash, no dock that
needs plumbing or refilling, and nothing to drag across a carpet by accident.
It also cannot mop.

What to do with that: if you have carpet through most of the house, a mop on a
robot is a liability rather than a feature and this machine removes the
question entirely. If your floors are wood and tile, you are giving up the
thing you most wanted and should look at the eufy X10 Pro Omni at $449.99
instead, which mops and costs a hundred dollars less.

## What we could not find out

Shark's own product page for this SKU redirected to a corporate landing page
rather than serving a specification when we fetched it on 10 August 2026, so
everything here comes from the Amazon listing.

Suction is not published as a figure — Shark does not compete on Pascals and
prints none. Runtime, bin capacity, noise, dimensions and weight are not
published either. All recorded as not disclosed rather than borrowed.

## Who should buy one

Somebody with carpet through most of the house who wants a robot to vacuum it
and nothing else, and who wants object detection rather than a machine that
finds obstacles by hitting them. Five hundred and fifty dollars is a lot for a
vacuum with no mop, and the 3D detection is what you are paying for.

Anyone with hard floors wanting one machine to do both should not be on this
page. Anyone who wants the Shark and the mop wants the RV2820ZE, which is a
different ASIN, a different review pool and fifty dollars more.
