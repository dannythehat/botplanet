If you came here looking for the roborock S8 MaxV Ultra, this is the page for
you and this is not that machine. The S8 MaxV Ultra has no first-party listing
on Amazon US. We looked on 10 August 2026 and every result carrying that exact
string in its title was a third-party accessory kit — mop pads, dust bins,
filter bundles, twenty to thirty-five dollars apiece.

Accessories outliving the machine they fit is what a superseded model looks
like from the outside. What roborock does sell in that place in its range is
the S8 Max Ultra, at $949.99, rated 4.5 from 1,227 ratings.

## Why we built the page under the other name

A comparison site that prints a name on a machine which does not carry it is
useless the moment somebody opens the box. So the product record, the title
and the slug all say S8 Max Ultra.

What we will not do is leave the question unanswered. Nine thousand nine
hundred people a month search for a MaxV Ultra, and the useful thing to tell
them is that it is not on the shelf and this is what stands where it stood.
Whether roborock discontinued it or simply never listed it on Amazon US we
cannot say from outside.

## What the machine does

Twenty millimetres of mop lift, which is the largest figure of the eleven
vacuums in our catalogue and nearly double the eufy X10 Pro Omni's twelve.
Roborock's own page states the mop "lifts up to 20mm above the ground while
cleaning carpets, allowing simultaneous vacuuming and mopping without causing
unwanted dampness".

Obstacle avoidance is Reactive 3D, using structured light rather than a camera
alone. The dock empties the bin during a clean as well as after one, which
roborock rates at seven weeks between bag changes. Suction is 8,000 Pa, the
same figure the $449.99 eufy publishes, which is a useful reminder of how
little that number is doing.

The FlexiArm side brush swings out to reach into corners. Whether it earns
five hundred dollars over the eufy is a different question from whether it
works.

## Twenty millimetres against twelve

Here is the practical difference. Twelve millimetres clears a fitted carpet
and a thin rug. Twenty clears those and gives you room for a thicker rug
without the pad touching it.

What it still does not clear is a deep or shag pile. Roborock makes no
high-pile claim for this machine — its own Saros 10, five hundred dollars
further up the range, is the one whose chassis lifts on high pile — so a house
carpeted in deep pile throughout is not served by this machine either. The
lift figure is about rugs, not about carpet you can lose a coin in.

## What we could not find out

Roborock publishes a suction figure, a lift height, a navigation description
and a dock interval. It does not publish, anywhere we could read, a runtime, a
bin volume, a water tank capacity, a noise figure or dimensions, so those are
recorded as not disclosed.

We also cannot tell you what happened to the MaxV Ultra. The absence of a
listing is evidence that it is not for sale on Amazon US today. It is not
evidence of a discontinuation, and we will not print one as the other.

## Who should buy one

Somebody with hard floor and rugs who wants the mop out of the way properly,
and who will use a dock that empties itself mid-clean rather than waiting for
the end. At $949.99 it is twice the eufy X10 Pro Omni and the extra buys lift
height, a better dock and structured-light avoidance.

Anyone whose house is mostly hard floor should look at the eufy first and keep
the five hundred dollars. Anyone with deep pile should look at the Saros 10,
which is the only machine in our catalogue whose maker makes a high-pile claim
at all.
