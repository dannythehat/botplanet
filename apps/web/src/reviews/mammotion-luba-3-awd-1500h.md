Eighty per cent. That is a 38.6-degree slope, it is what all four driven wheels
buy you, and it is roughly double what most of this category manages.

Mammotion sells the LUBA 3 AWD in two sizes and they are the same machine in
every way that decides whether it suits your garden except one: how much of it
there is. The 1500H is $2,399 for 0.37 of an acre. The 3000H is $2,799 for
0.75. This page covers both, because the only question that separates them is
how much grass you have, and answering it on two pages would mean writing the
same review twice and hoping you found the right copy.

## Who should not buy this

Anybody with a flat lawn. Two thousand four hundred dollars against the WORX
Landroid Vision at a fraction of the price, which covers a bigger area — half an acre against
0.37 — and cuts flat grass just as well. If your garden is level, the
all-wheel drive is dead weight you are financing, and that is true of the 3000H
at $2,799 as well.

Anybody under about an eighth of an acre. Both of these are large, heavy
machines built for ground you do not have, and our matcher discounts a mower
rated for far more area than the reader needs — not because it would fail, but
because you are paying for size, weight, noise and price you will never use.

## Which of the two, and the sum is simple

Measure your lawn, then read this table:

- 1500H — 0.37 acre (16,117 sq ft), $2,399
- 3000H — 0.75 acre (32,670 sq ft), $2,799

Four hundred dollars separates them and it buys twice the ground. If you are
anywhere near the middle — say half an acre — the 1500H is rated for less than
you have and is therefore not a candidate at all. Our matcher rules it out
rather than ranking it lower, which is the right treatment for a machine that
cannot finish: an unfinished lawn is not a slower lawn.

That single sum is why the 3000H is the better buy of the two more often than
the four hundred dollars suggests. Overshooting the area costs you $400 once.
Undershooting it costs you a mower that never gets to the end of the garden.

## What the 3000H adds beyond the acreage

Mammotion's own listing titles publish three differences its specification
tables do not, and they are worth knowing before you decide the extra four
hundred dollars is only about area:

- **Navigation.** The 1500H reads "360° LiDAR + Dual-Camera AI Vision". The
  3000H reads "360° LiDAR + NetRTK + AI Vision" — network RTK positioning on
  top of the same LiDAR and cameras.
- **Mowing rate.** 4,300 sq ft an hour against 5,400.
- **Zones.** Fifteen multi-zone management against thirty.

Everything else matches: the same eighty per cent, the same all-wheel drive,
the same 15 Ah battery and 215-minute run, the same cutting heights.

The navigation difference is the interesting one and it cuts both ways. Network
RTK is a real addition on open ground. Under a mature canopy it is the part
that stops working, which is exactly the condition LiDAR and cameras were
chosen to survive — so on a wooded slope the 1500H is not the lesser machine,
it is the one carrying less that can fail.

## What eighty per cent actually means

A one-in-1.25 gradient. Standing on it, you would call it a bank rather than a
slope.

Here is the comparison that matters, because "handles slopes" is a phrase four
of these seven machines use and behind it sit numbers that are not close:

- Mammotion LUBA 3 AWD — 80% (38.6°), all-wheel drive
- Dreame A3 AWD 1000 — 80% (38.7°), all-wheel drive
- Husqvarna Automower 410iQ — 45%, and 15% at the boundary
- Segway Navimow i110N — 30% (17°)
- WORX Landroid Vision WR320 — 30% (17°)
- eufy E15 — 18°

What to do with that: go and look at the steepest part of your lawn. If a
wheelbarrow feels dangerous on it you are above thirty per cent and four of
these seven machines are out. If you would not walk down it in wet shoes you
are near eighty and you are choosing between these two and the Dreame.

## Navigation, and the reason this is not the cheap way to climb

360-degree LiDAR plus AI vision on both. Not satellite positioning alone —
which is the point, because a satellite-guided mower is defeated by a canopy of
mature trees and no setting fixes it. Steep ground and tree cover go together
more often than not, and a machine that solves one and fails the other is no
use.

Fifteen amp-hours of battery and 215 minutes of running per charge, which is
two and a half times what the Husqvarna manages on its 5 Ah pack. On a slope
that matters: climbing costs power.

## The H is a cutting height, not a trim level

The "H" is not a trim level. Mammotion sells a standard version cutting from
1.0 to 2.7 inches and a high version cutting from 2.2 to 4.0.

If you have St Augustine, Bahia or any warm-season grass that wants to be left
long, the standard version's 2.7-inch ceiling is below where you want to mow
and the machine is wrong for your lawn before it leaves the box. Warm-season
grasses are cut at three inches and up, so a 2.7-inch ceiling scalps them every
pass.

Both of the listings this page links to are the high version — 2.2 to 4.0
inches — and both model names carry the H that says so. Check the letter before
you order, because the two versions differ by one character.

## What we could not find out

Mammotion's product page gives coverage, slope, drive, cutting height, battery
and runtime for both machines. It does not publish a cutting width for the
LUBA 3 anywhere we could read — the 15.7-inch figure in circulation belongs to
the LUBA 2 series and we are not carrying it across, because a superseded
model's number printed as this one's is exactly the mistake this site exists
not to make.

Also not published for either: noise, IP rating, machine weight, warranty
length, and how many separate zones each manages beyond the count in the
listing title. Recorded as not disclosed.

One thing worth knowing before you search. Mammotion's own US page for the
LUBA 2 AWD is titled "2025 Model | Upgraded to 2026 LUBA 3", so the machine
most of the internet's reviews are about is the one its maker has marked
superseded. If a comparison you are reading says Luba 2, it is out of date.

## Who should buy one

Somebody with a genuine bank and, more often than not, trees. That is a narrow
description, and these two are among only three machines here that answer it.

Under 16,117 square feet of grass, buy the 1500H. Over it and up to 32,670,
buy the 3000H — and if you are close to the line, buy the 3000H, because the
penalty for guessing high is $400 and the penalty for guessing low is a lawn
that never gets finished.

If your sloped garden is under 10,764 square feet, read the Dreame A3 AWD 1000
first — same eighty per cent climb, $1,599.99, eight hundred dollars less than
the cheaper of these two. And if this much grass is flat, do not pay for
all-wheel drive at all.
