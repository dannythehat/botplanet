Eighty per cent. That is a 38.6-degree slope, it is what all four driven wheels
buy you, and it is roughly double what most of this category manages.

The LUBA 3 AWD 1500H is $2,399 for 0.37 of an acre, which is a lot of money for
not much ground. The ground is not what you are paying for.

## Who should not buy this

Anybody with a flat lawn. Two thousand four hundred dollars against the WORX
Landroid Vision at $1,022.54, which covers a bigger area — half an acre against
0.37 — and cuts flat grass just as well. If your garden is level, the
all-wheel drive is dead weight you are financing.

Anybody with more than 16,117 square feet. The 1500H is rated for 0.37 acre and
the lawn matcher treats a rated area as a rule-out rather than a ranking, which
is correct: a mower that cannot finish is not a slower mower, it is an
unfinished lawn. Its own sibling the 3000H covers 0.75 acre for four hundred
dollars more, and that is the machine for a bigger garden.

## What eighty per cent actually means

A one-in-1.25 gradient. Standing on it, you would call it a bank rather than a
slope.

Here is the comparison that matters, because "handles slopes" is a phrase four
of these seven machines use and behind it sit numbers that are not close:

- Mammotion LUBA 3 AWD — 80% (38.6°), all-wheel drive
- Dreame A3 AWD 1000 — 80% (38.7°), all-wheel drive
- Husqvarna Automower 410iQ — 45%, and 15% at the boundary
- Segway Navimow i110N — 30% (17°)
- WORX Landroid Vision WR320 — 30% (17°)
- eufy E15 — 18°

What to do with that: go and look at the steepest part of your lawn. If a
wheelbarrow feels dangerous on it you are above thirty per cent and four of
these seven machines are out. If you would not walk down it in wet shoes you
are near eighty and you are choosing between this and the Dreame.

## Navigation, and the reason this is not the cheap way to climb

360-degree LiDAR plus AI vision. Not satellite positioning — which is the point,
because a satellite-guided mower is defeated by a canopy of mature trees and no
setting fixes it. Steep ground and tree cover go together more often than not,
and a machine that solves one and fails the other is no use.

Fifteen amp-hours of battery and 215 minutes of running per charge, which is
two and a half times what the Husqvarna manages on its 5 Ah pack. On a slope
that matters: climbing costs power.

## The high version, which is a different machine

The "H" is not a trim level. Mammotion sells a standard version cutting from
1.0 to 2.7 inches and a high version cutting from 2.2 to 4.0.

If you have St Augustine, Bahia or any warm-season grass that wants to be left
long, the standard version's 2.7-inch ceiling is below where you want to mow
and the machine is wrong for your lawn before it leaves the box. Check which
one you are buying, because the model names differ by one letter.

## What we could not find out

Mammotion's product page gives coverage, slope, drive, cutting height, battery
and runtime. It does not publish a cutting width for the LUBA 3 anywhere we
could read — the 15.7-inch figure in circulation belongs to the LUBA 2 series
and we are not carrying it across, because a superseded model's number printed
as this one's is exactly the mistake this site exists not to make.

Also not published: noise, IP rating, machine weight, warranty length, and how
many separate zones it manages. Recorded as not disclosed.

One thing worth knowing before you search. Mammotion's own US page for the
LUBA 2 AWD is titled "2025 Model | Upgraded to 2026 LUBA 3", so the machine
most of the internet's reviews are about is the one its maker has marked
superseded. If a comparison you are reading says Luba 2, it is out of date.

## Who should buy one

Somebody with a genuine bank, under 16,117 square feet of grass, and trees.
That is a narrow description and this is one of only two machines here that
answers it.

The other is the Dreame A3 AWD 1000 at $1,599.99, which climbs the same eighty
per cent for eight hundred dollars less on a smaller quarter acre. If your
sloped garden is under 10,764 square feet, read that one first.
