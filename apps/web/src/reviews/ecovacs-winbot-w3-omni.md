The W3 Omni is the strongest window robot ECOVACS publishes numbers for, and the
numbers are not close. Ten thousand pascals of maximum suction against 5,500 on
the W2 PRO Omni. Whether you need that is a different question, and for most
houses the answer is no.

## Who this is for

Big glass. Sliding doors, picture windows, a wall of it in a room that was built
to have a view — panes where a robot has to travel a long way without losing its
grip, and where a missed patch is obvious from the sofa.

ECOVACS gives it 3,300 Pa ±100 while moving, which is the figure that decides
whether a machine holds glass in motion rather than only when parked. That is
the highest moving figure in this catalogue and it is the honest reason to buy
the W3 over anything cheaper.

Eight cleaning modes, six spray nozzles, an 80 ml ±5 tank and WIN-SLAM 5.0
navigation, which is a generation ahead of everything else here.

## Who should look elsewhere

Ordinary domestic windows do not need 10,000 Pa. A robot that holds the glass is
holding the glass; there is no partial credit above that threshold, and the extra
suction buys margin rather than a cleaner pane.

If your windows are normal, the WINBOT W2 PRO does the same job for
substantially less. If they are small, the WINBOT Mini is the right machine and
the W3 would be faintly absurd on a bathroom pane. If your glass is sloped —
a conservatory roof, an angled skylight — the W3 does not claim it and the
HUTT S55 Pro does.

## The tank is the quiet upgrade

Eighty millilitres against sixty everywhere else in the ECOVACS range.

That sounds trivial and is not, because refilling is the part of using one of
these that people actually resent. You take the robot off the glass, carry it to
a tap, fill it, carry it back, re-seat it, restart the cycle. On a wall of glass
a 60 ml tank means doing that more than once. The W3's extra third is a third
fewer interruptions.

Six nozzles spread the spray rather than wetting one stripe, which is the other
half of the same problem.

## Twelve stages, and the number that matters

ECOVACS states twelve protection stages and a power-off hold of 30 minutes or
more, the longest figure it publishes for any WINBOT.

The stages are a specification. The hold is the thing you will care about, and
here is what it means in practice: when the power goes, the robot stays stuck to
your window for at least half an hour on reserve. That covers a fuse, a tripped
breaker, somebody unplugging it to charge a phone. It does not cover going out.

Attach the safety rope every time above the ground floor. Twelve stages of
protection is a good specification and a rope is a rope.

## Frameless glass, and what "supported" means

The W3 is rated for framed and unframed panes. On a frameless window the robot
has no physical stop, so it works out where the edge is from sensors and turns
before it reaches one — and WIN-SLAM 5.0 is the newest version of the system
that does that.

That is worth something real if your windows are frameless. It is worth nothing
if they are not, and it is a large part of what you are paying for.

## The verdict

The best specification in the catalogue, and the wrong purchase for most people.

Buy it if you have large panes, sloped-free but genuinely big, and you want the
strongest hold and the biggest tank. Buy the W2 PRO if your windows are the size
most windows are. The gap between them on a normal pane is money rather than
result, and the machine that cleans your glass properly for less is the better
buy however the specification reads.
