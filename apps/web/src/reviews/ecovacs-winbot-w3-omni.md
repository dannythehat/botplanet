## Who this is for

Somebody with genuinely large glass. Sliding doors, picture windows, a wall of
it.

Grip is not about whether a robot falls off a clean pane in a showroom. It is
about whether it holds while carrying 80 ml of water across a surface with a
smear on it, and every extra pascal is margin against the one bad moment. On
ordinary windows that margin is invisible. On a four-foot pane it is the whole
purchase.

Also the household with no socket near the window that needs cleaning. The
station is what this shares with the W2 PRO Omni, and it is the only thing in
this range that solves that problem.

And one reader nothing else here serves: **ECOVACS rates this machine to ±90°
of incline and says it suits horizontal surfaces.** That means a skylight, a
glass roof or a table top. No other robot in this catalogue makes that claim,
and if it is the reason you are shopping, this is the only machine on the list.

## Who should look elsewhere

**Anyone with windows the size most windows are.** The W2 PRO Omni does the
same job with the same station for less, and its 5,500 Pa is not the reason
your glass looks the way it does. This is the honest rule-out and it applies to
most people reading.

**Anyone counting.** At $550 this is roughly double the W2 PRO. What the money
buys is suction margin, a bigger tank and a newer navigation generation — not a
cleaner pane.

**Small panes.** 271 mm square is too big for glazing bars. The WINBOT Mini
exists for that and costs a quarter as much.

## The tank is the underrated part

80 ml ±5, a third more than every other WINBOT.

Suction gets the headline and the tank is what you actually notice. A third more
water is a third fewer trips back to the tap, and on a wall of glass that is the
difference between one session and three. Nobody markets this and everybody who
owns one talks about it.

Set against that: ECOVACS publishes no weight for this model, alone in the
range. Every other WINBOT has a figure. You are lifting this overhead and the
maker will not tell you what it weighs.

## WIN-SLAM 5.0, and what a navigation generation is worth

The newest generation ECOVACS ships, against 4.0 on the W2 machines and 3.0 on
the W1 PRO.

What that buys is route efficiency — fewer passes, less doubling back, a
shorter cycle. What it does not buy is a different result at the end. A robot
that takes eleven minutes and one that takes fourteen leave the same pane
behind, and if you are not standing over it the difference is a number on a
spec sheet.

It matters most where the pane is largest, which is the same reader the suction
argument is for.

## Twelve tiers, and the one number to actually read

Twelve protection tiers, against ten on the plain W2 PRO. Power-off protection,
an emergency lockout, an optocoupler sensor and a gravitational acceleration
sensor among them.

The tier count is the specification everybody quotes and it is close to
meaningless on its own — ECOVACS publishes the number and not the breakdown, so
twelve against ten tells you there are two more of something. The figure worth
reading is the power-off hold, which is 30 minutes across this range, and the
compound cable, which is what keeps the machine attached when the suction stops.

Above the ground floor you attach the safety rope regardless. No tier count
changes that.

## What we cannot tell you

**The weight.** Published for every other WINBOT, absent here. On a machine you
lift to a window that is a real gap, not a formatting one.

**What the 10,000 Pa is measured against.** Manufacturers do not measure
suction the same way and ECOVACS does not publish its method. The figure is
comparable within this range and should not be set against a HUTT or a Mamibot
number as though they were the same test.

**How it performs.** Not tested. Every figure here is ECOVACS's own, including
the 110-minute battery, the 90 m² working area and the ±90° incline rating,
all of which come from ECOVACS's own model comparison rather than from us.

## The verdict

Buy it if your glass is large enough that grip margin is worth $550, and if the
window you care about has no socket under it.

For everything else in this range the W2 PRO Omni is the machine, and this
review exists partly to say so. The step up is real, it is measurable, and most
people do not need it.
