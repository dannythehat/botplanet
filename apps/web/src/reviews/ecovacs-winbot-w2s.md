The WINBOT W2S trades the W2 PRO's six spray nozzles for TruEdge scrubbers and
asks a mid-range price for it.

That is one specific bet: that the border of the pane bothers you more than the
middle of it. If it does, this is the machine. If it does not, ECOVACS will not
tell you enough about this one to justify picking it.

## Who this is for

Somebody whose complaint is the edge.

The border is the strip a cloth reaches last and a circular cleaning path
reaches worst, and it is where every robot in this category does its poorest
work. TruEdge is hardware aimed at exactly that — scrubbers along the edge
rather than a wider spray pattern hoping to cover it.

If you have looked at a pane after a robot has been over it and the only thing
wrong was a rim of haze at the frame, this is the only machine here built for
that complaint.

## Who should look elsewhere

**Anyone who wants to compare it properly, because you cannot.** ECOVACS
publishes maximum suction for every other WINBOT in this range and **none for
this one.** No tank figure, no weight, no dimensions. The W2 PRO publishes all
four.

That is the honest reason to walk past it. Not that it is bad — we have no
evidence either way — but that a mid-priced machine with no published grip figure
is a machine you are buying on a brand name.

**Anyone whose problem is the middle of the pane.** Three nozzles against the
W2 PRO's six. The scrubbers do more of the work at the border and less water
goes down across the glass.

**Anyone above the ground floor wanting a full picture of the safety story.**
Same generation and same family as the W2 PRO, but with no published weight
you cannot judge what the tether is holding.

## TruEdge is unmeasured by anybody independent

It is a real piece of hardware and a sensible idea. It is also a marketing name
for a mechanism nobody outside ECOVACS has tested.

We have not run one. No independent lab figure exists that we could find. What
we can say is what it is aimed at, which is a genuine and common complaint, and
that the trade against it — half the nozzles — is stated plainly in the
specification rather than hidden.

Treat the edge performance as plausible and unproven, and buy on whether the
edge is actually your problem.

## Same navigation, different priorities

WIN-SLAM 4.0, the same generation as the W2 PRO. Not a step up, not a step
down.

So the choice between these two machines is not about how well they find their
way around a window. It is entirely about where you want the effort spent:
spread across the pane with six nozzles, or concentrated at the border with
scrubbers and three.

That is an unusually clean decision for this category, and it is the reason
this page exists rather than a paragraph on the W2 PRO review.

## What we cannot tell you

**Suction.** Maximum or moving. Published for every sibling, absent here, and
it is the number that decides whether a robot holds glass while it travels.

**Weight, tank capacity and dimensions.** All three published for the W2 PRO
and none of them for this.

**Whether TruEdge works better than a good spray pattern.** Nobody has
published a comparison and we have not made one.

**How it performs.** Not tested. Everything above is read from ECOVACS.

## The verdict

Buy it only if the edge of the pane is specifically what you want fixed, and
buy it knowing you are doing so on less published evidence than any other
WINBOT here.

If your answer is "I just want clean windows", the W2 PRO is the same money,
the same navigation, twice the nozzles, and a maker willing to print its
numbers.
