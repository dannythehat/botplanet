That is the entire argument for the Sphero BOLT over the $50 Mini, and it is

## Three ways to code it, and the third one is the point

Draw a path with a finger. Snap blocks together. Type Python or JavaScript.

Most coding robots stop at the second step. BOLT keeps going into text, in the
same Sphero Edu app, on the same machine — so the child who starts at eight
drawing shapes is still using it at twelve writing loops. Nothing else here
does that.

Whether your child reaches step three is unknowable in advance. If they do not,
you have bought a $179 version of a $50 ball.

## The LED matrix is what makes block coding feel like something

A programmable 8×8 grid on the shell. It displays messages, animations and
sensor data.

This sounds like decoration and is not. The weakness of block coding on a
plain robot is that every program produces the same output — the thing moves.
A child writes ten programs and watches ten rolls. With a matrix, the output
of a program can be a face, a number, a scrolling word, so the code and the
result stop feeling like the same event twice.

That is the step where children usually quit, and it is the step this hardware
is built to survive.

## BOLT+ exists and Google is confused about it

Search the model name and Sphero's own site returns the BOLT+ three times, plus
a help page titled "BOLT vs BOLT+".

They are different robots. The BOLT+ is larger, more expensive and newer. Our
buy button points at the original BOLT, ASIN B07DLM5DL7, and if you are
comparing prices across sites make sure both are quoting the same one — the
plus sign is easy to lose in a listing title.

We have not tested the BOLT+ and do not sell it.

## Four hours, and it charges without a socket

Lithium-ion polymer, 4+ hours of play, inductive charging.

Set it on the base and walk away. There is no cable to lose and no lid to open,
which sounds minor until you have owned the Mini, where the shell comes apart
to reach the port. Four hours also means a rainy afternoon does not end halfway
through.

It is waterproof, so a pool or a puddle is a feature rather than an accident.

## Who this is for

A child roughly eight to thirteen with an interest that has already shown
itself. Not a test — a commitment.

Also the household that would rather buy once. Between the Mini at $50 and this
at $179 there is a temptation to buy the cheap one twice, and if the interest
holds, you will have spent $229 to arrive here anyway.

## Who should look elsewhere

**Under-eights.** Sphero rates it 8+ and the drawing mode does not rescue a
five-year-old from an app-driven robot. The Sphero indi is screen-free and
built for four.

**Anyone finding out whether the interest is real.** The Mini runs the same
app for a quarter of the money. Buy that, and buy this if it sticks.

**Anyone who wants to see how a robot is put together.** BOLT is sealed. The
Makeblock mBot arrives in pieces and teaches the hardware.

**A classroom.** Sphero sells education packs directly and the single-robot
listing is not the right purchase order.

## What we cannot tell you

**How far the text-coding step actually gets used.** It is the reason to pay
$179 and we have no data on how many children reach it. Sphero does not
publish that, and neither does anybody else.

**Battery life over years.** The cell is not user-replaceable and no
manufacturer figure exists for how many charge cycles it survives.

**What the BOLT+ changes.** Named here because the search results will put it
in front of you. Not reviewed, not sold, not compared.
