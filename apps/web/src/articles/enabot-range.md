Enabot makes seven driving pet cameras. They span a wide price range, the
names do not explain themselves, and every review you will find covers exactly
one of them.

This page covers the range, because choosing between them is the actual
problem. Every Enabot EBO shares the same idea and the differences between them
are smaller than the prices suggest.

## The short answer

**Buy the EBO Air 2.** It is the middle of the range and gives up nothing that
matters.

**Buy the EBO SE instead if your pet hides.** It is 3.5 inches tall and goes
under the sofa. Nothing else here does.

**Buy the ROLA PetPal if you have a dog** you want to reward rather than merely
watch.

Everything above the PetPal buys a sharper picture. It does not buy a robot that gets
to more places.

## What every one of them does

The category is one idea: a camera you drive.

A fixed camera shows you the room it points at. These show you wherever the
animal actually went, which for anyone whose cat has a favourite hiding place
is the difference between a useful device and a view of an empty rug. If what
you actually want is company for the animal rather than a view of it, a robot
pet is a different product and a different page.

All seven have night vision, two-way talk, and dock themselves when the battery
runs low. That last one decides whether you keep using it — a camera you have
to remember to plug in becomes a camera you stop bothering with.

**None of them needs a subscription.** Furbo and Petcube both sell monthly
plans for features Enabot includes, and that is the strongest single argument
for the brand.

## The seven, and what each adds

In price order, cheapest first. Live prices are on each product below and in
the comparison table, because a figure typed into a sentence is a figure nobody
re-checks.

**EBO SE.** 1080p, 360-degree view, 3.5 inches tall. The cheapest, and the only
one that follows a cat under furniture.

**EBO ROLA Mini.** 2K. The Enabot ROLA Mini has no treat dispenser, despite the
ROLA name it shares with the PetPal, and that catches people out constantly.

**EBO Air 2.** 2K, tracked wheels, an emoticon face. The one to buy.

**ROLA PetPal.** 2.5K and a built-in treat dispenser, with an optional
Interactive Module sold separately. Three times the footprint of the Air 2.

**EBO Mini.** 2K, for more than the Air 2.

**EBO Air 2S.** 2.5K.

**EBO Air 2 Plus.** 3K, plus a chat mode running GPT and Gemini. Roughly three
times the price of the SE.

## Where the money goes, and where it does not

Read that list twice and the pattern is resolution and software.

The Air 2 Plus drives exactly as well as the Air 2. It has a sharper camera and
it will talk to you. It does not reach anywhere the cheaper machine
cannot.

So the honest framing is that Enabot's range has two real decisions in it —
**how small** and **whether treats** — and one soft one, which is how many
megapixels you want to pay for. The SE is the answer to the first. The PetPal
is the answer to the second. Everything else is the third.

## The limitation that applies to all seven

**Stairs.** They have wheels. No driving pet camera sold in the US climbs
stairs.

Leave one downstairs and it patrols downstairs. If the animal spends its day on
a bed upstairs, you need two robots or a fixed camera on the other floor, and no
amount of resolution changes that. Our category page sets out how stairs decide
this purchase before any specification does.

## Two names that catch people out

**The ROLA Mini is not the ROLA PetPal.** The Mini is cheaper and has no treat
dispenser. The dispenser is the entire reason to pay the difference. Check the
full model name.

**The EBO Air and the EBO X are gone.** We checked every Enabot listing on
Amazon US and found neither. The Air was replaced by the Air 2, which is why
searching for the old model brings back Air 2 results — including CNET's review
of a machine you can no longer buy under the name you searched.

## One thing to check before you order

Enabot's own listings say **Enabot Official Store** as the seller.

There is at least one other listing carrying an Enabot product name at the same
price under a different company. Same words, different company. Look at who is
selling before you buy, on any of these.

## What we cannot tell you

**How long any of them runs.** Enabot publishes battery capacity — 2,500 mAh on
the SE and Air 2 — and no runtime, on the listings or its own store. Every
competitor in this space publishes a duration.

**How well the self-docking works in a cluttered room.** It is the feature that
decides whether one of these stays in use, and no maker states a success rate.

**Whether the Air 2 Plus's chat mode is worth the premium.** We have not lived with
one, and the reviews that have are reviewing the novelty rather than a year of
ownership.
