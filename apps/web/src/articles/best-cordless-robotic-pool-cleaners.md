## What cordless actually buys you

Not better cleaning. Every claim a cordless robotic pool cleaner makes about
floors, walls and the waterline is matched by a corded machine somewhere in the
same price band, and the corded one will never stop halfway.

What cordless buys is handling. There is no cable to feed out, no float to
position, nothing coiled on the deck for somebody to trip over and nothing to
untangle when the robot has spent two hours turning your cable into a spring.
You drop it in and you take it out. For a lot of owners that is the entire
reason they replaced a machine that was cleaning perfectly well.

It is a good reason. It is worth being clear that it is the reason, because the
thing you give up in exchange is real and it is on a timer.

## Runtime is the spec that decides it

A cordless robot has to finish your pool on one charge. One that stops at eighty
per cent does not leave the pool eighty per cent clean — it leaves a visible
stripe, and you run the whole cycle again tomorrow.

Across this catalogue runtime runs from 90 minutes on the Aiper Seagull SE to
2 hours 30 on the Polaris FREEDOM. Set that against the rated pool size rather
than against your own pool, because the manufacturer's rating already assumes
their own runtime: 100 ft on the Aiper Scuba X1 Pro Max, about 50 ft on the
Aiper Scuba S1 and the Polaris FREEDOM, 1,615 sq ft on the WYBOT C1, and 3,875
sq ft of floor per cycle on the AquaSense 2 Ultra. Where a maker publishes an
area instead of a length, the comparison table below shows the area, because
those are two different measurements and converting between them invents a
figure nobody stated.

## Cordless no longer means floor-only

This was true and it is worth saying loudly that it has stopped being true. Six
of the seven machines on this page climb walls and scrub the waterline. Two of
them — the Aiper Scuba X1 Pro Max and the AquaSense 2 Ultra — also skim the
surface, which no corded machine in our catalogue does at all.

The exception is the Aiper Seagull SE, and it is the exception on purpose: floor
only, above-ground pools, ninety minutes, no app. At the cheapest end of the
market that is the honest product rather than the compromised one.

## The battery is the whole risk

A robot that lives in chlorinated water, gets lifted out wet and is stored in a
garage over winter contains one component that is guaranteed to be worse in five
years than it is today, and on most of these it is not a part you can replace
yourself.

That single fact changes what you should compare. On a corded machine the
warranty covers a motor, a gearbox and some seals — parts that either work or
have failed. On a cordless machine the warranty is the only commitment anybody
makes about the part that fades gradually rather than breaking. The terms across
this page are 2 years on the WYBOT C1, 3 years on the Aiper Scuba X1 Pro Max, and
a 3-year full replacement term on the AquaSense 2 Ultra — which is the strongest
answer to this problem anyone in the catalogue offers. Aiper does not publish a
warranty term for the Aiper Scuba V3 at all, and that absence is worth noticing
on a machine whose whole premium is its electronics.

## When to buy corded instead

Three cases, plainly.

- **You run a long pool and you run it often.** Unlimited runtime is not a
  feature you appreciate on day one and it is the one you notice in year three.
  The Dolphin Nautilus CC Plus does floor and walls to 40 ft and never charges.
- **You keep equipment for a decade.** Nothing on this page is a decade
  purchase. The BuBlue Bubot 800P covers floor, walls and waterline on mains
  power and publishes more of its own figures than several larger brands.
- **The pool's shape is the problem, not the cable.** Steps and a sun ledge are
  what a machine skips, and the Dolphin Proteus DX4 Plus is the one here built
  for them.

None of that makes cordless the wrong purchase. It makes "cordless" a preference
you are paying for rather than an upgrade you are receiving, and that is a much
easier thing to decide honestly.

## Above-ground pools

Three machines on this page are rated for an above-ground pool: the Aiper Seagull
SE, the Aiper Scuba S1 and the WYBOT C1. An above-ground liner is vinyl with a
rounded join where the wall meets the floor, and a robot built to grip plaster
and drive up a vertical wall does not behave well in one — which is why the other
four are rated for in-ground pools only.

If yours is above ground, that is the whole shortlist: the Aiper Seagull SE if
the floor is the job, the WYBOT C1 or the Aiper Scuba S1 if the walls need doing
too. The general best robotic pool cleaner page covers the corded options as
well.
