## The short answer

The cheapest sensible robot mower is a small, wired, single-zone machine — and
the money you save is real, because the expensive part of this category is
navigation rather than cutting.

Every robot mower sold cuts grass to much the same standard. What separates the
bottom of the market from the top is how much lawn a machine can keep on top of,
how it knows where the lawn ends, and what it does when the ground tilts. If
your garden is small, flat and one piece, you are being asked to pay for
problems you do not have.

Buy at the bottom of the market and you accept three things: you lay a cable,
you get one zone, and you do not get a slope. If none of those matter to you,
the saving is enormous and nothing about the cut suffers.

## Where the money actually goes

Four things set the robot lawn mower price, and only one of them is the blade.

**Area.** Every machine states a maximum it can maintain, and the figure assumes
it mows most days. A mower rated for a quarter of an acre costs a fraction of
one rated for a full acre, because the bigger machine needs a larger battery, a
wider deck and the endurance to keep going. Buying more area than you own is the
most common way to overspend here.

**Navigation.** A boundary wire is a cable and a spool. Satellite, camera and
laser systems are electronics, a fixed antenna and a phone app behind them, and
they roughly double the entry cost. Our page on the wire free robot lawn mower
sets out when that is money well spent; for a small settled garden it seldom
is.

**Terrain.** Climbing ability comes from wheel design, weight distribution and
motor torque, and all three cost money. Cheap machines state modest gradients
and mean them.

**Zones and extras.** Rain sensors, theft alarms, GSM tracking and multi-zone
scheduling are the features that pad a specification sheet without changing what
lands on your lawn.

## What you give up, honestly

**You lay a cable.** An afternoon on your knees with a pegging tool, once. It is
tedious rather than difficult, and a small lawn takes less time than the
instructions suggest.

**One lawn, one dock.** Most entry machines handle a single continuous area. A
front lawn and a back lawn separated by a driveway means carrying the mower, or
buying up.

**No real slope.** This is the hard one. If any part of your lawn is a bank you
would think twice about pushing a mower up, the budget end of the market cannot
help you and the page on the best robot lawn mower for hills is the one you
want.

**Basic obstacle handling.** Cheap mowers bump into things and turn away. They
do not recognise a hosepipe, a dog toy or a sleeping hedgehog, and the more
expensive machines that claim to are not uniformly good at it either.

**A shorter warranty, usually.** The single most useful number to compare at
this end, because it is the only commitment anybody makes about how long the
machine lasts.

## What you do not give up

The cut. A budget machine takes a few millimetres off, most days, and drops the
clippings back as mulch — which is the same thing the flagship does. Grass kept
at one height by frequent cutting looks better than grass cut weekly, and that
result has nothing to do with what you paid.

You also do not give up the quiet, the not-doing-it, or the hour a week back.
Those are the reasons people buy these machines and they are fully available at
the bottom of the range.

## The trap at the very bottom

There is a price below which a robot mower stops being a bargain and starts
being a returned parcel.

The tells are consistent. A stated maximum area with no daily runtime figure
beside it. A brand with no parts listing, which matters because blades are a
consumable and a mower you cannot re-blade is disposable. A battery described by
voltage alone with no capacity given. No mention of what happens when the
machine cannot find its dock.

None of these are about cheapness. They are about a manufacturer declining to
publish the numbers that would let you judge it, and that is a different problem
from a low price.

## What to do next

Measure the lawn before you shop, in square metres or in fractions of an acre,
and take the largest number seriously. Then walk it and find the steepest part.

Small, flat, one piece: buy at the bottom, lay the wire, and spend the
difference on something else. Big, sloped or split into pieces: the cheap end of
this market will not serve you, and the honest move is to save rather than to
compromise.
