## How these were picked

Searches for the best robot pool cleaner and for the best robotic pool cleaner
return the same set of pages, so this one answers both rather than splitting the
argument across two.

Every machine below is in our own catalogue, which means it has a review, a
verified retail identity and a specification transcribed from the manufacturer's
own page rather than from another review site. Where a maker does not publish a
figure, we say so instead of borrowing a number.

The ranking is by job, not by score. A single ordered list would have to decide
in advance whether your pool is above ground, whether your walls get dirty and
whether leaves are your real problem — and it cannot, so it would quietly pick
for the largest group and be wrong for everyone else. Commission plays no part
in the order; the reasoning behind that is in our editorial policy and our
review methodology.

## Start with the surface, not the price

Three surfaces get dirty and they are three separate capabilities. The floor
collects sand and sunk leaves. The walls hold a film that shows on plaster and
pebble and usually does not on vinyl or fibreglass. The waterline is the band of
scum at the surface where sunscreen and body oils collect, and it is the one
people buy a robot to fix.

A robot can climb your wall and never touch that band. The Dolphin Nautilus CC
Plus is exactly that machine — floor and walls, reliably, for years, and no
waterline whatever the packaging suggests. If the ring is your complaint, the
word to look for on the box is waterline, specifically, and the machines that
claim it here are the Aiper Scuba S1, the WYBOT C1, the Aiper Scuba V3, the
Polaris FREEDOM, the Dolphin Proteus DX4 Plus, the BuBlue Bubot 800P and both
flagships.

## In-ground and above-ground are different machines

An above-ground pool has a vinyl liner and a rounded join where the wall meets
the floor. A machine built to grip plaster and drive up a vertical wall does not
behave well in one, and most manufacturers simply do not rate their cleaners for
it. Of the ten cleaners we hold, only four are rated for above-ground use, and
the cheapest of them — the Aiper Seagull SE — is floor-only by design.

That is not a compromise at that price. It is the correct product. A robot that
promises to climb an above-ground liner and then fails to is worse than one that
never promised.

## Big pools: the number that decides it

Makers rate their machines two ways and rarely both: a maximum length in feet, or
a maximum cleaning area in square feet. The two are not interchangeable, and a
robot that stops at eighty per cent of your pool leaves a visible stripe and
means you run it twice.

Longest reach here is the Aiper Scuba X1 Pro Max at 100 ft. The Aiper Scuba S1
and the Polaris FREEDOM are both rated to about 50 ft, the Dolphin Nautilus CC
Plus to 40, and the Dolphin Proteus DX4 Plus to 33 — shorter than most people
assume from a Maytronics machine at that price. Where a maker publishes area
instead, the comparison table below shows the area rather than printing "not
disclosed" against a rating that exists.

## Leaves, and the mistake nearly everyone makes

If your pool sits under trees, the robot is not your first purchase. A floor
cleaner deals with leaves after they have sunk, stained and started to break
down. A skimmer takes them off the surface before any of that happens, and the
two are different product classes doing different jobs — which is why the Betta
SE Plus is in our catalogue but is not on this list. The solar pool skimmers have a
page of their own, where the Betta sits beside the Aiper EcoSurfer S2 and the
Beatbot iSkim.

The strongest setup for a pool under trees is a surface skimmer running alongside
a floor robot. The alternative is one of the two flagships that do both: the
Aiper Scuba X1 Pro Max and the AquaSense 2 Ultra are the only machines here that
claim the surface as well as the floor, walls and waterline.

## The budget end is more honest than it used to be

The cheap end of this market used to mean floor-only. It does not any more. The
WYBOT C1 does floor, walls and waterline with a weekly cycle timer and a stated
2-year warranty, and it is the cheapest machine here that climbs at all. The
honest trade-offs are a 180-micron filter with no ultra-fine layer and mid-pack
runtime.

What has not changed is that a cheap robot with a long feature list is usually
lying about one of them. The Aiper Seagull SE is the counter-example worth
holding on to: it claims one job and does it.

## Corded is not the old option

Seven of the eleven machines we hold are cordless, and it is easy to read that as
a generation gap. It is not. A corded machine has unlimited runtime, no component
that degrades on a calendar, and nothing that has to be replaced by the
manufacturer in four years. The Dolphin Nautilus CC Plus and the Dolphin Proteus
DX4 Plus are both current products, and the BuBlue Bubot 800P makes the corded
case in the upper mid-range with published figures several larger brands withhold.

Buy cordless because the cable is the specific thing you hate — untangling it,
positioning the float, tripping over it. That is a real reason. "It is newer" is
not, and the full argument, including the seven cordless machines ranked against
each other, is on the cordless page.

## What is not on this list

Betta SE Plus is a skimmer, not a cleaner, and putting it in a list of cleaners
would repeat the exact confusion this page is trying to clear up. The BuBlue Bubot
800P Gen2 is a genuinely good corded machine held back only by a short brand track
record, and it is named in the corded section rather than given an award of its own.
