## Why above-ground is a different purchase

Two things change, and both of them rule machines out rather than making them
worse.

The first is the liner. An above-ground pool is vinyl, and vinyl is soft. A
robot built to grip plaster and drive up a vertical wall puts its weight and
its brushes into a surface that was never meant to take either, and the damage
does not announce itself — it shows up as a seam that starts weeping two
seasons later.

The second is the shape. In-ground pools meet their walls at a corner. Above-
ground pools meet theirs at a curve, and a machine that expects an edge to climb
finds a slope that throws it off instead. That is why so many robots simply do
not claim above-ground at all: it is not marketing caution, it is the join.

Of the ten cleaners in our catalogue, four are rated for above-ground use. That
is the entire shortlist, and everything below comes from it.

## The four, and what separates them

**If the floor is the whole job**, the Aiper Seagull SE. Ninety minutes,
cordless, floor only, no app, and the cheapest machine we hold. It does not
climb because it does not claim to, and on a flat-bottomed above-ground pool
there is nothing for it to climb.

**If the walls need doing too**, it comes down to the WYBOT C1 against the
Aiper Scuba S1. Both are cordless, both do floor, walls and waterline, and both
are rated for vinyl. The C1 is cheaper and adds a weekly cycle timer; the S1
brings 3-micron filtration and a published 50 ft length rating rather than an
area figure. Rated capacity is close — 1,615 sq ft against 1,600.

**If you would rather not own a battery**, the BuBlue Bubot 800P Gen2 is the
only corded machine here rated for above-ground, at 1,076 sq ft. Mains power
never runs out mid-cycle and there is no cell to degrade over winters. The
trade is a cable in the water and a young brand behind the warranty.

## Size, and the number that actually rules

Three of the four publish a maximum area rather than a maximum length, which is
the right way round for a round or oval pool — you can work out your area from
the diameter, and a length rating means very little on a circle.

- WYBOT C1 — up to 1,615 sq ft
- Aiper Scuba S1 — up to 1,600 sq ft, or 50 ft long
- BuBlue Bubot 800P — up to 1,076 sq ft
- Aiper Seagull SE — no published rating, and that absence is the honest
  reason it belongs on small pools

A 24 ft round pool is about 450 sq ft. An 18 by 33 oval is about 470. Almost
any above-ground pool sits inside all three published ratings, which means for
most readers size is not the deciding factor here — coverage is.

## If your pool is under trees

Buy a skimmer as well, and the reasoning is the same as it is for in-ground
pools. A floor robot deals with leaves after they have sunk and started to
break down. The Betta SE Plus floats, runs on sunlight and takes them off the
surface first, and it is rated for above-ground use.

None of the four machines above skims. The two flagships that do are in-ground
only, so for an above-ground pool the skimmer is genuinely a second purchase
rather than a feature you can buy your way to.

## What we would not do

Put an in-ground robot in an above-ground pool because the numbers look
similar. The rating is not a performance claim, it is a compatibility one, and
the liner does not get a second chance.

Buy on suction figures. Every maker measures its own way and none of them
publish a standard, and on a flat vinyl floor the difference between a good
figure and a great one is not something you will see.
