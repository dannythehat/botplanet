## Measure two things before you read the ranking

This category rules machines out rather than ranking them, and it does it on
two numbers you can get in ten minutes.

**How much grass, in square feet.** Every mower here is sold with a rated area,
and that figure is a ceiling rather than a suggestion. A mower rated for less
ground than you have does not cut your lawn more slowly — it never finishes it.
Pace the lawn out, multiply, and write the number down.

**How steep the worst bit is.** Go and look at the steepest part. If pushing a
wheelbarrow up it feels unwise you are above thirty per cent, and four of the
six machines below are already out. If you would not walk down it in wet shoes
you are near eighty, and there are exactly two answers.

Everything else — app quality, cutting width, how the boundary is set — is a
preference. Those two are arithmetic, and they decide this before anything
else does.

## Best overall: the WORX Landroid Vision WR320

Half an acre of rated area, at a price nothing else here approaches.

The next cheapest machine rated for the same ground is the Husqvarna, at nearly
two and a half times the money. The Landroid navigates by camera with no
antenna to mount and no boundary wire to bury, which removes the installation
day that puts most people off this category entirely.

Two things it does not give you. Any slope past thirty per cent is outside its
rating, and WORX publishes no runtime figure anywhere we could read — unusual
in a category where everybody else does.

For a flat half acre, it is still the machine to beat on price by a distance
that no feature list closes.

## When something else is the right answer

**A genuine bank.** The Mammotion LUBA 3 AWD claims eighty per cent — 38.6
degrees — on all four driven wheels, and it navigates by LiDAR and cameras
rather than satellites. That pairing matters more than it sounds: steep gardens
are usually wooded gardens, and a satellite-guided mower under a mature canopy
stops in the middle of the lawn and cannot tell you why. Two sizes, 0.37 and
0.75 of an acre, covered on one page.

**The same climb for less money.** The Dreame A3 AWD 1000 does the same eighty
per cent for several hundred dollars less than the Mammotion, on the same
LiDAR-first approach. The catch is area: a quarter acre is the ceiling. If your slope is
steep and your lawn is small, this is the cheaper of the only two answers.

**An awkward shape, and a warranty.** The Husqvarna Automower 410iQ is the only
maker here that publishes its rated area twice — half an acre if your lawn is a
sensible shape, a quarter if it is not — which is the most honest specification
in the category. It also carries four years of warranty against silence from
most of this list. It will not climb, though: forty-five per cent inside the
area and fifteen at the boundary.

**An open quarter acre.** The Segway Navimow i110N runs Network RTK with no
antenna to mount and no subscription — Segway carries the cellular data cost.
It needs a clear view of the sky to do it, so read the next entry if you have
trees.

**A small lawn under trees.** The eufy E15 navigates purely by vision: no
wires, no antenna, no satellites, so a canopy is not a problem. Its ceiling is
800 m² — 8,611 square feet — and eighteen degrees of slope, which is the
tightest pair of limits here. Inside them there is nothing to install.

## What none of them publishes

Cutting width, for the LUBA 3 in either size. The 15.7-inch figure in
circulation belongs to the LUBA 2 series, and a superseded model's number
reprinted as this one's is the mistake this site exists not to make.

Runtime, for the WORX. Noise, ingress rating and machine weight for most of
them. Where a maker does not publish a figure we record it as not disclosed
rather than borrowing one from a review site that borrowed it from another
review site.

The one number worth chasing a maker for is rated area, and to their credit
every one of these publishes it.

## Where they all disappoint

The edge, and every brand is quiet about it.

A round or square deck cannot cut right up against a wall, a fence or a border,
so a margin is left everywhere the lawn meets something solid. Makers print
"cut to edge" and none of them prints how close in inches. Plan on a strimmer
a few times a season regardless of which machine you buy.

Long grass is the other one. These are mulching mowers that cut a little every
day, and they are not a machine for a lawn that has been left three weeks. Cut
it conventionally first, then let the robot hold it.

## The one that also clears snow

None of the six above does anything in January. One machine on this site does,
and it is not a mower with an attachment — it is the other way round.

Yarbo sells a self-driving Core that takes a mowing module, a snow blower, a
leaf blower or a trimmer. Bought as a mower it is not in this ranking, because
we do not hold it as a mower and will not rank a machine we have not
catalogued. Bought for snow it is the only autonomous snow blower on sale in
the United States, which is a statement about the market rather than a
compliment.

It is worth knowing about here for one reason: if you are choosing a mower and
you also shovel a driveway every winter, the sums are not the sums on this
page. Our Yarbo Snow Blower review has the numbers, including why the same
three words are quoted at two prices nearly four times apart depending on
whether the robot is included.

## How to choose in one minute

Area first: over half an acre and only the larger LUBA 3 qualifies. Under
8,611 square feet and everything qualifies, so buy on price.

Then slope: past thirty per cent, it is the Mammotion or the Dreame and nothing
else. Under it, the WORX is cheapest per square foot by a wide margin.

Then trees: heavy canopy rules out the Segway, and points at the eufy for a
small lawn or the LUBA 3 for a large one.
