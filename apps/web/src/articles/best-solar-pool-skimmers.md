## What a solar powered pool skimmer is for

A solar powered pool skimmer is a small floating robot that sits on the water, runs from a solar panel and a battery, and collects whatever lands on the surface: leaves, pollen, insects, hair, small twigs. It does nothing for the floor, the walls or the waterline.

That is the whole point. A floor robot deals with leaves after they have sunk, stained and started to break down. A skimmer takes them off first, before they ever reach the bottom.

> **BotPlanet takeaway**
>
> If leaves and pollen land on your pool faster than they sink, a robotic pool skimmer is the right first purchase. If the floor or the waterline is what bothers you, you want a cleaner instead.

## Skimmer or robotic pool cleaner?

These are two different machines doing two different jobs, and most pools under trees end up wanting both.

- **A skimmer** floats and works through the day. It cannot reach the bottom, so it will not touch sand, silt or algae.
- **A cleaner** runs a cycle of a couple of hours on the floor and walls and then stops. It collects what has already sunk. Our [cordless](/best-robots/robotic-pool-cleaners/cordless/) and [above-ground](/best-robots/robotic-pool-cleaners/above-ground-pools/) lists rank those.
- **Both together** works best for a pool under trees, because the skimmer stops the cleaner inheriting a floor full of leaf mush.

## What to compare on a skimmer

Four things decide most skimmer purchases, and the headline runtime is the least useful of them.

- **Battery on a grey week.** Betta claims 30 hours and Aiper 35, but a battery that size is refilled by a panel the size of a tray. The question that matters is how long it runs after three cloudy days, and none of the makers answer it.
- **Basket size and mesh.** Beatbot lists a 9 litre basket, the biggest of the four. Betta uses a 200 micron mesh and Aiper 150 micron. A bigger basket means fewer trips to empty it; a finer mesh catches pollen but clogs sooner.
- **How it gets out of trouble.** Steps and ledges strand a floating machine. Aiper and BRINBO both describe adjustable bars on the underside, and Beatbot uses a guide wheel along the edge.
- **How you get it out.** Beatbot says the iSkim parks itself on one tap in its app, and the BRINBO app has a park button. The others you lift out with a pole or by hand.

> **BotPlanet takeaway**
>
> Heavy leaf fall: put basket size first. A pool with steps or awkward corners: put the anti-stranding design first. A pool that gets plenty of sun: runtime matters less than you think.

## Saltwater pools and app control

Two things buyers often overlook with a solar pool skimmer are saltwater compatibility and how much control you get from your phone.

**Saltwater.** Two of the four say something about salt. Betta describes its twin motors as salt chlorine tolerant, and the Beatbot iSkim listing says it is saltwater-safe. The Aiper and BRINBO listings we read do not mention salt either way. That does not mean they cannot be used in a salt pool, but we would check with the maker before buying one for that purpose.

**App control.** Beatbot, Aiper and BRINBO all come with an app, which gives you monitoring and manual control, and in Beatbot's and BRINBO's case a way to park the machine. The Betta SE Plus has no app. It runs on its own, with a remote when you want to steer it by hand.

That is not automatically a disadvantage. If you want status and control on your phone, an app is useful. If you just want a skimmer that patrols the surface all day with no input from you, Betta's simpler approach may suit you better.

> **BotPlanet takeaway**
>
> Saltwater pool: the Betta SE Plus and the Beatbot iSkim are the two that say they are built for it.
>
> Want phone control: look at the Beatbot iSkim, Aiper EcoSurfer S2 or BRINBO SK01.
>
> Want the least interaction possible: the app-free Betta SE Plus makes a lot of sense.

## Which skimmer for which pool

- **A pool that sheds a lot, with heavy tree cover.** The [Beatbot iSkim](/robots/robotic-pool-cleaners/beatbot-iskim/): the largest basket of the four, behind a covered intake that Beatbot says keeps debris in when the machine reverses.
- **A pool with steps, ledges or awkward corners.** The [Aiper EcoSurfer S2](/robots/robotic-pool-cleaners/aiper-ecosurfer-s2/): two dToF sensors and adjustable anti-stranding columns, both described on its listing.
- **A buyer who wants to read the manual first.** The [Betta SE Plus](/robots/robotic-pool-cleaners/betta-se-plus/): the only one with a manufacturer's page, a stated one-year warranty and published charge times.
- **A buyer who wants an app and will accept a newer brand.** The [BRINBO SK01](/robots/robotic-pool-cleaners/brinbo-sk01/): an app, two speeds and a 2.5 hour adapter charge, from a brand with 28 ratings so far.
- **A pool in permanent shade.** None of them. A solar skimmer needs sun to refill its battery, and the mains adapter is a workaround, not a plan.

> **BotPlanet takeaway**
>
> There is no single best solar pool skimmer, only the best one for your pool. If you are unsure, the Betta SE Plus is the safest start because you can read what the maker commits to. The iSkim is the pick for heavy debris, and the Aiper for tricky pool shapes.
