## What actually separates these brands

Corded or cordless, and almost nothing else at the level of the badge.

Every Aiper in this catalogue runs on a battery. Every Dolphin runs on mains
power. Polaris sits on the cordless side, BuBlue and its Bubot 800P on the corded
side, and the two flagships from Beatbot and Aiper are both cordless. Once you
know which side of that line you want to be on, the brand argument mostly answers
itself and the real comparison becomes what each machine reaches.

The rest of what gets called a brand difference is either a specification you can
check in the table below or a claim nobody has verified. Suction figures are the
worst offender: makers measure them differently, most publish nothing, and the
ones that do are quoting their own benchmark.

## Compare on reach, not on power

Three surfaces get dirty and they are three separate capabilities. A machine that
climbs your wall can still leave the waterline untouched, and that is not a
cheap-machine problem — the Dolphin Nautilus CC Plus is a well-built corded robot
that does the floor and the walls and does not scrub the band at the surface.

Ranked by what they reach, the catalogue splits four ways:

- **Floor only** — the Aiper Seagull SE, and honestly so.
- **Floor and walls** — the Dolphin Nautilus CC Plus.
- **Floor, walls and waterline** — the Aiper Scuba S1, the WYBOT C1, the Aiper
  Scuba V3, the Polaris FREEDOM, the Dolphin Proteus DX4 Plus and the BuBlue
  Bubot 800P.
- **All four, surface included** — the Aiper Scuba X1 Pro Max and the AquaSense 2
  Ultra, and nothing else.

The Betta SE Plus is in none of those groups because it is a skimmer, not a
cleaner. That distinction is worth more than any brand preference on this page.

## Then compare on the pool you actually own

The second filter is size, and it is the most common rule-out on this site. Makers
rate a machine two ways and rarely both — a maximum length in feet or a maximum
cleaning area in square feet — and converting between them invents a number
nobody stated, so the table shows whichever the maker published.

The spread is wider than the prices suggest. The Aiper Scuba X1 Pro Max is rated
to 100 ft. The Aiper Scuba S1 and the Polaris FREEDOM are rated to about 50. The
Dolphin Nautilus CC Plus stops at 40, and the Dolphin Proteus DX4 Plus at 33 —
shorter than most people expect from a Maytronics machine at that price, and the
single fact most likely to make an otherwise good purchase wrong.

Above-ground is the other hard filter. Only four of the ten cleaners here are
rated for a vinyl liner, and the cheapest of them is floor-only by design.

## Where the brands stop mattering

At the point where two machines reach the same surfaces and are rated for the
same pool, the badge stops carrying information and three things take over:
whether the filter is easy enough to reach that you will actually rinse it, what
the warranty says, and whether you want a cable in the water.

The warranty is the one worth dwelling on, because nobody publishes a lifespan
and the term is the only commitment anyone makes. On a corded machine it covers a
motor and some seals. On a cordless one it is also covering the battery — the
part that fades rather than fails, and the part you usually cannot replace
yourself. That is why a 3-year full replacement term reads differently on a
cordless flagship than a 3-year parts warranty does on a corded mid-ranger.
