## Start with your glass, not with the ranking

Two questions decide this category, and both are about your windows rather than
about the machines.

**Is your glass frameless?** A robot that navigates by feeling for a frame will
drive off the edge of a pane that has none. Most models here handle frameless
glass and a few do not, and where a maker does not claim it, that is not
timidity — it is the answer.

**Is there a socket near the windows you actually want cleaned?** Almost every
machine in this category runs on mains power with a battery inside for
emergencies rather than for cleaning. The stairwell landing, the conservatory,
the window behind the sofa: those are the ones that stay dirty, and only one
machine here solves it.

Answer those two and the list below shortens to about three.

## Best overall: the WINBOT W2 PRO

The one most people should buy, and it is not the flagship.

It carries the same navigation, the same six nozzles, the same tank and the
same cleaning modes as the machine above it in the range, with suction inside
an overlapping tolerance band. What you are not paying for is a battery station
that weighs more than three times the robot. If your windows have sockets near
them, that station is a solution to a problem you do not have.

## When something else is the right answer

**No socket nearby.** The WINBOT W2 PRO Omni is the only machine in this
catalogue that cleans a window nowhere near a plug, and that is the entire
product. On glass it is a good mid-range robot. Off the tether of a wall
socket, it is the only one.

**Big glass, edges, or small panes.** All three are answered inside the same
range rather than by a different machine, and they are covered on the two WINBOT
reviews above rather than given pages of their own. The W3 Omni is the station
machine for a wall of glass; the W2S trades the nozzle array for edge scrubbers
and publishes almost no figures; the Mini is the small-pane model, and it
out-grips both of the models above it. None of the three changes the answer for
an ordinary window, which is why this list does not rank them separately.

**Sloped or roof glass.** The HUTT S55 Pro is the only machine here that offers
to work on slanted glass at all — conservatory roofs, skylights, gable windows.
It is also from the least-verified maker in the catalogue, and that is the trade
you are making.

**Refilling is the part you resent.** The HOBOT 2S carries two replaceable water
tanks, which nothing else here offers, and an ultrasonic atomiser instead of
pumped nozzles. You cannot compare its grip against a WINBOT, because the
figures that would let you are not published.

**A tight budget, and no app.** The Cop Rose X5S is the cheapest machine we hold
and the only one with no app at all — a remote control instead, which for the
right buyer is the feature rather than the compromise. It does not claim
frameless glass, and that rules it out for a lot of modern windows.

**Anything but an ECOVACS.** Three of the eight machines here are one brand,
which is a real concern if you would rather not bet a house on one maker's
service operation. The Mamibot W120-DP is the mid-price alternative, with a
proper four-nozzle spread.

## Two we hold and do not recommend

The WINBOT W1 PRO is a competent entry machine that has been overtaken inside
its own range: the Mini variant costs less and grips harder. Buy it only if you
specifically want the larger body.

The HOBOT 298 is the machine we know least about. Its own maker publishes almost
nothing measurable about it, and the W1 PRO costs about the same while
publishing everything the 298 does not. If you want ultrasonic spray, the 2S is
the one to have.

Neither is a bad machine. Both are beaten on the page by something already on
the list, and giving them an award anyway would make this a catalogue rather
than a ranking.

## The high rise question, answered honestly

Several makers use the words "high rise" and almost none of them publish the
number that would justify it.

The figure that matters is how long the machine holds the glass after the power
fails, and it should be stated in minutes with the battery capacity behind it.
Where a maker gives it, weigh it. Where a maker writes "high rise" and gives no
duration, treat it as positioning. Our category page sets out what power-off
protection actually means and what to look for.

Whatever the number, use the safety rope on every window above the ground floor,
every single time. It is the only part of this category where the failure mode
is a machine falling from a height onto something below.

## Where they all disappoint

Streaking at the very edge of the pane, and none of the marketing mentions it.

The pattern is consistent across brands and price points: the middle comes up
well, the border comes up worst, and the last centimetre against the frame is
where a cloth still beats a robot. Buy one to stop doing the whole window, not
to stop doing windows. If you have not settled whether window cleaning robots
work at all, that question has its own page and a blunter answer than this one.

Two more honest limits. These clean one side of the glass, so the outside of an
upstairs window is only reachable if the window opens inwards. And they are
loud — closer to a vacuum than to an appliance you can ignore in the next room.

## How to choose in one minute

Frameless glass narrows the field first. No socket nearby narrows it to one.
After that it is pane size: large glass wants the W3 Omni variant, small panes
want the Mini, and everything in between is the W2 PRO itself.
