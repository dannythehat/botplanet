## The short answer

Measure the gradient before you shop for anything else, because slope is the one
constraint in this category that cannot be worked around.

A robot lawn mower that cannot hold your bank will not learn to. It will slip,
scalp the turf where the wheels spin, get stuck at the bottom, and eventually be
carried back up by the person who bought it to stop carrying things around the
garden. Area you can compromise on. Navigation you can choose. Gradient is a
hard yes or no.

The good news is that the number you need is easy to get and the machines state
theirs plainly.

## Work out your actual gradient first

Manufacturers quote slope two ways and the two are not the same number.

**Percent** is rise over run: a bank that climbs 1 metre over 3 metres of
ground is 33%. **Degrees** is the angle. A 45% slope is about 24 degrees, which
is steeper than it sounds and steeper than most machines will take. Read which
unit is on the specification before comparing two mowers, because 35 in one unit
and 35 in the other are different hills.

To measure it: run a straight plank up the bank, put a spirit level on it, and
measure the drop at one end against a known length. Rise divided by run, times
a hundred. A phone inclinometer app on a plank does the same job in less time.

Two other numbers matter as much as the headline gradient, and neither gets
published:

- **The steepest metre, not the average.** A lawn that averages 20% with one 40%
  lip at the top is a 40% lawn as far as the mower is concerned.
- **The slope at the boundary.** A machine turning across a gradient is less
  stable than one driving straight up it, and the turn usually happens at the
  edge, which is where the drop is.

## What actually makes a mower climb

Four things, and the marketing mentions one of them.

**Wheel tread and diameter.** Deep, widely spaced lugs on a large wheel bite wet
grass. Small smooth wheels do not. This is visible in a photograph, which makes
it the easiest thing to check before buying.

**Weight over the driven wheels.** Traction is weight multiplied by grip. A
light mower with good tyres still slips, which is why the machines that climb
well in this category are rarely the small ones.

**All-wheel drive.** Two driven wheels on a bank means the front is being pushed
rather than pulling. Machines built for gradient tend to drive all four, and
they cost accordingly.

**Cutting height on the descent.** Going downhill the deck rides differently and
a low setting scalps the crown of the bank. Setting the height 10 millimetres
above what you would use on the flat solves most complaints about bald patches.

## Wet grass is the real test

Every published gradient figure is measured on dry grass, and no manufacturer we
have read says so out loud.

Grass that is wet with dew — which is exactly when these machines prefer to
work, because nobody is on the lawn — carries perhaps two-thirds of the traction
of the same slope at midday. If your maximum gradient sits within a few percent
of a machine's rating, it will manage in July and spin in October.

What to do with that: treat the published figure as the dry-weather ceiling and
buy at least 10 percentage points of headroom above your steepest metre. That is
the single most useful sentence on this page.

## When the answer is no mower at all

Some lawns are not robot lawns, and saying so is more useful than selling you a
machine that will be returned.

Above roughly 50% on any part of the lawn, nothing in this category is a safe
bet. A terraced garden with steps or retaining walls between levels is several
lawns rather than one steep one, and it is a multi-zone problem before it is a
gradient problem — an area the wire free robot lawn mower systems handle far
better than a wired machine, because you can draw the zones instead of cabling
them.

A bank you cannot walk across comfortably is a bank a robot will struggle on,
and a strimmer on a pole is the honest tool for it.

## What to do next

Get your steepest number, add ten points of headroom, and use that as a hard
filter before you look at anything else. Then check that the machine's rated
area still covers your lawn, because the mowers that climb are often not the
ones with the biggest range.

If your garden turns out to be flatter than you feared, the cheap robot lawn
mower page is where the money is, since gradient is one of the three things that
pushes the price up. If it is steep and also large, expect to pay for
all-wheel drive and a bigger battery together.
